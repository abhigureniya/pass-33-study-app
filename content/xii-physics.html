5. Must-do question bank (115 questions)

Tags: years = board papers listed above where this or a very close version appeared. "SQP" = CBSE Sample Paper 2026-27. "Practice" = standard NCERT-type question, no year claimed. Constants (as given in the paper): c = 3 × 10^8 m/s, h = 6.63 × 10^-34 J s, e = 1.6 × 10^-19 C, 1/(4πε0) = 9 × 10^9 N m²/C², μ0 = 4π × 10^-7 T m/A. Handy: E(eV) = 1240 / λ(nm).

Chapter 1 – Electric Charges and Fields

Q1. (MCQ) A point charge +Q is at the centre of a spherical Gaussian surface of radius R. If the radius is doubled, the flux will: (A) halve (B) double (C) remain the same (D) become four times. [SQP; similar 2015]

Answer: (C). Flux = q(enclosed)/ε0. It does not depend on size or shape of the closed surface.

Q2. (1 mark) What is the electric flux through a cube of side 1 cm which encloses an electric dipole? [2015]

Answer: Zero, because net charge enclosed = +q − q = 0.

Q3. (2 marks) Define electric flux. Is it scalar or vector? Give its SI unit. [2018]

Answer: Electric flux through a surface = number of field lines crossing it = ∮ E·dA (= EA cos θ for uniform field). It is a scalar. Unit: N m² C^-1 (or V m).

Q4. (Derivation, 3–5 marks) Derive the electric field of a dipole at a point on its axial line. Show that for r >> a, E = 2p / (4πε0 r³). [2015, 2017]

Steps:

  1. Dipole: −q at A, +q at B, AB = 2a, p = q × 2a (from −q to +q). Point P on axis at distance r from centre O, on the side of +q.
  2. Field due to +q: E+ = kq/(r − a)², directed away from +q (along p).
  3. Field due to −q: E− = kq/(r + a)², directed towards −q (opposite to p).
  4. Net E = kq [1/(r − a)² − 1/(r + a)²] = kq × 4ar / (r² − a²)².
  5. E = k × 2p r / (r² − a²)², along p.
  6. For r >> a: E = 2kp / r³ = 2p / (4πε0 r³). Direction: along p. Diagram: straight line with −q, O, +q, and P to the right; arrows E+ (right) bigger than E− (left).

Q5. (Derivation, 3–5 marks) Derive the field at a point on the equatorial line of a dipole. [2019, 2023; 2025 asked field at (0, y) for charges at (±a, 0)]

Steps:

  1. P on perpendicular bisector at distance r from O. Distance from each charge = √(r² + a²).
  2. Magnitudes: E+ = E− = kq/(r² + a²).
  3. Components perpendicular to the axis cancel. Components parallel to the axis add (both point from +q side towards −q side, i.e. opposite to p).
  4. E = 2 E+ cos θ, with cos θ = a/√(r² + a²).
  5. E = kq × 2a / (r² + a²)^(3/2) = kp / (r² + a²)^(3/2), direction opposite to p.
  6. For r >> a: E = p / (4πε0 r³). (Half of axial value. If distance is halved, E becomes 8 times – 2023 part.)

Q6. (3 marks) Write the torque on a dipole in a uniform field. Show stable and unstable equilibrium. [2017; SQP-type]

Answer: τ = p × E, magnitude τ = pE sin θ. Net force on dipole in uniform field = 0. Stable: p parallel to E (θ = 0°), τ = 0. Unstable: p antiparallel (θ = 180°), τ = 0. Maximum torque pE at θ = 90°.

Q7. (Numerical, 2 marks) A dipole: −1.0 pC at (0, 0) and +1.0 pC at (3 mm, 4 mm). Field E = 1000 V/m along +x is switched on. Find the torque. [2024]

Steps:

  1. p = q × (vector from −q to +q) = 10^-12 × (3 × 10^-3 î + 4 × 10^-3 ĵ) = (3î + 4ĵ) × 10^-15 C m.
  2. τ = p × E = (3î + 4ĵ) × 10^-15 × 1000 î.
  3. î × î = 0, ĵ × î = −k̂. So τ = −4 × 10^-12 k̂ N m.
  4. Magnitude 4 × 10^-12 N m, along −z.

Q8. (Derivation, 3 marks) Using Gauss's law find E due to an infinitely long straight wire of linear charge density λ. Draw E vs r graph. [2018; 2026 used it for force between two line charges]

Steps:

  1. Gaussian surface: cylinder of radius r, length l, coaxial with wire.
  2. Flux through flat ends = 0 (E parallel to them). Flux through curved surface = E × 2πrl.
  3. Charge enclosed = λl. Gauss: E × 2πrl = λl/ε0.
  4. E = λ / (2πε0 r), radially outward for λ > 0. Graph: E vs r is a falling curve (E ∝ 1/r), like a hyperbola.

Q9. (Derivation, 3 marks) Using Gauss's law, find E due to an infinite plane sheet of charge density σ. [2016, 2017]

Steps: Take a cylinder (pillbox) of cross-section A crossing the sheet. Flux only through the two flat faces: EA + EA = 2EA. Charge enclosed = σA. So 2EA = σA/ε0 → E = σ / (2ε0), perpendicular to sheet, away for +σ, towards for −σ. E does not depend on distance.

Q10. (Derivation, 3 marks) Field due to a uniformly charged thin spherical shell (radius R, charge q) (i) outside (r > R) (ii) inside (r < R). [2025; 2026 (shell flux parts)]

Steps:

  1. Outside: Gaussian sphere of radius r > R. E × 4πr² = q/ε0 → E = q / (4πε0 r²) (as if all charge at centre).
  2. Inside: Gaussian sphere r < R encloses no charge → E × 4πr² = 0 → E = 0.
  3. Graph: E = 0 up to R, jumps to maximum at R, then falls as 1/r².

Q11. (Numerical, 3 marks) Electric field E = 500x î N/C (x in m). A cube of side 0.1 m has its faces at x = 0.1 m and x = 0.2 m (other faces parallel to the x-axis). Find the flux through the cube and charge inside. [2024 – figure positions assumed here as above]

Steps:

  1. Only the two faces perpendicular to x have flux. Area A = 0.01 m².
  2. At x = 0.1 m: E = 50 N/C, flux in = −50 × 0.01 = −0.5 N m²/C.
  3. At x = 0.2 m: E = 100 N/C, flux out = +100 × 0.01 = +1.0 N m²/C.
  4. Net flux = 0.5 N m²/C.
  5. q = ε0 × flux = 8.854 × 10^-12 × 0.5 ≈ 4.4 × 10^-12 C.

Chapter 2 – Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance

Q12. (Numerical, 2 marks) Two charges +q and +q are a distance d apart. How much work is needed to bring another +q from infinity to the midpoint? [SQP]

Steps: Potential at midpoint V = kq/(d/2) + kq/(d/2) = 4kq/d. Work W = qV = 4kq²/d = q² / (πε0 d).

Q13. (Derivation, 3 marks) Derive the potential due to a dipole at a point on its axis; show V = p / (4πε0 r²) for r >> a. [2019; 2024 (far point)]

Steps: V = kq/(r − a) − kq/(r + a) = kq × 2a/(r² − a²) = kp/(r² − a²). For r >> a, V = kp/r² = p/(4πε0 r²). On the equatorial line V = 0 (equal and opposite contributions).

Q14. (Derivation, 3 marks) Derive the potential energy of a dipole in a uniform field. When is it maximum and minimum? [2024]

Steps:

  1. Torque τ = pE sin θ. Work to rotate from θ0 to θ: W = ∫ pE sin θ dθ = pE (cos θ0 − cos θ).
  2. Take θ0 = 90° as zero energy: U(θ) = −pE cos θ = −p·E.
  3. Minimum U = −pE at θ = 0° (stable). Maximum U = +pE at θ = 180° (unstable).

Q15. (2 marks) Define equipotential surface. Draw them for (i) a point charge (ii) uniform field along z. Why is E always perpendicular to them? [2016, 2019]

Answer: Surface where potential is the same at every point. (i) concentric spheres around the charge, getting farther apart outward. (ii) equally spaced planes parallel to the x-y plane. No work is done moving a charge on it (ΔV = 0), so E has no component along the surface; E ⟂ surface.

Q16. (Numerical, 2 marks) How will you connect 6 μF, 9 μF and 18 μF capacitors to get (i) minimum (ii) maximum capacitance? [SQP]

Steps: (i) Series: 1/C = 1/6 + 1/9 + 1/18 = (3 + 2 + 1)/18 = 1/3 → C = 3 μF (minimum). (ii) Parallel: C = 6 + 9 + 18 = 33 μF (maximum).

Q17. (MCQ) Ten capacitors of 1 μF each are joined in parallel to 100 V. Total energy stored = ? [2024]

Answer: C = 10 μF. U = ½CV² = ½ × 10^-5 × 10^4 = 5.0 × 10^-2 J.

Q18. (Numerical, 3 marks) Two capacitors C1 and C2, first in series then in parallel across 100 V, store 0.045 J and 0.25 J. Find C1 and C2 and charge on each in parallel. [2015]

Steps:

  1. Parallel: ½(C1 + C2)(100)² = 0.25 → C1 + C2 = 5 × 10^-5 F = 50 μF.
  2. Series: ½ C1C2/(C1 + C2)² = 0.045 → C1C2/(C1 + C2) = 9 μF → C1C2 = 450 (μF)².
  3. Solve x² − 50x + 450 = 0 → x = 38.2 μF and 11.8 μF.
  4. Charges (parallel, V = 100 V): Q = CV → 3.82 × 10^-3 C and 1.18 × 10^-3 C.

Q19. (2 marks) What factors does the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor depend on? Why can an isolated conductor not have a large capacitance? [SQP]

Answer: C = Kε0A/d → depends on plate area A, separation d (C ∝ 1/d), and dielectric constant K of medium. Isolated sphere C = 4πε0R; even a sphere as big as the Earth has only about 711 μF, so a single conductor cannot have large C.

Q20. (Numerical, 2 marks) Show E = −dV/dr. Air breaks down at 2.5 × 10^6 V/m. A metal sphere must reach 5 × 10^5 V. Minimum radius? [SQP]

Steps: Work done moving unit charge by dr against E: dV = −E dr → E = −dV/dr. For a sphere, V = kQ/R and E = kQ/R² → E = V/R. R = V/E = 5 × 10^5 / 2.5 × 10^6 = 0.2 m.

Q21. (Numerical, 2 marks) Two isolated metal spheres of radii 1 cm and 3 cm have the same surface charge density 10^-9 C/m². They are joined by a thin wire. Find the new charge on the smaller sphere. [2024; 2016 similar idea]

Steps:

  1. Q1 = σ × 4πr1² = 10^-9 × 4π × 10^-4 = 4π × 10^-13 C; Q2 = 10^-9 × 4π × 9 × 10^-4 = 36π × 10^-13 C. Total Q = 40π × 10^-13 C.
  2. After joining, potentials equal: Q1'/r1 = Q2'/r2 → Q1' : Q2' = 1 : 3.
  3. Q1' = Q/4 = 10π × 10^-13 ≈ 3.14 × 10^-12 C. (Charge flows from the larger sphere to the smaller, since with equal σ the larger sphere is at higher potential.)

Chapter 3 – Current Electricity

Q22. (Derivation, 3 marks) Derive the relation I = neAvd. Also derive vd = eEτ/m. [2016, 2026]

Steps:

  1. Electron acceleration a = −eE/m. Averaged over relaxation time τ: vd = −eEτ/m (magnitude eEτ/m).
  2. In time Δt, electrons in length vdΔt cross area A: number = n A vd Δt; charge = neAvdΔt.
  3. I = charge/time = neAvd. (2026 part: if length doubled at same V, E = V/l halves, so vd halves.)

Q23. (Derivation, 3 marks) Show j = σE with σ = ne²τ/m, and resistivity ρ = m/(ne²τ). [2018, 2024, 2025]

Steps: j = I/A = nevd = ne(eEτ/m) = (ne²τ/m)E. So σ = ne²τ/m and ρ = 1/σ = m/(ne²τ). Current density is a vector; unit A/m².

Q24. (Numerical, 2 marks) At what temperature will a silver wire have twice its resistance at 20 °C? α = 4.0 × 10^-3 /K. [2024]

Steps: R = R0[1 + α(T − 20)]. 2R0 = R0[1 + 4 × 10^-3 (T − 20)] → T − 20 = 1/(4 × 10^-3) = 250 → T = 270 °C.

Q25. (MCQ) Cell's open-circuit pd is 8 V; it falls to 4 V when 4 A is drawn. Internal resistance = ? [2023]

Answer: r = (E − V)/I = (8 − 4)/4 = 1 Ω.

Q26. (Numerical, 2 marks) A battery of emf 21 V, internal resistance 3 Ω gives 3 A through a resistor. Find (i) resistor (ii) terminal voltage. [2026]

Steps: I = E/(R + r) → 3 = 21/(R + 3) → R = 4 Ω. V = E − Ir = 21 − 9 = 12 V (check: IR = 3 × 4 = 12 V).

Q27. (Numerical, 3 marks) With R = 4 Ω the current is 1 A; with R = 9 Ω it is 0.5 A. Find E and r. Draw V vs I. [2015]

Steps: E = 1(4 + r) and E = 0.5(9 + r). So 4 + r = 4.5 + 0.5r → r = 1 Ω, E = 5 V. Graph: V = E − Ir is a straight line falling from V = E (at I = 0) with slope −r.

Q28. (Numerical, 2 marks) Battery 12 V, r = 2 Ω, connected to 4 Ω. Show voltmeter across cell and across resistor read the same. Why is voltmeter in parallel and ammeter in series? [2016; 2017 similar]

Steps: I = 12/(4 + 2) = 2 A. Across resistor: 2 × 4 = 8 V. Across cell: 12 − 2 × 2 = 8 V. Same. Ammeter has very low resistance (does not change current); voltmeter has very high resistance (draws almost no current).

Q29. (Derivation, 3 marks) Using Kirchhoff's rules, get the balance condition of a Wheatstone bridge. [2015, 2024; 2026 A-R; SQP MCQ]

Steps:

  1. Arms P (AB), Q (BC), R (AD), S (DC); galvanometer between B and D; cell across A and C.
  2. At balance Ig = 0, so current I1 in P and Q, I2 in R and S.
  3. Loop ABDA: −I1P + I2R = 0 (Ig = 0) → I1P = I2R.
  4. Loop BCDB: −I1Q + I2S = 0 → I1Q = I2S.
  5. Divide: P/Q = R/S. (Swapping cell and galvanometer does not change balance.) Kirchhoff rules: Junction rule – sum of currents at a junction = 0 (charge conservation). Loop rule – sum of emfs = sum of IR drops in a closed loop (energy conservation).

Q30. (MCQ) Bulbs rated 200 V–60 W and 200 V–100 W. Ratio R1/R2 = ? [SQP]

Answer: R = V²/P. R1/R2 = P2/P1 = 100/60 = 5 : 3.

Q31. (MCQ) Two identical heaters (1000 W, 220 V) in parallel across 220 V. Total power? [SQP]

Answer: Each works at rated voltage, P = 1000 + 1000 = 2000 W.

Q32. (2 marks) Bulbs P and Q have resistances in ratio 1 : 2 and are in series. Ratio of powers? [2018]

Answer: Same current in series, P = I²R → PP : PQ = 1 : 2. (In parallel it would be 2 : 1, since P = V²/R.)

Q33. (2 marks) Describe the path of free electrons in a conductor (a) with an electric field (b) without it. [SQP]

Answer: (a) Random zig-zag motion with collisions, plus a slow net drift opposite to E → current flows. (b) Only random thermal motion in all directions; average velocity zero → no current.

Chapter 4 – Moving Charges and Magnetism

Q34. (Derivation, 3 marks) Using Biot–Savart law, find B at the centre of a circular loop of radius R carrying I (and state B on the axis). [2016, 2017, 2023; 2025 asked vector form]

Steps:

  1. Biot–Savart: dB = (μ0/4π) I (dl × r) / r³, magnitude dB = (μ0/4π) I dl sin θ / r².
  2. At centre, every element is at distance R and dl ⟂ r (θ = 90°), all dB in the same direction.
  3. B = (μ0/4π)(I/R²) × ∮dl = (μ0/4π)(I/R²)(2πR) = μ0I / (2R).
  4. On the axis at distance x: B = μ0IR² / [2(R² + x²)^(3/2)], along the axis (right-hand thumb rule). Diagram: loop with field lines passing through centre like a small bar magnet.

Q35. (MCQ) A long wire with a semicircular loop of radius 10 cm carries 5 A. B at centre of the semicircle? [SQP]

Answer: B = μ0I/(4R) = 4π × 10^-7 × 5/(4 × 0.1) = 5π × 10^-6 T = 5π μT. (Straight parts pass through the centre line, so give zero.)

Q36. (2 marks) State Ampere's circuital law and use it to find B due to a long straight wire. [syllabus; 2015 used Ampere's law]

Answer: ∮B·dl = μ0 I(enclosed). Take a circle of radius r around the wire: B × 2πr = μ0I → B = μ0I/(2πr). Field lines are concentric circles (2025 MCQ).

Q37. (Numerical, 3 marks) A proton (KE 1.3384 × 10^-14 J) moving horizontally north to south enters B = 2.0 mT pointing east. q/m = 1.0 × 10^8 C/kg. Find (a) speed (b) acceleration (c) radius. [2024]

Steps:

  1. m = q/(q/m) = 1.6 × 10^-19 / 10^8 = 1.6 × 10^-27 kg.
  2. v = √(2K/m) = √(2 × 1.3384 × 10^-14 / 1.6 × 10^-27) = √(1.673 × 10^13) ≈ 4.09 × 10^6 m/s.
  3. a = qvB/m = (q/m)vB = 10^8 × 4.09 × 10^6 × 2 × 10^-3 ≈ 8.2 × 10^11 m/s².
  4. r = mv/(qB) = v/[(q/m)B] = 4.09 × 10^6 / (2 × 10^5) ≈ 20.4 m.

Q38. (Numerical, 3 marks) An alpha particle (m = 6.4 × 10^-27 kg, q = 3.2 × 10^-19 C) of energy 8.0 MeV enters B = 0.5 T at right angles. Find radius. When does it move in a helix / go undeviated? [2026]

Steps:

  1. K = 8 × 1.6 × 10^-13 = 1.28 × 10^-12 J.
  2. p = √(2mK) = √(2 × 6.4 × 10^-27 × 1.28 × 10^-12) = 1.28 × 10^-19 kg m/s.
  3. r = p/(qB) = 1.28 × 10^-19 / (3.2 × 10^-19 × 0.5) = 0.8 m.
  4. Helix: velocity at an angle (not 0°, 90°, 180°) to B. Undeviated: velocity parallel or antiparallel to B (F = qvB sin θ = 0).

Q39. (2 marks) Find the condition for a charged particle to pass undeviated through crossed E and B fields (velocity selector). [2017]

Answer: v, E and B mutually perpendicular with electric and magnetic forces opposite. qE = qvB → v = E/B. Only particles with this speed go straight.

Q40. (Numerical, 3 marks) Two long parallel wires A and B, 2.5 cm apart, carry 5.0 A and 2.5 A in opposite directions. Find force on 10 cm of wire B. Define 1 ampere. [2023; 2019, 2024 A-R similar]

Chapter 6 – Electromagnetic Induction

Q49. (2 marks) State Faraday's laws and Lenz's law. Show Lenz's law follows conservation of energy. [2026; SQP]

Answer: Faraday: induced emf e = −dΦ/dt (N turns: −N dΦ/dt). Lenz: the induced current opposes the change that produces it. If a magnet's N-pole is pushed towards a coil, the near face becomes N and repels it; we must do work against this repulsion, and that work appears as electrical energy. If the current helped the motion, we would get energy for nothing – impossible.

Q50. (2 marks) A bar magnet falls through a metal ring. Why is its acceleration less than g? [SQP]

Answer: Changing flux induces a current in the ring. By Lenz's law this current opposes the motion (repels when approaching, attracts when leaving), so there is an upward force → a < g.

Q51. (Numerical, 1–2 marks) A square loop of side 50 cm is in B = 3.0 T perpendicular to its plane. It is rotated by 90° in 0.3 s. Average emf? [2026]

Q53. (Derivation, 3 marks) Define mutual inductance. Derive M for two long coaxial solenoids of the same length l. [2015, 2017, 2023 (coil on solenoid)]

Q57. (Derivation, 3–5 marks) Using a phasor diagram, get the impedance of a series LCR circuit and the resonance condition. [2023, 2025, 2026, SQP; 2019]

Steps:

  1. Same current i = i0 sin ωt in all. VR in phase with i; VL leads i by 90°; VC lags i by 90°.
  2. Phasors: VR along i; (VL − VC) perpendicular.
  3. V² = VR² + (VL − VC)² → V = i √[R² + (XL − XC)²].
  4. Z = √[R² + (XL − XC)²], tan φ = (XL − XC)/R, XL = ωL, XC = 1/(ωC).
  5. Resonance: XL = XC → ω0 = 1/√(LC), f0 = 1/(2π√(LC)). Then Z = R (minimum), current maximum, circuit acts purely resistive, VL and VC are 180° out of phase.
  6. Use: radio tuning – changing C changes f0; the station whose frequency equals f0 gives maximum current (SQP). Graph: current vs ω rises to a sharp peak at ω0.

Q58. (Derivation, 3 marks) Derive average power in an ac circuit; show it is zero for an ideal inductor. [2024, 2016]

Steps: v = v0 sin ωt, i = i0 sin(ωt − φ). p = vi = v0i0 sin ωt sin(ωt − φ) = (v0i0/2)[cos φ − cos(2ωt − φ)]. Average of cos(2ωt − φ) over a cycle = 0. So P = Vrms Irms cos φ. Ideal inductor: φ = 90°, cos φ = 0 → P = 0 (wattless current). Power factor = cos φ = R/Z.

Q59. (Numerical, 3 marks) A lamp is designed for 110 V dc and 11 A. To run it on 220 V, 50 Hz ac, a coil is put in series. Find the inductance. [2024]

  2. Needed Z = 220/11 = 20 Ω.
  3. XL = √(Z² − R²) = √(400 − 100) = √300 ≈ 17.3 Ω.
  4. L = XL/(2πf) = 17.3/314 ≈ 0.055 H (55 mH).

Q60. (Numerical, 3 marks) An inductor draws 1 A from 200 V dc but only 0.5 A from 200 V, 50 Hz ac. Why? Find L. [2019]

Q61. (3 marks) A bulb is in series with an inductor and an ac source. What happens to brightness if (i) an iron rod is put inside the coil (ii) a capacitor with XC = XL is added (iii) frequency is decreased? [2015, 2016, 2026]

Q62. (Long, 5 marks) Draw a labelled diagram of a step-up transformer, state its principle and working, and give three energy losses. Does it violate energy conservation? Numerical: Np = 200, Ns = 3000, Vp = 90 V, Is = 2.0 A; find Vs and Ip. [2024; 2015, 2016, 2018, 2019, SQP]

Chapter 10 – Wave Optics

Q79. (Derivation, 3 marks) Using Huygens' principle, derive Snell's law of refraction. Which prediction of wave theory was later verified? [SQP; 2017, 2019 (or), 2016]

Steps:

  1. Plane wavefront AB (in medium 1, speed v1) strikes surface PP' at angle i. While B travels BC = v1t to reach the surface at C, a secondary wavelet from A travels AE = v2t in medium 2.
  2. Tangent CE from C to the wavelet (radius v2t) is the refracted wavefront, at angle r.
  3. In triangle ABC: sin i = BC/AC = v1t/AC. In triangle AEC: sin r = AE/AC = v2t/AC.
  4. sin i / sin r = v1/v2 = constant = n21 (= n2/n1) → Snell's law.
  5. Prediction: speed of light in a denser medium is less than in air (v2 < v1 when light bends towards normal) – later verified experimentally (Foucault). Diagram: incident wavefront AB, surface, arc of radius v2t from A, refracted wavefront EC.

Q80. (Derivation, 3 marks) State Huygens' principle; prove i = r for reflection using it. [2018, 2019, 2024]

Answer: Huygens: (1) every point on a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets moving with the wave's speed; (2) the forward envelope (common tangent) of these wavelets gives the new wavefront. Proof: Wavefront AB hits mirror at A. In time t, B reaches C (BC = vt). Wavelet from A has radius AE = vt. Tangent CE is the reflected wavefront. Triangles ABC and CEA are congruent (common AC, right angles at B and E, BC = AE) → i = r.

  3. Substitute: m[n²h²/(4π²m²r²)]/r = ke²/r² → r = n²h²/(4π²mke²) = ε0n²h²/(πme²).
  4. So rn ∝ n² (r1 = 0.53 Å). vn = 2πke²/(nh) ∝ 1/n. En = −13.6/n² eV.
  5. Total energy E = −ke²/2r; KE = +ke²/2r; PE = −ke²/r (2025 case).

Q99. (Numerical, 2 marks) Ground-state energy of H is −13.6 eV. Find KE and PE in the third excited state. [2023]

Steps: Third excited state = n = 4. E = −13.6/16 = −0.85 eV. KE = −E = 0.85 eV. PE = 2E = −1.70 eV.

Q100. (Numerical, 2 marks) H atom in ground state absorbs a photon and goes to n = 4. Frequency of photon? [2018; 2023 (n = 5)]

Steps: ΔE = 13.6(1 − 1/16) = 12.75 eV = 12.75 × 1.6 × 10^-19 J. ν = ΔE/h = 2.04 × 10^-18 / 6.63 × 10^-34 ≈ 3.1 × 10^15 Hz.

Q101. (Numerical, 2 marks) An electron jumps from −1.51 eV to −3.4 eV. Wavelength emitted and series? [2017]

Steps: ΔE = 1.89 eV. λ = 1240/1.89 ≈ 656 nm (red). −1.51 eV is n = 3, −3.4 eV is n = 2 → Balmer series (visible).

Q102. (MCQ) The second line of the Balmer series in H corresponds to the transition: [2024]

Answer: ni = 4 → nf = 2. (First line: 3 → 2.) Series: Lyman (to n = 1, UV), Balmer (to n = 2, visible), Paschen (to n = 3, IR) – energy-level diagram asked 2023.

Q103. (A-R / 3 marks) Rutherford's α-scattering: why do most α-particles pass undeflected? Give drawbacks of Rutherford's model and how Bohr removed them. [SQP (A-R answer B); 2024; 2015]

Answer: Most of the atom is empty space; the positive charge and mass are in a tiny nucleus (≈ 10^-15 m vs atom ≈ 10^-10 m). (In the SQP A-R, both statements are true but R is not the correct reason → B.) Drawbacks: (1) an accelerating (revolving) electron should radiate energy, spiral into the nucleus – atom would be unstable; (2) it predicts a continuous spectrum, but hydrogen gives a line spectrum. Bohr: electrons move only in certain stationary orbits without radiating; radiation is emitted only during a jump, hν = Ei − Ef. Ionisation energy of H = 13.6 eV (SQP).

Chapter 13 – Nuclei

Q104. (Numerical, 3 marks) Atomic mass of O-16 is 15.99493 u. mp = 1.00727 u, mn = 1.00866 u, 1 u = 931.5 MeV. Find binding energy per nucleon. [SQP]

Steps:

  1. 8 protons + 8 neutrons: 8(1.00727) + 8(1.00866) = 8.05816 + 8.06928 = 16.12744 u.
  2. Mass defect Δm = 16.12744 − 15.99493 = 0.13251 u.
  3. BE = 0.13251 × 931.5 ≈ 123.4 MeV.
  4. BE per nucleon = 123.4/16 ≈ 7.71 MeV.

Q105. (Derivation, 2–3 marks) Using R = R0 A^(1/3), show nuclear density is the same for all nuclei. [2015, 2019, 2023, 2024]

Steps: Mass ≈ mA (m = mass of a nucleon). Volume = (4/3)πR³ = (4/3)πR0³A. Density = mA / [(4/3)πR0³A] = 3m/(4πR0³) – no A, so same for all nuclei (≈ 2.3 × 10^17 kg/m³). Nuclear density >> atomic density because nearly all mass is packed in a tiny nucleus. 2026 MCQ: r1/r2 for A = 64 and 27 = (64/27)^(1/3) = 4/3.

Q106. (3 marks) Draw the BE/A vs mass number curve. Give two features. Why do light nuclei fuse and heavy nuclei split? [2023, 2018; SQP MCQ]

Answer: Curve rises steeply for light nuclei, peaks at about 8.75 MeV near A = 56 (iron – most stable), then falls slowly to about 7.6 MeV at A = 238. Features: (1) nearly constant (≈ 8 MeV) for A = 30–170 – nuclear force is short-range (saturation); (2) lower for very light and very heavy nuclei. Fusion of light nuclei and fission of heavy nuclei both move towards higher BE/A, so energy is released.

Q107. (3 marks) Differentiate between nuclear fission and fusion with one example each. [2026; 2015]

Answer: Fission – a heavy nucleus splits into two medium nuclei: 235U + 1n → 144Ba + 89Kr + 3 1n + about 200 MeV. Happens with slow neutrons, used in reactors. Fusion – light nuclei join to form a heavier one: 2H + 3H → 4He + 1n + 17.6 MeV. Needs very high temperature (~10^7 K), powers the Sun.

Q108. (2 marks) Define mass defect and binding energy. Give two properties of nuclear force. [2026; 2024]

Answer: Mass defect Δm = (Zmp + Nmn) − M(nucleus). Binding energy = Δm c² = energy needed to separate the nucleus into its nucleons. Nuclear force: very strong (much stronger than Coulomb), short range (a few fm), charge-independent, attractive at 0.8–2 fm but repulsive below about 0.8 fm. (2026 A-R "nuclear forces are always attractive / R: nuclear force is weak" → both false → D.)

Chapter 14 – Semiconductor Electronics

Q109. (3 marks) Draw energy band diagrams of a conductor, insulator and semiconductor. Why is diamond an insulator but tin a conductor (both group IV)? Why is Si used, not diamond? [SQP; 2025 MCQ (Sn conducts)]

Answer: Conductor – valence and conduction bands overlap (or partly filled conduction band). Insulator – large gap Eg > 3 eV (diamond 5.4 eV). Semiconductor – small gap, Eg < 3 eV (Si 1.1 eV, Ge 0.7 eV). Diamond's gap is too big for thermal excitation at room temperature; in tin the bands overlap so free electrons are available. Silicon's small gap lets electrons cross with thermal energy, and conduction can be controlled by doping.

Q110. (2 marks) Distinguish intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductors. How are charge carriers created in an intrinsic semiconductor? [2015, 2023, 2026]

Answer: Intrinsic: pure; ne = nh = ni; conductivity depends only on temperature. Extrinsic: doped; ne ≠ nh; conductivity depends on doping and temperature. In intrinsic, thermal energy breaks some covalent bonds; each freed electron goes to the conduction band and leaves a hole in the valence band (electron-hole pairs).

Q111. (2 marks) Ge is doped with arsenic. What type is formed? What are majority/minority carriers in n- and p-type? [2024 MCQ; 2025, 2023]

Answer: As is pentavalent (donor) → n-type; number of conduction electrons increases. n-type: majority electrons, minority holes. p-type (trivalent dopant: B, Al, Ga, In – acceptor): majority holes, minority electrons. Even so, n-type is electrically neutral (each donor ion is positive, balancing the extra electron) – 2025 A-R answer (A). Donor level is about 0.01 eV (Ge) to 0.05 eV (Si) below the conduction band (2023 MCQ: order 0.01 eV).

Q112. (Numerical, 2 marks) Pure Si has ni = 1.5 × 10^16 m^-3. On doping, hole concentration becomes 4.5 × 10^22 m^-3. Identify dopant type and find electron concentration. [Type seen 2025; NCERT values]

Steps: Holes increase → trivalent (acceptor) dopant → p-type. ne × nh = ni² → ne = (1.5 × 10^16)² / 4.5 × 10^22 = 2.25 × 10^32 / 4.5 × 10^22 = 5 × 10^9 m^-3.

Q113. (3 marks) Explain formation of depletion region and potential barrier in a p-n junction (role of diffusion and drift currents). [2016, 2023, 2025]

Answer: Electrons diffuse from n to p and holes from p to n (concentration difference) → diffusion current (p to n). They leave behind fixed positive donor ions on the n-side and fixed negative acceptor ions on the p-side near the junction → a layer with no free carriers = depletion region. These ions set up an electric field from n to p, which pushes minority carriers across → drift current (n to p). At equilibrium diffusion current = drift current; the potential difference across the layer is the barrier potential (≈ 0.7 V Si, 0.3 V Ge). Diagram: p | depletion layer (− ions on p side, + ions on n side) | n, with field arrow from n to p.

Q114. (3 marks) With circuit diagrams, explain forward and reverse bias. Draw the V–I characteristic of a Si diode. [2026, 2023]

Answer: Forward bias: p to + terminal, n to −. Barrier and depletion width decrease; current rises sharply after about 0.7 V (knee/threshold voltage); current in mA. Reverse bias: p to −, n to +. Barrier and depletion width increase; tiny reverse current (μA) due to minority carriers, almost constant until breakdown. Graph: forward curve in first quadrant rising steeply after 0.7 V; reverse curve in third quadrant almost flat and close to the axis. Minority carrier injection happens in forward bias (2026 MCQ). Forward resistance = ΔV/ΔI, e.g. (1.0 − 0.8) V / 2 mA = 100 Ω (2026 MCQ).

Q115. (3 marks) Draw a full-wave rectifier circuit and explain its working with input and output waveforms. What is the output ripple frequency for 50 Hz input in half-wave and full-wave? [2017, 2018, 2019, 2024 case; 2016 half-wave]

Answer: Centre-tap transformer, two diodes D1, D2 and load RL. In the first half cycle top end A is +ve: D1 forward biased conducts, D2 reverse biased; current through RL in one direction. In the next half, B is +ve: D2 conducts, D1 off; current through RL in the same direction. So both halves give output in one direction – pulsating dc. At any instant one diode is forward and the other reverse biased (2024 MCQ); each diode conducts for half a cycle. Half-wave (one diode) gives output only in alternate half cycles. Ripple frequency: half-wave 50 Hz, full-wave 100 Hz. Waveforms: input sine; half-wave output = only positive humps with gaps; full-wave output = continuous positive humps.

Key definitions (one line each)

  • Electric flux: Φ = ∮E·dA (scalar, N m² C^-1).

7. Answer-writing tips and 30-day timetable

How to grab step marks

  1. Numericals: Write "Given:" (with units in SI), the formula, substitution, then the answer with unit. In CBSE marking schemes, formula and substitution each usually carry ½ to 1 mark, even if the final arithmetic is wrong.
  2. Derivations: Always draw the labelled diagram first (often ½–1 mark). Number your steps. Box the final result.
  3. Graphs: Label both axes with quantity and unit, mark the special point (threshold frequency, stopping potential, knee voltage, ω0, peak at A = 56).
  4. Definitions: Write the definition plus formula plus SI unit. That is usually full marks for a 1–2 mark question.
  5. Assertion–Reason: First decide if A is true, then if R is true, then if R explains A. Write only the option letter clearly.
  6. Case-based: Most parts are 1-mark MCQs. Read the paragraph – the answer is often written in it.
  7. Choice questions: In Section E, choose the option with a derivation you know plus a simple numerical.
  8. Never leave blanks. Write the relevant formula at least – it can get partial credit.
  9. Units and signs: In optics use sign convention (distances against the light are negative). Convert cm to m for power (P = 100/f(cm)).
  10. Time plan (180 min): Section A 25 min, B 20 min, C 45 min, D 15 min, E 50 min, revision 25 min.
