Who this is for: students who need to pass, meaning at least 33% in the theory paper and 33% in internal assessment. How to use it: First learn the writing formats (Section 6a). They give the easiest marks in the paper. Then learn the starred (★) answers in Section 5. Practise by writing the answers, not only reading them.

3. The 33-mark plan (target about 40-45% = 32-36 marks in theory)

Main idea: English has no "deleted" safe chapter and no numericals, but it has many easy marks: format marks in writing, MCQs in the passages and extracts, and short answers that come again and again. Attempt every question. There is no negative marking, and a half-correct answer in simple English still earns marks.

3a. Study order (easiest and highest return first)

  1. Writing formats (18 marks available): learn the 6 formats in Section 6a by heart. The format alone is 4 marks (1 in each question). Then learn a few ready-made "content" lines.
  2. Reading (22 marks available): no learning, only practice. Most answers are in the passage. Learn the tricks in Section 7a.
  3. Extract questions Q7-Q9 (16 marks): most are MCQs or "complete the sentence". If you know the story/poem summary (Section 6c), you can answer them.
  4. The 5 poems: short, and asked every year (extract or short answer). Learn the poet, the theme and 2-3 key lines of each.
  5. Flamingo prose short answers (Q10): learn the ★ answers in Section 5.
  6. Vistas (Q8, Q11, Q13): learn the ★ answers. The Enemy, On the Face of It and The Tiger King come almost every year.
  7. Long answers (Q12, Q13): learn 4 ready long answers (Section 5, Part G). Even a half-correct long answer gets 2-3 marks.

3c. How internal assessment saves you

IA is 20 marks, and you need 7 to pass it. Most students get high marks here, but only if they:

  • Submit the project on time (5 marks for the written report/script, up to about 1000 words, and 5 marks for the viva). Suggested projects from the curriculum: an interview-based research report, a report on a podcast or TV documentary, an audio/video script, or a one-act play.
  • Speak in the speaking test, even simply. Marks are given for interaction, fluency, pronunciation, and vocabulary/grammar. Saying something is always better than staying silent.
  • Practise the listening test with the teacher's audio clips.

IA marks do not add to your theory pass mark. You must still get 27 out of 80 in the written paper.

5. Must-do question bank (80 questions)

★ = asked many times, so do this first. Years in brackets are board papers (from those read) where the same or a very similar question appeared. "SQP" = 2026-27 Sample Paper. Model answers follow the 2026-27 marking scheme where it covers the same question; the rest are written to the NCERT text. Word limits: short answers 40-50 words; long answers 120-150 words. Part A: Writing (Q1-Q9), with full sample answers

Q1 ★ Notice (4 marks; notices in 2019, 2020, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026, SQP). You are Shivani/Shivam, President, Sports Club of ABC International School, Newtown. Write a notice about an inter-house handball tournament, with participation criteria, registration process and contact information. Put it in a box. (2026)

Model answer (write it inside a box):

ABC INTERNATIONAL SCHOOL, NEWTOWN NOTICE 10 November 20XX INTER-HOUSE HANDBALL TOURNAMENT The Sports Club is organising an Inter-House Handball Tournament for students of Classes IX-XII. Dates: 25-27 November 20XX | Time: 2 pm-5 pm | Venue: School Sports Ground Eligibility: each house can send one team of 12 players (boys and girls). Registration: give names to your House Captain by 18 November. For details, contact the undersigned. (Signature) Shivani President, Sports Club

Checklist for full format mark: box, school name at top centre, NOTICE, date on the left, heading, and name + designation + signature at the bottom left. Answer what, when (date, time), where, who can take part, how to register, whom to contact.

Q2 Notice (4 marks). As Secretary of Saket Kunj RWA, Navi Mumbai, write a notice telling residents there will be no water supply for two days due to repair work. Mention dates and the timings when tankers will be available. (2023)

Model answer (in a box):

SAKET KUNJ RESIDENT WELFARE ASSOCIATION, NAVI MUMBAI NOTICE 5 March 20XX NO WATER SUPPLY ON 8 AND 9 MARCH All residents are informed that there will be no water supply on 8 and 9 March 20XX because of urgent repair work on the main pipeline. Water tankers will be available in the RWA premises from 7 am to 9 am and from 5 pm to 7 pm on both days. Please store water in advance and use it carefully. (Signature) Anu Secretary, RWA

Q3 ★ Formal invitation, third person (4 marks; invitations in 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026, SQP). You are the President of the Student Council of XYZ Public School, Hawapur. On behalf of the Principal, draft a formal invitation card for parents and guests to an Arts and Crafts Exhibition on Annual Day. Mention a highlight. (2026)

Model answer:

XYZ PUBLIC SCHOOL, HAWAPUR The Principal, staff and students request the pleasure of your company at the Arts and Crafts Exhibition held as part of our Annual Day Celebrations on Saturday, 15 December 20XX, at 10 am in the School Auditorium. Highlight: a live pottery and clay-modelling show by our students. Chief Guest: Shri R. K. Verma, District Education Officer RSVP: President, Student Council (Ph. 98XXXXXX10)

Rules: a formal invitation card is written in the third person ("request the pleasure of your company"). There is no "Dear Sir" and no signature. Give the occasion, day, date, time, venue, chief guest, and RSVP.

Q5 Reply declining an invitation (4 marks; 2023, 2024). You are Vineeta/Vinay Sharma. Draft a formal reply declining an invitation to dinner on 28 February at Army Parade Grounds, hosted by Major J. P. Gautam. (2023)

Q6 ★ Letter to the Editor (5 marks; 2019, 2020, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026, SQP). Waste management in your colony is a grave concern. Write a letter to the editor of a local daily giving the reasons and suggesting solutions. You are Shireen/Sahil of Prestige Colony, Rahagarh. (2026)

Model answer (about 150 words):

15, Prestige Colony Rahagarh 10 November 20XX

The Editor The Daily Times Rahagarh

Subject: Poor waste management in Prestige Colony

Sir, Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper, I wish to draw the attention of the authorities to the poor waste management in our colony.

Garbage bins overflow for days, and stray animals spread waste on the roads. The foul smell and flies have made life difficult, and diseases like dengue and diarrhoea are spreading. The main reasons are lack of awareness about separating wet and dry waste, irregular collection by the civic body, and too few bins.

The civic authorities should collect waste every day and place separate bins for wet and dry waste. The government should help them get more trucks and workers. Residents' associations and schools can run awareness drives on segregation and composting. Fines for littering will also help.

I hope the authorities will act soon.

Yours truly, Sahil (Resident, Prestige Colony)

Format (1 mark): sender's address, date, "The Editor" + newspaper + city, subject, "Sir/Madam", and "Yours truly" (the marking scheme says to use "Yours truly" for a letter to the editor). Organisation: para 1 = why you are writing; para 2 = the problem and its effects; para 3 = suggestions; last line = hope for action.

Q8 ★ Article (5 marks; articles in 2019, 2020, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026, SQP). You are Rajni/Raghav, upset about the increase in cyber bullying among school students. Write an article exploring the reasons and suggesting solutions. (2026)

Model answer (about 150 words):

CYBER BULLYING: STOP THE HIDDEN HURT By Raghav

Today almost every teenager has a phone, and with it has come a new danger: cyber bullying. Hurtful messages, fake profiles and embarrassing photos are making many students anxious, lonely and afraid to go to school.

Why does it happen? Peer pressure makes some students join in to look "cool". Low self-esteem makes others hurt people to feel powerful. A lack of empathy, and the freedom to hide behind a screen, make bullies feel safe.

The answer lies with all of us. Schools must teach safe online behaviour and set up clear rules and a simple way to report bullying. Parents should talk to their children and watch for signs of stress. Students must never forward hurtful posts, and must support the victim.

The internet should be a safe space for learning and friendship. If we act together, it can be.

Format: a heading and a by-line (your name) = 1 mark. Organisation: intro (the problem), body (causes, effects), solutions, conclusion.

Q9 ★ Report (5 marks; reports in 2019, 2020, 2023, 2025, 2026, SQP). You are Prem Kumar, Secretary of the Health Club, Sarvodaya Vidyalaya, New Delhi. Write a report for the school magazine on the launch of the "Tobacco Free Youth Campaign" in your school. (SQP 2026-27)

Model answer (about 150 words):

SCHOOL SAYS NO TO TOBACCO By Prem Kumar, Secretary, Health Club New Delhi, 12 November 20XX

The Health Club of Sarvodaya Vidyalaya launched the "Tobacco Free Youth Campaign" on 10 November 20XX, in support of the national goal of a tobacco-free India. The Principal flagged off the campaign in the morning assembly.

More than 300 students joined an awareness rally from the school gate to the local market. They carried posters with slogans like "Choose Life, Not Tobacco". Format (from the 2026-27 marking scheme): headline, by-line (name and designation), place and date. Para 1: what, why, who, when, where. Para 2: how it happened (details). Para 3: steps taken or to be taken / result.

Part B: Reading skills (Q10-Q12), solved from the 2026-27 SQP

You cannot learn the passage in advance, but you can learn the question types. These three are from the SQP with the official answers.

Q10 (MCQ / tone, SQP) The passage about the Geminids meteor shower gives facts on its origin, causes and how to watch it. The writer's tone is best described as: (A) persuasive and promotional (B) explanatory and informative, aimed at scientific understanding (C) speculative and argumentative (D) celebratory and emotional.

Ans: (B). The passage explains facts in a neutral way. (Tip: factual passages are almost always "informative / objective".)

Q11 (find evidence, SQP) Which evidence from paragraph 1 tells us that the Geminids are exceptionally active?

Ans: "Observation rates can exceed 100 meteors per hour." (Tip: "evidence" = copy the exact line or number from the paragraph named.)

Q12 (case-based data, 2 marks, SQP) UPI transactions grew from 4.21 billion (Oct 2021) to 16.58 billion (Oct 2024), and their value from ₹7.71 lakh crore to ₹23.49 lakh crore. Rohan pays for groceries and transport by phone. Give two reasons why UPI is more suitable for him in 2024 than in 2021.

Ans (any two, 1 mark each): (1) The huge rise in the number of transactions shows the system is now reliable and stable for daily use. (2) It is now accepted almost everywhere, even by small roadside vendors, so he does not need cash. (3) The rise in value shows people trust UPI even for bigger payments.

Part C: Flamingo prose, short answers 40-50 words (Q13-Q37)

The Last Lesson (Alphonse Daudet)

Q13 ★ (2019) Whom did M. Hamel blame for the neglect of learning French by boys like Franz?

Ans: M. Hamel blamed everyone. The parents preferred to send children to work on farms and in mills to earn money. Franz himself was lazy and kept putting off learning. M. Hamel blamed himself too, because he sent pupils to water his flowers and gave a holiday when he wanted to go fishing.

Q14 ★ (2024, 2026) "I won't scold you, little Franz; you must feel bad enough." Why must Franz feel bad? / How did the people realise the value of their language only when they were about to lose it?

Ans: Franz could not say the rule for participles, and it was the last French lesson, as an order from Berlin said only German would be taught in Alsace and Lorraine. He felt sorry for wasting time. The villagers sitting in the class also regretted not learning French; the fear of losing it made them value it.

Q15 What did M. Hamel say about the French language and about language in general?

Ans: He said French was the most beautiful language in the world, the clearest and the most logical. He asked them to guard it, because when a people are enslaved, as long as they hold fast to their language, it is as if they had the key to their prison.

Lost Spring (Anees Jung)

Q16 ★ (2023, 2026, SQP) What vicious circle are the bangle makers of Firozabad trapped in? Why can they not imagine a different future?

Ans: They are trapped by the sahukars, middlemen, policemen, keepers of law, bureaucrats and politicians, who together keep them poor. They are also bound by caste and the belief that bangle-making is their God-given lineage (karam). Fear of the police, lack of education and of leaders keep them from organising, so suffering seems unchangeable.

Q17 ★ (2019, long answer idea) "Seemapuri, a place on the periphery of Delhi, yet miles away from it, metaphorically." Explain.

Ans: Seemapuri is close to Delhi, but life there is very different. About 10,000 ragpickers, refugees from Bangladesh (1971), live in mud huts with no sewage, drainage or running water. They have ration cards but no identity. For their children, garbage is "gold", their daily bread. Delhi's comforts are far away from them.

Q18 What was Saheb's new job, and was he happy with it?

Ans: Saheb started working at a tea stall for 800 rupees and all his meals. He was not happy. Earlier, as a ragpicker, he was his own master. Now the steel canister he carried was heavier than the plastic bag on his shoulder, because the canister belonged to the tea-stall owner. He had lost his freedom.

Deep Water (William O. Douglas)

Q19 ★ (2020, 2023) How did Douglas's fear of water start?

Ans: At three or four, a wave knocked him down on a California beach and frightened him. At ten or eleven, at the YMCA pool, a big boy of about eighteen picked him up and threw him into the deep end. He nearly drowned, going down three times, and the terror of water stayed with him for years.

Q20 ★ (2019, 2025) Why was Douglas determined to overcome his fear, and how did the instructor make him a swimmer?

Ans: The fear spoiled his fishing, canoeing and swimming, so he decided to beat it. He hired an instructor, who tied a rope to a belt round him through a pulley and taught him, bit by bit for months, to put his face in water, exhale under water, inhale above it, and kick his legs. Finally he taught all of him to swim.

Q21 ★ (SQP, 2026) What does Douglas's experience suggest about fear?

Ans: Fear is made worse by anticipation, by imagining what may happen. His terror came more from the memory and imagined danger than from water itself. Through steady practice he overcame it and learned that "All we have to fear is fear itself" (President Roosevelt). Facing fear brings freedom.

The Rattrap (Selma Lagerlöf)

Q22 ★ (2023) Who was Edla Willmansson? Why did she come to the forge at night?

Ans: Edla was the ironmaster's daughter. Her father had failed to persuade the peddler, whom he took for his old comrade Captain von Stahle, to come home for Christmas. Edla went to the forge out of kindness. Her gentle, friendly manner made the peddler agree to come.

Q23 ★ (2024) What did the peddler say in his defence when it was clear he was not the person the ironmaster thought him to be?

Ans: He said it was not his fault. He had never pretended to be anyone but a poor trader; it was the ironmaster who mistook him. He said the whole world is a big rattrap, and warned that the ironmaster too might one day be tempted by good things and be caught.

Q24 ★ (SQP) How did the Christmas episode change the peddler?

Ans: Edla treated him like a captain, with trust and kindness, even after she knew the truth, and asked that he spend Christmas with them. This woke his conscience. He left the thirty kronor he had stolen from the old crofter in a small rattrap as a gift for her, signing himself Captain von Stahle. Kindness freed him from his own "rattrap".

Indigo (Louis Fischer)

Q25 ★ (2020) What was the arrangement between the sharecroppers of Champaran and their British landlords?

Ans: Most of the land was owned by British planters and worked by Indian tenants. By a long-term contract, the peasants had to plant 15% of their land (3 parts in 20) with indigo and give the whole indigo harvest to the landlords as rent. This was called the tinkathia system.

Q26 ★ (2026) How did Rajendra Prasad's servants treat Gandhi?

Ans: Gandhi went with Rajkumar Shukla to Rajendra Prasad's house in Patna. The servants knew Shukla as a poor yeoman and thought Gandhi was another peasant. They let him stay on the grounds but did not let him draw water from the well, in case drops from his bucket polluted it, because they did not know his caste.

Q27 ★ (2025) Give an account of Gandhi's reception at Motihari.

Ans: At Motihari, a huge crowd greeted Gandhi at the station. He was served a notice to leave Champaran at once, but he refused. The next day thousands of peasants gathered around the court. This spontaneous demonstration was the beginning of their freedom from fear of the British. The case was later dropped.

Q28 ★ (2019) Why did Gandhi consider the Champaran episode a turning point in his life?

Ans: Gandhi said, "What I did was a very ordinary thing. I declared that the British could not order me about in my own country." Champaran showed that peaceful civil disobedience could succeed. It taught the peasants courage and self-reliance, and it became the start of his struggle against British rule.

Poets and Pancakes (Asokamitran)

Q29 ★ (2023, 2025, 2026, SQP) In what way was the make-up department of Gemini Studios ahead of AIR and Doordarshan?

Ans: The make-up department had people from all over India: a Bengali head, then a Maharashtrian helped by a Dharwar Kannadiga, an Andhra, a Madras Indian Christian, an Anglo-Burmese and local Tamils. So there was real national integration long before AIR and Doordarshan began broadcasting programmes on it.

Q30 ★ (2020) What qualities of Kothamangalam Subbu did the writer admire?

Ans: Subbu was the No. 2 at Gemini Studios. He was loyal to his boss and very creative: he could suggest many ways of filming a scene, which made film-making look easy. He was a poet, a novelist and a fine actor. He was also kind and generous; his house was open to many relatives and friends.

Q31 (2025) What caused the lack of communication between the English visitor and the people at Gemini Studios?

Ans: The Englishman, a poet and editor (later known to be Stephen Spender), spoke in a strong English accent, and nobody at the studio could understand him. He also talked about poetry, which did not interest a Tamil film studio. So the audience was confused and the visit remained a mystery.

The Interview (Christopher Silvester / Umberto Eco)

Q32 ★ (2026) What does Umberto Eco call "interstices"?

Ans: Interstices are the empty spaces of time between tasks, for example while waiting for a lift to come up. Eco said he uses these small gaps to write, and so he was able to do so much work as a scholar, novelist and writer.

Q33 ★ (2024) What, according to Umberto Eco, do most journalists and publishers believe?

Ans: They believe that people like junk and easy reading, and do not like difficult reading experiences. Eco says the huge success of The Name of the Rose, a serious and difficult novel, proves them wrong.

Q34 (2020, idea) Why do some celebrities dislike interviews?

Ans: Some writers see interviews as an unwanted invasion of privacy. V. S. Naipaul felt people are wounded by interviews and lose a part of themselves. Lewis Carroll had a "just horror of the interviewer" and never gave one. Rudyard Kipling called interviewing immoral, a crime. Saul Bellow compared them to thumbprints on his windpipe.

Going Places (A. R. Barton)

Q35 ★ (2025) Sophie said she would open a boutique. Why did Jansie disagree?

Ans: Jansie said a boutique needs a lot of money. Both girls were from poor families, and they were to work in the biscuit factory after school. Sophie's plans of a boutique, then being a manager, an actress or a fashion designer showed she was a dreamer who ignored reality.

Q36 ★ (2019, 2020, 2026) Describe Sophie's imagined meeting with Danny Casey and how it ended.

Ans: Sophie told her brother Geoff that she met Danny Casey, the young Irish football star, in the arcade, and that he asked her out. Nobody believed her. She went to the canal and waited by the tree, but Danny never came. She felt sad and was worried about what to tell the family.

Q37 What was Geoff like, and why did Sophie admire him?

Ans: Geoff was Sophie's elder brother, an apprentice mechanic. He was quiet and silent, and often went to places she did not know. Sophie admired his secret, grown-up world and wished to be part of it. Geoff was the only one with whom she shared her fantasy about Danny Casey.

Part D: Flamingo poetry (Q38-Q52)

My Mother at Sixty-Six (Kamala Das)

Q38 ★ (2019, 2026) Why does the poet compare her mother's face to a "late winter's moon"?

Ans: It is a simile. A late winter's moon is pale, dim and about to set. Her mother's face looked pale, lifeless and tired with age, like that moon. It suggests the mother is near the end of her life, which awakens the poet's fear of losing her.

Q39 ★ (2019, 2023) What was the poet's "childhood fear"? Why did she smile and smile?

Ans: Her childhood fear was the fear of losing her mother (separation and death). At the airport she hid this pain. She only said "see you soon, Amma" and smiled and smiled, to hide her sadness and give her mother, and herself, hope and courage.

Q40 ★ (SQP, 2024) What contrast does the poet show between her mother and the scenes outside the car?

Ans: Inside, her mother dozed with her mouth open, her face ashen like a corpse, a sign of old age and decay. Outside, young trees were "sprinting" and merry children were "spilling out of their homes", signs of youth, energy and life. The contrast shows life moving on while her mother ages.

Keeping Quiet (Pablo Neruda)

Q41 ★ (2023, 2026) Why does the poet ask us to count to twelve and keep still?

Ans: Twelve stands for the hours on a clock or months of a year. The poet asks everyone to stop all activity and not speak in any language for a moment. This shared silence will make us look inward, understand ourselves and others, and stop the harm we do to people and nature.

Q42 ★ (2025) "What I want should not be confused with total inactivity." Explain.

Ans: The poet is not asking for death or laziness. He wants a short pause for introspection. "Life is what it is about." Stillness will help us see how our single-minded rush causes wars, sadness and destruction, and will help us live better.

Q43 What will happen during the "exotic moment" of silence?

Ans: There will be no rush and no engines. Fishermen will not harm whales; the salt gatherer will look at his hurt hands; those who prepare green wars, wars with gas and fire will stop and walk with their brothers. All will be together in a sudden strangeness, and peace and brotherhood will grow.

A Thing of Beauty (John Keats)

Q44 ★ (2025, SQP) "A thing of beauty is a joy for ever." Explain. / Why is beauty everlasting?

Ans: A beautiful thing gives us joy that never ends. Its loveliness keeps increasing and never passes into nothingness. Like a quiet bower, it gives us peaceful sleep full of sweet dreams, good health and quiet breathing. Its memory comforts us long after we see it.

Q45 ★ (2026) What does Keats refer to by "dark spirits"?

Ans: "Dark spirits" means our sad and gloomy mood. Despondence, the lack of noble people, gloomy days and unhealthy ways of searching fill us with sadness. Some shape of beauty removes this "pall" (cover of sadness) from our dark spirits and gives hope.

Q46 List the things of beauty mentioned in the poem.

Ans: The sun, the moon, old and young trees giving shade to sheep, daffodils in green surroundings, clear rills (streams) that make a cool cover for themselves, the mid-forest thicket with musk-rose blooms, the grand stories of the mighty dead, and all lovely tales. Together they are an endless fountain of immortal drink.

A Roadside Stand (Robert Frost)

Q47 ★ (2025) How does the poet show the mean and selfish nature of city dwellers?

Ans: City people rush past in their cars without looking at the stand. If one stops, it is only to ask the way or to complain that the stand spoils the landscape, or that the sign is badly painted (N and S turned wrong). They do not buy. Even those who help the poor are called "greedy good-doers" and "beneficent beasts of prey".

Q48 ★ (2026, SQP) Why is the stand's plea called "pathetic"? Why does the poet say it is not asking for "a dole of bread"?

Ans: The small stand begs travellers to stop and buy, but they ignore it, which makes its plea sad and helpless. The rural people do not want charity (a dole of bread). They want to earn some city money through fair trade, the cash that keeps cities prosperous.

Q49 What promise was made to the rural poor, and how does the poet feel about it?

Ans: The rural poor were told they would be moved to villages next to theatres and stores, where they would not have to think for themselves. These "good-doers" would take away their freedom and land. The poet feels deep pain at their wait and longs to end it all with one blow.

Aunt Jennifer's Tigers (Adrienne Rich)

Q50 ★ (2023, 2024) Why do Aunt Jennifer's tigers not fear the men beneath the tree? What do they represent?

Ans: The tigers are proud, bold and confident, "topaz denizens of a world of green". They pace in "sleek chivalric certainty". They represent the freedom and courage Aunt Jennifer lacks in her own life. Through them she expresses her hidden wish to be free and fearless.

Q51 ★ (2020) "The massive weight of Uncle's wedding band / Sits heavily upon Aunt Jennifer's hand." Explain.

Ans: The wedding ring stands for marriage and the husband's control. Its weight is not physical but emotional; the duties and fear of an unhappy, male-dominated marriage crush her. That is why her fingers flutter and she finds even the ivory needle hard to pull through the wool.

Q52 ★ (2020, SQP) "When Aunt is dead, her terrified hands will lie / Still ringed with ordeals she was mastered by." What is the irony?

Ans: Even in death Aunt Jennifer will not be free; her hands stay "ringed" by the trials of marriage. But the tigers she made will go on prancing, proud and unafraid. The irony is that she creates fearless tigers while she herself remains timid and suppressed all her life.

Part E: Vistas short answers 40-50 words (Q53-Q70)

The Third Level (Jack Finney)

Q53 ★ (2025, 2026) Why was Charley keen to buy old currency? Why did the booking clerk refuse his money?

Ans: At the third level (set in 1894), Charley tried to buy two tickets to Galesburg with modern money. The clerk said, "That ain't money, mister," because those notes were not in use in 1894, and thought Charley was trying to cheat him. So Charley took nearly $300 out of the bank and exchanged it for old-style currency (getting less than $200 in old bills), hoping to go back and buy the tickets.

Q54 ★ (2023) What did Charley find in his collection of first-day covers?

Ans: He found a letter from his friend Sam, the psychiatrist, dated July 18, 1894 and postmarked Galesburg. Sam wrote that he had found the third level, was now living in Galesburg in 1894, and asked Charley and Louisa to keep looking for it. It proved the third level was real.

Q55 ★ (SQP, 2020) What does the third level stand for? How did Sam explain it?

Ans: The third level is Charley's escape from the insecurity, fear, war and worry of modern life into the peaceful past of 1894. Sam called it a "waking-dream wish fulfilment"; he said Charley was unhappy, and that his stamp collecting was also a temporary refuge from reality.

The Tiger King (Kalki)

Q56 ★ (2019, 2023) How did the infant Tiger King surprise the astrologers? Why did the chief astrologer place his finger on his nose?

Ans: The astrologers predicted the baby would be killed by a tiger. The ten-day-old infant suddenly spoke: "O wise prophets! ... Let tigers beware!" The chief astrologer placed his finger on his nose in amazement, because he had never seen a ten-day-old baby speak.

Q57 ★ (2024) What confused the Dewan when the Maharaja spoke of his marriage?

Ans: The Maharaja, who had killed many tigers, asked the Dewan to find a bride. The Dewan was confused, wondering why the king suddenly wanted to marry. The king explained he wanted to marry a girl from a royal family whose state had a large tiger population, so he could kill tigers there and complete his count of 100.

Q58 ★ (2020, 2026, SQP) What gift did the Maharaja give his son on his third birthday? What is ironic about his death?

Ans: He gave a wooden toy tiger, carved crudely by a carpenter, bought for two annas and a quarter. A sliver of wood on its surface pricked his right hand. The hand became infected and he died during the operation. Irony: after killing 100 tigers to escape the prophecy, he was killed by a toy "tiger", so the prophecy came true.

Journey to the End of the Earth (Tishani Doshi)

Q59 ★ (2020, 2025) What was Gondwana? How was it different from today's world?

Ans: Gondwana was a huge supercontinent, centred around present-day Antarctica, about 650 million years ago. The climate was much warmer and it had many kinds of plants and animals. There were no humans. It broke up into separate countries around the time the dinosaurs died out.

Q60 ★ (2025) Why did Tishani Doshi feel she was walking into a giant ping-pong ball?

Ans: In Antarctica everything was white, with an endless blue sky. There were no human markers: no trees, buildings, billboards or other reference points. Without these, she lost all sense of perspective and time, as if she were inside a huge white ping-pong ball.

Q61 ★ (2024, 2026, SQP) Why is Antarctica a good place to study climate change? / How is the book a warning?

Ans: Antarctica has no human population, so it is a pristine place. Its ice cores hold half-million-year-old carbon records, so it shows clearly how the earth's climate has changed. Retreating glaciers and collapsing ice shelves warn us: will the West Antarctic ice sheet melt? Small changes, like a few degrees of warming, can have huge effects, so humans must act responsibly now.

The Enemy (Pearl S. Buck)

Q62 ★ (2026) Why was Hana relieved when the messenger said the General was in pain again?

Ans: Hana feared the messenger had come to arrest Sadao for hiding the American prisoner of war in their house. When she learnt he only wanted Sadao to treat the old General, she said "Is that all?" with relief. She was more tired from her fright than from her work.

Q63 ★ Why did the servants leave Sadao's house?

Ans: The servants did not like Sadao and Hana sheltering and treating an enemy American soldier. They thought it was wrong and unpatriotic and feared trouble for themselves. They left the house together, and Yumi, the children's nurse, had already refused to wash the white man.

Q64 ★ How did Sadao help the American soldier escape?

Ans: Sadao gave him his own boat, food, water and bottled water, a flashlight and clothes. He told him to row to a small island nearby, wait there for a Korean fishing boat, and flash the light at sunset if he needed more food. The soldier left safely at night.

On the Face of It (Susan Hill)

Q65 ★ (2020, 2023, SQP) How did Mr Lamb try to put Derry at ease? How has he kept himself open to everyone?

Ans: Mr Lamb talked to Derry in a friendly, calm way. He did not ask about his burnt face or show pity. He talked of bees, crab apples and weeds, and gave him a job in the garden. His gate is always open, and he has no curtains, so anyone can come in. This openness made Derry trust him.

Q66 ★ (2025) Why did Derry return to Mr Lamb's garden?

Ans: Mr Lamb had treated him as a normal person and made him feel accepted. He taught Derry that what matters is how a person looks at the world, not how he looks. For the first time Derry felt hopeful, so he defied his mother and returned, saying, "nobody'd stop me".

Q67 (2023) "You think, Poor boy." Explain with reference to Derry.

Ans: Derry's face was burnt with acid. He believes people pity him or feel disgust and are afraid of him. Others look at him and think "poor boy", but they do not see his feelings. This hurts him, so he hides from people and avoids them.

Memories of Childhood (Zitkala-Sa and Bama)

Q68 ★ (2024, 2026) How did Zitkala-Sa try to save her hair from being cut? Why was it her hardest trial?

Ans: Her friend Judewin warned her about the haircut. Zitkala-Sa crept upstairs and hid under a bed in a dark room. She was found, dragged out, tied to a chair, and her long hair was cut. Among her people, shingled hair was worn only by cowards and mourners, so she felt she had lost her spirit and dignity.

Q69 ★ (2019, 2020) What distracted Bama on her way back from school?

Ans: Bama took half an hour to an hour to cover a walk of about ten minutes, because she enjoyed the sights: the performing monkey, the snake charmer, the cyclist who had been cycling for days, the Maariyaata temple, the Pongal offerings, and stalls selling snacks and fruits. These made the walk home fun.

Q70 ★ (2023, 2026, SQP) What did Bama see on her way home, and what was Annan's advice?

Ans: She saw an elder of her street carrying a packet of vadai by its string, without touching it, for the landlord. Annan explained this was untouchability. He told her that people of their community get no respect, but if they study hard and progress, they can throw away these indignities. Bama studied hard and stood first.

Part F: Extract practice (Q71-Q72), the question style used in Q7-Q9

Q71 ★ Keeping Quiet extract (2026; Keeping Quiet extracts also 2023, 2025) "Now we will count to twelve / and we will all keep still. / For once on the face of the Earth / let's not speak in any language, / let's stop for one second, / and not move our arms so much." (i) "we will all keep still" reflects: (A) the poet's willingness to keep quiet (B) the significance of stillness (C) the poet's appeal for collective engagement (D) the purpose of counting to twelve.

Ans: (C), he asks all of us to be still together. (ii) What does the poet want us to stop? Ans: all our activity: speaking, moving and the rush of work, for a moment. (iii) A: The poet advocates keeping quiet. R: He believes silence can bring profound changes in our perspective and lifestyle. Ans: (B) Both true, and R explains A. (iv) The poet urges people to count to twelve so that everyone stops at the same moment and becomes still and silent. (v) Counting to twelve is a synchronized effort. (vi) How can this message promote peace today? Ans: If people and nations pause to reflect before acting, they will understand one another and avoid fights and wars.

Q72 ★ Lost Spring extract (2026; Lost Spring extracts also 2024) "And in dark hutments, next to lines of flames of flickering oil lamps, sit boys and girls with their fathers and mothers, welding pieces of coloured glass into circles of bangles. Their eyes are more adjusted to the dark than to the light outside. That is why they often end up losing their eyesight before they become adults." (i) What do "flickering oil lamps" suggest?

Ans: poor, dim light and unsafe, unhealthy working conditions (no electricity). (ii) The description highlights the pathetic situation of the workers. (iii) The irony in "Their eyes are more adjusted to the dark than to the light outside": they make bright, colourful bangles, yet they live and work in darkness and lose the light of their eyes. (iv) Children sitting with their parents in hutments indicates child labour: the whole family, even children, works at bangle-making. (v) The last line shows the health hazard of the work: children go blind before they grow up.

Part G: Long answers, 120-150 words (Q73-Q80)

How to write a long answer for 5 marks: 1 opening line that answers the question directly + 3-4 points from the story (names, events, a short quote) + 1 closing line with the message. Keep to about 150 words.

Q73 ★ (2020; bangle makers also asked 2023, 2026, SQP) The life of the bangle makers of Firozabad shows the grinding poverty and traditions that condemn thousands to a life of misery. Elaborate.

Ans: Firozabad is the centre of India's glass-bangle industry, and about 20,000 children work there illegally. Families like Mukesh's work in dark, dingy cells beside hot furnaces, without air or light, so many children lose their eyesight. They live in poverty in stinking lanes. They cannot escape because they are trapped in a vicious circle of the sahukars, middlemen, policemen, keepers of law, bureaucrats and politicians. Fear of the police, and having no leader, stop them from forming cooperatives. They also believe bangle-making is their "karam", their God-given lineage. Even Savita, a young girl, makes bangles that are a symbol of a married woman's happiness, yet the old woman who has made bangles all her life has not had a full meal. Only Mukesh dares to dream of becoming a motor mechanic. Education and courage are the only way to break this circle.

Q74 ★ (2026; The Rattrap 2019, 2025; Going Places 2019) The peddler's temptation for money and Sophie's unrealistic dreams were the root causes of their suffering. Analyse with reference to "The Rattrap" and "Going Places".

Ans: Both characters suffer because they chase something unreal. The peddler believed the world is a rattrap that tempts people with "bait". Yet he himself fell into it: he stole the old crofter's thirty kronor, then lost his way in the forest and wandered in fear. He also accepted the ironmaster's invitation by pretending to be Captain von Stahle. Only Edla's kindness freed him, and he returned the money. Sophie, a poor girl due to work in a biscuit factory, dreamed of owning a boutique, becoming an actress and meeting football star Danny Casey. She imagined a date with Danny and waited for him by the canal, but he never came. Her fantasy left her sad and alone, and her family did not believe her. So greed and daydreams trap us. Honest work, realistic goals and the kindness of others help us escape.

Q75 (2026) Analyse the portrayal of fear and freedom in "Deep Water" and "Aunt Jennifer's Tigers".

Ans: Both texts show how fear can imprison a person, but they end differently. In "Deep Water", William Douglas developed a terror of water after being thrown into the deep end of the YMCA pool as a boy. The fear spoiled his fishing, canoeing and swimming for years. He fought back: he hired an instructor and practised for months, and then swam alone in Lake Wentworth and Warm Lake. He won freedom from fear, learning that "All we have to fear is fear itself." Aunt Jennifer, in Rich's poem, is crushed by the weight of an unhappy marriage, shown by "Uncle's wedding band". Her hands are "terrified" and stay "ringed with ordeals" even in death. Her only freedom is in her art: the tigers she sews are proud, fearless and free. So Douglas finds real freedom through his own effort, while Aunt Jennifer finds it only in imagination.

Q76 (2025; both poems asked many years) "My Mother at Sixty-Six" and "Aunt Jennifer's Tigers" explore the themes of pain and conflict. Explain.

Ans: Both poems show women's pain, and a conflict between inner feelings and outward behaviour. In "My Mother at Sixty-Six", Kamala Das sees her ageing mother dozing in the car, her face ashen "like that of a corpse" and pale "as a late winter's moon". She feels her old childhood fear of losing her mother. But she hides this pain; she says only "see you soon, Amma" and smiles. The conflict is between her fear and her wish to stay hopeful. In "Aunt Jennifer's Tigers", the pain is caused by a male-dominated marriage. The heavy wedding band and her "terrified hands" show her suffering. Her inner wish for courage and freedom is shown through the bold tigers she embroiders, who "do not fear the men beneath the tree". Both poems show that people often hide their pain and find small ways, like a smile or art, to cope with it.

Q77 ★ (2023, 2024, 2025, SQP) Dr Sadao is torn between his duty as a doctor and his duty as a patriotic Japanese. Elaborate. / Is he both a true patriot and a compassionate human being?

Ans: Dr Sadao Hoki, a Japanese surgeon, finds a wounded American prisoner of war washed up on the beach outside his house during the war. As a patriot, he knows he should hand the enemy over to the police. But as a doctor, he cannot let a wounded man die. He chooses humanity. He and his wife Hana take the soldier in, and Sadao removes the bullet and saves his life, even though the servants leave the house in protest. Then he does his duty as a citizen too: he tells the General. The General promises to send his private assassins, but forgets. Sadao finally helps the soldier escape by giving him his boat, food and a flashlight, so that he is not a danger to his country either. So Sadao is both a loyal patriot and a compassionate human being. His duty as a doctor to save life wins over hatred.

Q78 ★ (2019, 2026, SQP) Derry and Mr Lamb both have physical impairments, but their attitudes to life are completely different. / People with physical impairments deserve acceptance, not sympathy. How does "On the Face of It" show this?

Ans: Derry, a fourteen-year-old boy, has a face burnt by acid. He feels ugly and unwanted. He hates people's pity and stares, so he hides from everyone and is bitter and full of self-pity. Mr Lamb has a tin leg, but he is cheerful and open. Children call him "Lamey-Lamb", but he does not mind. His garden gate is always open, and he loves bees, crab apples and even weeds. He tells Derry that what matters is how you see the world, not how the world sees you, and that a person can choose to live fully. Mr Lamb does not pity Derry; he treats him as a normal friend and gives him work. This acceptance gives Derry new confidence, and he defies his mother to return to the garden. The play shows that people with disabilities need acceptance and respect, not sympathy, and that a positive attitude is more important than appearance.

Q79 ★ (2020; Tiger King every year) How did the Tiger King meet his end? What is ironical about his death?

Ans: When the Maharaja of Pratibandapuram was born, the astrologers said he would be killed by a tiger. To escape this, he decided to kill 100 tigers. He banned tiger hunting by anyone else, spent huge sums, nearly lost his throne to a British officer, and even married a princess from a state with many tigers. The 100th tiger was actually brought from the People's Park in Madras. The king's bullet missed it, but his frightened hunters killed it so that the king would not know. On his son's third birthday, the king bought a wooden toy tiger, crudely made by a carpenter. A sliver from it pierced his right hand. It became infected, and he died during the operation. The irony is that the king who killed so many real tigers was killed by a toy tiger. The prophecy came true, showing that no one can escape fate, and the story satirises the pride and cruelty of rulers.

Q80 (2025; Memories of Childhood every year) "Memories of Childhood" gives the autobiographical episodes of Zitkala-Sa and Bama. Both come from marginalised communities. How do they reflect on their relationship with mainstream culture?

Ans: Both writers faced discrimination as children, though in different countries and cultures. Zitkala-Sa, a Native American girl, was taken to a missionary school. There she had to wear tight clothes and hard shoes, and her long hair was cut, even though among her people short hair was worn only by mourners and cowards. She hid under a bed but was dragged out and tied to a chair. She felt like "one of many little animals driven by a herder" and lost her spirit. Yet she resisted. Bama, a Dalit girl from Tamil Nadu, saw an elder of her street carrying vadai by a string for a landlord, without touching it. Her brother Annan explained untouchability and told her that education could remove such indignities. She studied hard and stood first. Both show the pain of being treated as inferior, and both chose courage and learning to fight back.

6c. Poetic devices that are asked in extracts

Device Meaning Example from the syllabus
Simile comparison using "like/as" "pale as a late winter's moon"; "ashen, like that of a corpse" (My Mother at Sixty-Six)
Metaphor direct comparison "A bower quiet for us" (A Thing of Beauty); "flower of cities" (A Roadside Stand)
Personification human qualities to things "young trees sprinting" (My Mother at Sixty-Six)
Alliteration same sound repeated "prancing, proud"; "sleek chivalric certainty" (Aunt Jennifer's Tigers)
Repetition words repeated for effect "smile and smile and smile" (My Mother at Sixty-Six)
Oxymoron opposite words together "greedy good-doers", "beneficent beasts of prey" (A Roadside Stand)
Transferred epithet adjective given to the wrong noun "terrified hands" (Aunt Jennifer's Tigers)
Rhyme scheme pattern of end sounds A Thing of Beauty and Aunt Jennifer's Tigers are in rhyming couplets (aabb)

6d. Reading section question types and tricks

Question type What to do
"Which evidence / line from para X ..." Copy the exact line or number from that paragraph
Analogy (A : B :: C : ?) Find how A and B are related (synonym? cause?), then find a word from the named paragraph with the same link
Tone / main idea MCQ Factual passages: "informative / objective". Choose the option that covers the whole passage
"Complete the sentence" Use words from the passage; keep it short and grammatical
Data / chart (case-based) Say "increased / decreased / highest / lowest" and quote the figures
2-mark "evaluate / assess" Give a judgement word (largely / partly / highly) + one reason from the passage
Fill with one word from brackets Read the sentence twice with each word; pick the one that matches the passage

7. Answer-writing tips and a 30-day timetable

7a. Tips to collect marks

  1. Format first. In Q3-Q6, the format mark is the easiest mark in the paper. Draw the box for a notice. Write "Yours truly" for a letter to the editor and "Yours sincerely" for a job application.
  2. Use every cue given in the writing question. One cue = one or two sentences.
  3. Never leave an MCQ blank. There is no negative marking. But write only one option; the marking scheme gives zero if you write two.
  4. For literature short answers (2 marks): write 3 short sentences. Sentence 1 answers the question directly; sentence 2 gives a detail from the chapter (a name or event); sentence 3 gives the reason or result.
  5. Name the characters and the chapter in long answers. Examiners look for "textual evidence".
  6. Simple English is fine. Short, correct sentences get the grammar mark. Avoid long sentences that you are not sure of.
  7. Choose the easier option in every question, and do not attempt both. If you attempt an extra question, only the better one is counted.
  8. Order of writing the paper: Section B (writing) first while you are fresh (about 40 min), then Section C (literature, about 70 min), then Section A (reading, about 50 min). Write the question numbers exactly as in the paper (e.g. 7(a)(iii)).
  9. Keep to the word limit roughly. A long answer of 120-150 words is about 12-15 lines.
  10. Leave 10 minutes to check spellings of names: Douglas, Edla, Champaran, Sadao, Zitkala-Sa, Derry.
