Who this is for: students who need to pass, meaning at least 33 out of 100 overall (theory 80 + internal assessment 20 added together). How to use it: Learn the two fixed formats (formal letter and analytical paragraph) first, then the grammar rules, then the starred (★) literature answers in Section 5. Write the answers in your own notebook; do not only read them.

3. The 33-mark plan (target about 40% of theory = 32+ marks)

Main idea: English is the easiest subject to pass, because 30 marks (reading + grammar + writing formats) need almost no memorising. Then add the 10 most repeated literature chapters.

3a. Study order (highest return first)

  1. Formal letter format (Q4): learn the 8-part layout in Section 6. Format = 1 sure mark; a simple, correct body gives 2-3 more.
  2. Analytical paragraph (Q5): learn the 4-part plan (introduction, points, comparison/highest-lowest, conclusion). The data or points are given in the question.
  3. Grammar (Q3): learn the 5 rule boxes in Section 6 and do the 24 practice items in Section 5. 6 out of 10 is easy.
  4. Reading (Q1, Q2): read the question first, then find the paragraph it names. Answer every MCQ.
  5. Literature, most repeated chapters: Bholi, The Necklace, Madam Rides the Bus (Valli), Nelson Mandela, The Thief's Story, A Letter to God + The Sermon at Benares (pair), Mijbil the Otter, Glimpses of India, A Tiger in the Zoo + How to Tell Wild Animals (pair), Amanda!.
  6. Bonus chapters: The Midnight Visitor, A Triumph of Surgery, The Book that Saved the Earth, The Ball Poem, Custard the Dragon, The Proposal.

3c. How internal assessment helps

  • IA is 20 marks. Most regular students score 14-18. If you score even 12 in IA, you need only 21 out of 80 in theory to reach 33 overall.
  • ALS (5 marks): speak clearly, finish your sentences, answer the teacher's questions about the listening text. Practise a 1-minute talk on a familiar topic (my school, my favourite festival).
  • Portfolio / notebook: complete and neat class work is easy marks. Submit everything on time.
  • Periodic tests: treat them as practice for the board pattern.

5. Must-do question bank (86 questions)

★ = asked many times, so do this first. Years in brackets are papers read where the same or a very similar question appeared. "SQP" = 2026-27 Sample Paper. Questions without a year are standard NCERT-based practice.

Part A: Reading skills (Q1-Q2 pattern)

How to attack a passage: (1) Read the questions first. (2) Each question names a paragraph; go to that paragraph only. (3) For MCQs, cut the options that are clearly wrong. (4) For 2-mark answers, write two separate points.

Q1 ★ (MCQ, SQP) The word 'mainstay' in "audio routines have become a mainstay in people's daily lives" suggests that audiobooks have ______ (overpowered people's lives / become a support in people's lives).

Ans: become a support in people's lives. "Mainstay" means a main support.

Q2 (Assertion-Reason, SQP) Assertion: The most preferred scenario for listening to audiobooks is when travelling. Reason: During travel, listeners struggle with short attention span problems.

Ans: (C) The assertion is true (63% listen on the go), but the reason is false (the passage does not say this). Method for every A-R: Is A true? Is R true? Does R explain A? Both true + explains = (A); both true, does not explain = (B); A true, R false = (C); A false, R true = (D).

Q3 (Fact or opinion, SQP) Classify: (i) BPA and lead are used in making mass-produced plastic toys. (iv) Wooden toys engage children's minds in deeper and more meaningful ways than plastic toys.

Ans: (i) is a Fact (it can be checked). (iv) is an Opinion (it is a judgement: "deeper", "more meaningful"). Tip: words like best, better, should, beautiful, meaningful usually mark an opinion.

Q4 (2 marks, 2026 pattern) Mention any two major challenges faced by the cosmetics industry (Paragraph 5).

Ans: Any two, copied from the paragraph: (1) intense competition; (2) complying with regulations; (3) ensuring product quality; (4) copied products / ethical sourcing. Tip: 2-mark "mention any two" = write two short points, numbered.

Part B: Grammar (Q3, 10 of 12)

Rule boxes are in Section 6c. For "Error / Correction" items, write only the wrong word and the right word in the format given.

Q5 ★ (SQP) Error correction: "Neither the software developers nor the project manager were aware of the compatibility issues."

Ans: Error: were → Correction: was. With "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject (the project manager = singular).

Q6 ★ (SQP) Error correction: "Each of the participants in the Science Fair have been given a set of guidelines."

Ans: Error: have → Correction: has. "Each" is singular.

Q7 ★ (2026) Error correction: "True tradition is not in celebrations, but in the culture we carries forward."

Ans: Error: carries → Correction: carry. "We" takes a plural verb.

Q8 (2026) Choose: "I don't know what it is. It feel like an incredibly lucky day for me." (A) don't-doesn't (B) is-have (C) feel-feels (D) lucky-luckily

Ans: (C) feel → feels. "It" is singular, so the verb takes -s in the present tense.

Q9 (2026) Error correction: "The farmer told his sons that he had hidden a treasure in the fields but he had forget the actual place."

Ans: Error: forget → Correction: forgotten. Past perfect = had + past participle (V3).

Q10 (2026) Error correction: "All tools and frequently touched surfaces must be disinfect after each client's treatment."

Ans: Error: disinfect → Correction: disinfected. Passive voice = be + V3.

Q11 ★ (SQP) "This hill station ______ (lose) its natural beauty since the last time I saw it."

Ans: has lost. "Since" + a result in the present = present perfect (has/have + V3).

Q12 (2026) "The group ______ (walk) for hours through forests and hills before it reached the barbed wire fence."

Ans: had walked (or had been walking). The action happened before another past action ("reached"), so use past perfect.

Q13 (SQP) "We are writing to confirm that your application ______ by our selection committee last week." (A) has been reviewed (B) was reviewed (C) has been reviewing (D) will be reviewed

Ans: (B) was reviewed. "Last week" = a finished past time, so simple past (passive). Do not use present perfect with "last week", "yesterday", "in 2020".

Q14 (2026) "A case ______ against the unidentified truck driver for causing damage." (A) was registered (B) is register (C) have been registered (D) may registered

Ans: (A) was registered. Simple past passive (was + V3). "A case" is singular, so not "have".

Q15 (2026) Replace the underlined word: "Since Sheenam study diligently for months, she will clear the exam." (A) studying (B) was studied (C) has been studying (D) have been studied

Ans: (C) has been studying. "For months" + still going on = present perfect continuous (has/have been + V-ing).

Q16 ★ (SQP) "All students ______ wear their identification badges at all times on campus." (A) might (B) could (C) must (D) may

Ans: (C) must. A rule or compulsion = must.

Q17 (SQP) "If you practise regularly, you ______ win the quiz competition next month." (might / must)

Ans: might. A possibility, not a certainty.

Q18 (2026) "Anita ______ (ought to / would / have to) likely complete the project on time as her team is collaborating effectively."

Ans: would. "Would likely" shows a probable result.

Q19 ★ (SQP) "Please note that ______ applicants who have submitted incomplete forms are required to resubmit." (A) these (B) those (C) this (D) that

Ans: (B) those. Plural noun (applicants) + "who..." pointing to a particular group = those.

Q20 (SQP) "Saira was disappointed when she saw there was ______ food left to serve the guests." (A) few (B) least (C) any (D) little

Ans: (D) little. Food is uncountable; "little" = hardly any (a negative sense, matching "disappointed").

Q21 (2026) "______ (Several/Both/All) people attended the convention because the entry was open to all at a very nominal fee."

Ans: Several. "Both" is only for two. "All people" is too general here; the sense is "many people". (Several is the answer in common keys; accept your teacher's key if different.)

Q22 (SQP) Error correction: "What among these books have you read before?" (A) What-Which (B) among-between (C) these-this (D) have-has

Ans: (A) What → Which. Use "which" when choosing from a limited group.

Q23 ★ (SQP) Report the doctor's statement: Doctor: "You should reduce your sugar intake and exercise regularly."

Ans: The doctor advised the patient to reduce his/her sugar intake and exercise regularly. (Advice → advised + to + verb.)

Q24 ★ (SQP) Isha: "I was absent yesterday because I had to visit the dentist for my regular check-up." Complete: Isha explained that ______.

Ans: Isha explained that she had been absent the previous day because she had had to visit the dentist for her regular check-up. (was → had been; yesterday → the previous day; I → she; my → her.)

Q25 ★ (SQP) Juhi asked her friend, "Are you prepared for the mathematics test next week?" Complete: Juhi asked her friend ______.

Ans: Juhi asked her friend if/whether she was prepared for the mathematics test the following week. (Yes/No question → if/whether; are → was; next week → the following week; no question mark.)

Q26 ★ (2026) Sahil said to Shekhar, "Which are your books?" (A) Sahil asked Shekhar which his books were. (B) ...which books are yours. (C) ...which has been his books. (D) ...which are his books.

Ans: (A). In reported questions the word order becomes a statement (subject + verb: "his books were") and the tense moves back (are → were).

Q27 (2026) Suhani to Sneha: "Which books are we going to purchase today from the Book Fest?" Complete: Suhani asked Sneha ______.

Ans: Suhani asked Sneha which books they were going to purchase that day from the Book Fest. (we → they; are → were; today → that day.)

Q28 (2026) Harry: "Good morning Bobby, how do you do?" Bobby: "Quite well, thank you; but these are hard times." Complete: ...Bobby thanked him and said that he was quite well, but remarked ______.

Ans: that those were hard times. (these → those; are → were.)

Part C: Writing (Q4 letter, Q5 analytical paragraph)

The formats are in Section 6a and 6b. Below are four full model answers (about 120 words in the body/paragraph). Learn Q29 as your master template; most letters can be changed from it by swapping the problem and the solutions.

Q29 ★ (Letter to the Editor; 2026, similar 2016, 2018, 2024, 2025) You are Sanjana/Sanjit of Avenue Apartments, XYZ Colony, Sakshamgarh. You read in the newspaper about adulteration in food and dairy products. Write a letter to the editor of a national daily expressing your views, highlighting the health risks and suggesting measures. (about 120 words)

Model answer:

Avenue Apartments
XYZ Colony
Sakshamgarh

6 March 20XX

The Editor
The Times of India
New Delhi

Subject: Rising food adulteration is a threat to public health

Sir/Madam,

Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper, I wish to draw the attention of the authorities to the growing problem of adulteration in food and dairy products.

Recent reports show that milk is mixed with water and detergent, spices with colour and sawdust, and sweets with harmful dyes. Such food causes stomach infections, food poisoning, kidney and liver damage, and even cancer in the long run. Children and old people suffer the most.

I suggest that the food safety department should check shops and dairies regularly and punish offenders strictly. Simple home tests for adulteration should be publicised, and consumers should buy only products with the FSSAI mark.

I hope the authorities take quick action.

Yours faithfully,
Sanjana
(Sanjana)

Marks check: Format (addresses, date, subject, salutation, close) = 1; problem + effects + measures = Content 2; clear paragraphs = Organisation 1; correct grammar = Accuracy 1.

Q30 ★ (Letter to an authority; 2026 similar; SQP, 2024, 2025 same type) You are Rita/Raghav Mishra, President RWA, Senatown. Write a letter to the Director, Municipal Board, requesting maintenance of the colony parks, installation of lighting poles and repair of walking tracks. (about 120 words)

Model answer:

Resident Welfare Association
Senatown

6 March 20XX

The Director
Municipal Board
Senatown

Subject: Request for maintenance of parks, street lights and walking tracks

Sir/Madam,

I am writing on behalf of the residents of Senatown to request urgent repairs in our colony parks.

Many residents, especially elderly people and children, use the parks for morning and evening walks. However, the grass is overgrown, the benches are broken and the walking tracks have cracks and potholes. Two people have already fallen and hurt themselves. Q31 ★ (Analytical paragraph on points; 2026) Analyse the given details of school activities (student councils, clubs; activities in the morning assembly; celebration of important days; competitions; sports and academic activities) and write an analytical paragraph about the impact of school activities on students. Focus on leadership, responsibility, communication skills, discipline, daily motivation and team building. (about 120 words)

Model answer (no title): School activities play a major role in shaping a student's personality. Firstly, student councils and clubs develop leadership and a sense of responsibility, as students plan events and take decisions. Secondly, activities in the morning assembly, such as speeches and news reading, build confidence and communication skills. Moreover, celebrating important days teaches students about culture and values, while competitions encourage healthy rivalry and hard work. Sports, on the other hand, build discipline, fitness and team spirit, because players learn to win and lose together. Academic activities like quizzes keep students motivated every day. However, these activities must be balanced with studies. In conclusion, school activities turn students into confident, disciplined and cooperative young people who are ready for life.

Q32 ★ (Analytical paragraph on a chart; 2023 type, 2024, 2025 type) The bar chart shows the hours per week spent by teenagers on: Social media 14, Studies outside school 10, Outdoor games 4, Reading books 2. Write an analytical paragraph in about 120 words. (Practice data, not from a paper.)

Model answer (no title): The bar chart shows how many hours a week teenagers spend on four activities. Social media takes the largest share at 14 hours, which is more than all the other leisure activities put together. Studies outside school come second with 10 hours, showing that students still give importance to self-study. However, outdoor games get only 4 hours a week, which is less than one-third of the time spent on social media. Reading books is the least popular activity at just 2 hours. Overall, the data suggests that screens are replacing physical activity and reading among teenagers. If this trend continues, it may affect their health and concentration. Therefore, parents and schools should encourage outdoor games and reading habits.

Analytical paragraph checklist: introduction (what the chart/points show) → highest → next → lowest (with exact numbers) → comparison words (whereas, however, twice as much) → conclusion/trend. Never give a title, and never just list the points without analysing them.

Part D: First Flight (prose and play)

Short answers are in about 40-50 words (3 marks). Long answers are in about 100-120 words (6 marks). For long answers, write 5-6 points in paragraphs, with an introduction line and a conclusion line.

A Letter to God

Q33 (3 marks, 2023) What did Lencho hope for and why?

Ans: Lencho hoped for rain, or at least a shower. His field of ripe corn, dotted with flowers, needed only a downpour to give a good harvest. When the rain came, he called the big drops "new coins". But the rain turned into a hailstorm that destroyed his whole crop.

Q34 (3 marks, SQP) How does the hailstorm work as more than just a weather event in the story?

Ans: The hailstorm is the turning point. It destroys Lencho's crop and leaves his family facing hunger, so it tests his faith. Because of it, Lencho writes to God, and the postmaster and his employees show kindness by collecting money. So the storm reveals both Lencho's strong faith and human goodness.

Q35 ★ (3 marks) Why did Lencho call the post office employees "a bunch of crooks"? What is ironic about it?

Ans: Lencho had asked God for 100 pesos but received only 70. He was sure God could not make a mistake, so he believed the post office employees had stolen the rest. The irony is that these were the very people who had collected the money out of kindness to save his faith.

Nelson Mandela: Long Walk to Freedom

Q36 ★ (3 marks, 2024; extract 2026) "We, who were outlaws not so long ago, have today been given the rare privilege to be host to the nations of the world on our own soil." What is the significance of this statement?

Ans: Under apartheid, South Africa's white government treated Black people as second-class citizens, and many countries had cut off relations with South Africa. Mandela and his comrades were called outlaws and jailed. Now, after the first democratic, non-racial election, they were hosting world leaders. It marked the victory of justice, peace and human dignity.

Q37 ★ (MCQ, 2026 extract) The tone of Mandela in the inaugural lines is: 1. unifying 2. determined 3. aggressive 4. pessimistic 5. hopeless. (A) 1, 2 and 4 (B) 2 and 4 (C) 1 and 2 (D) 4 and 5

Ans: (C) 1 and 2: unifying and determined.

Q38 ★ (3 marks) What are the "twin obligations" Mandela mentions? Why could a Black man not fulfil them under apartheid?

Ans: Every man has twin obligations: to his family (parents, wife and children) and to his people, community and country. In apartheid South Africa, a Black man who tried to serve his people was punished and isolated, and torn from his family. So he could not fulfil both duties.

Q39 ★ (6 marks, 2023) Mandela said, "If people can learn to hate, they can be taught to love." Discuss.

Ans:

  • Mandela believed that no one is born hating another person because of the colour of skin, background or religion. Hatred is learnt from society.
  • Since hate is taught, love can also be taught, because "love comes more naturally to the human heart than its opposite".
  • Even in the darkest times in prison, he saw "a glimmer of humanity" in a guard, which kept his hope alive.
  • He believed that the oppressor is also a prisoner of hatred; both must be freed.
  • This belief helped him forgive, unite Black and white South Africans and build a "rainbow nation".
  • Conclusion: Mandela's words show that goodness can be brought out in everyone through love, education and example.

Two Stories about Flying

Q40 ★ (3 marks) How did the young seagull's mother make him fly?

Ans: The young seagull was afraid to fly and was starving on the ledge. His mother flew towards him with a piece of fish in her beak and stopped just short of him. Maddened by hunger, he dived at the fish, fell outwards and downwards, then his wings spread out and he began to fly.

Q41 (3 marks, 2023) What risk did the pilot take in "The Black Aeroplane"? Why did he take it?

Ans: The pilot decided to fly his old Dakota straight into huge black storm clouds instead of going around them. He took the risk because he wanted to get home to England in time for his holiday and his English breakfast. Inside the storm his compass, radio and other instruments stopped working.

Q42 (3 marks) What was mysterious about the black aeroplane?

Ans: A strange black aeroplane without lights appeared in the storm, and its pilot guided the narrator safely out. After landing, the woman in the control centre said that no other aeroplane was flying in the sky that night. The black aeroplane had vanished, and the narrator never found out who had helped him.

From the Diary of Anne Frank

Q43 ★ (3 marks, 2026) Mention the details that Anne provides about her family in her diary.

Ans: Her father, Otto Frank, married her mother when he was 36 and her mother was 25. Her sister Margot was born in 1926 in Frankfurt, and Anne on 12 June 1929. Being Jewish, the family moved to Holland in 1933, where her father ran the Opekta Company. Her grandmother died in January 1942.

Q44 ★ (3 marks) Why did Anne call her diary "Kitty"? Why did she want to keep a diary?

Ans: Anne felt she had no true friend to share her deepest thoughts with, even though she had loving parents and many friends. She believed "paper has more patience than people". So she treated the diary as her friend and named it Kitty, writing to it as if to a real person.

Q45 (3 marks) How did Anne win over Mr Keesing?

Ans: Mr Keesing punished Anne for talking in class by giving her extra essays: "A Chatterbox" and "An Incorrigible Chatterbox". She argued cleverly that talking is a female trait inherited from her mother. When he gave a third topic, she wrote a funny poem, "Quack, Quack, Quack, said Mistress Chatterback". He laughed and let her talk after that.

Glimpses of India

Q46 ★ (3 marks, 2026) Bread plays a pivotal role in Goan culture. Support with evidence from the text.

Ans: In Goa, marriage gifts are meaningless without the sweet bread called bol, and a party loses its charm without bread. The lady of the house must make sandwiches for her daughter's engagement. Cakes and bolinhas are a must for Christmas and other festivals. So the baker (pader) is important to every village.

Q47 (3 marks, 2025) How does the description of the baker's lifestyle in the past evoke nostalgia?

Ans: The writer fondly remembers the baker's "jingling thud" of the bamboo staff that woke them up, the loaf-sellers in their knee-length frock called the kabai, and children running to meet him. Bakers were plump, with a jackfruit-like physique, and kept accounts in pencil on the walls. These warm memories show his longing for the old days.

Q48 ★ (3 marks, 2024) Coorg has been described as "a piece of heaven". Justify.

Ans: Coorg, between Mysore and Mangalore, has evergreen rainforests, coffee estates and spice plantations. It has mist-covered hills, the river Kaveri and wildlife such as elephants, birds and the Mahaseer fish. Its people, the Kodavus, are brave and hospitable. Activities like river rafting and trekking make it a heavenly place.

Q49 ★ (3 marks, 2023, SQP) What are the legends about the discovery of tea?

Ans: Chinese legend: an emperor always boiled his water. Once a few leaves from burning twigs fell into it, giving a tasty drink: tea. Indian legend: Bodhidharma, a Buddhist monk, cut off his eyelids because he felt sleepy while meditating. Ten tea plants grew from them; their leaves, put in hot water, banished sleep.

Mijbil the Otter

Q50 ★ (3 marks, 2026) What happened when Maxwell took Mijbil to the bathroom?

Ans: Mij followed Maxwell to the bathroom and was soon in the bath, going wild with joy in the water: plunging, rolling, shooting up and down underwater and making enough splash "for a hippo". Before long he even learned to turn on the tap himself, showing his love for water.

Q51 ★ (3 marks, 2016, 2024 type) How did Maxwell take Mijbil to England? What happened on the plane?

Ans: Animals were not allowed on the direct airline, so Maxwell booked another flight and put Mij in a box. Mij tore the box lining and got blood on himself. Maxwell opened the box, and Mij escaped and ran through the cabin, causing chaos. The kind air hostess finally let Maxwell keep Mij on his knee.

Q52 (3 marks, SQP) What guesses did people in London make about Mij? What do they show?

Ans: People guessed he was a baby seal, a squirrel, a walrus, a hippo, a beaver, a bear cub, a leopard and even a brontosaur. A labourer angrily asked, "What's that supposed to be?" These funny guesses entertain us and also show that people know very little about animals and like to put them in fixed boxes.

Madam Rides the Bus

Q53 ★ (3 marks, 2026) How did Valli plan her bus journey?

Ans: Valli listened carefully to neighbours who used the bus and asked a few questions. She learnt the fare was 30 paise each way and that the trip took 45 minutes. She saved 60 paise by resisting peppermints, toys and the merry-go-round, and went in the afternoon (1 pm) while her mother napped.

Q54 ★ (3 marks, 2025) Valli found joy in watching the bus pass her village. What does this reveal about her?

Ans: It shows that Valli was curious, observant and full of wonder about the world beyond her village. She enjoyed small, simple things and wanted to experience them herself. Her dream of riding the bus shows she was eager to learn and become independent.

Q55 ★ (6 marks, 2023; Valli also 2022, 2024, SQP) Valli learnt a lot from people and her surroundings. Mention the traits of her character that helped her learn.

Ans:

  • Curious: she watched the bus every day from her doorway and wanted to know where it went.
  • Careful planner: she quietly collected facts about the fare, timing and route from neighbours' conversations.
  • Determined and self-controlled: she saved 60 paise by giving up sweets, toys and rides.
  • Independent and self-respecting: she refused the conductor's help, paid her own fare and would not buy a cool drink with money she did not have to spare.
  • Sensitive: on the way back, the dead cow that had run in front of the bus made her sad; she learnt about death.
  • Conclusion: an 8-year-old girl learnt about the world, money and life itself because she observed, planned and acted on her own.

The Sermon at Benares

Q56 (3 marks, 2023) What did Siddhartha Gautama see by chance when he was about twenty-five years old?

Ans: While out hunting, the prince saw a sick man, then an aged man, then a funeral procession, and finally a monk begging for alms. These sights of suffering moved him deeply, so he left his palace to seek enlightenment, and later became the Buddha.

Q57 ★ (6 marks, 2024, 2025, 2026) How do Lencho ("A Letter to God") and Kisa Gotami ("The Sermon at Benares") respond to suffering and loss? How do they overcome it?

Ans:

  • Lencho lost his entire crop in a hailstorm. He did not lose hope; he had unshakable faith in God and wrote to Him for 100 pesos.
  • His faith was so strong that he did not doubt God even when he received only 70 pesos; he blamed the post office employees instead. His faith helped him face the loss.
  • Kisa Gotami lost her only son. In her grief she carried the dead body from house to house, asking for medicine to bring him back.
  • The Buddha asked her for a handful of mustard seed from a house where no one had lost a child, husband, parent or friend. She found no such house.
  • She realised that death is common to all and that she had been "selfish in her grief". This understanding brought her peace.
  • Comparison: Lencho overcomes loss through faith, Kisa Gotami through understanding and wisdom. Both show that hope and acceptance help people survive suffering.

The Proposal

Q58 (3 marks, 2024) "What a burden, Lord, to be the father of a grown-up daughter!" What does this show about Chubukov as a father?

Ans: It shows that Chubukov was eager to get his daughter Natalya married, and that he saw her as a responsibility. He was also practical and materialistic: he welcomed Lomov because Lomov was a wealthy neighbour. He even called himself happy and embraced Lomov at once.

Q59 (3 marks) What did Lomov and Natalya quarrel about?

Ans: First they quarrelled about who owned the Oxen Meadows; each claimed the land. Then they quarrelled about whose dog was better, Lomov's Guess or Natalya's Squeezer. These silly quarrels show that both were proud and quarrelsome, which is the play's comedy.

Part E: First Flight (poems)

Q60 ★ (3 marks) How did the "dust of snow" change the poet's mood? (Dust of Snow)

Ans: The poet was in a sad, regretful mood. A crow sitting on a hemlock tree shook down fine snow on him. This small natural moment cheered him up and "saved some part of a day" he had "rued" (regretted). The poem shows that small things in nature can change our mood.

Q61 ★ (3 marks, 2026) In "Fire and Ice", what do fire and ice stand for? How can they destroy the world?

Ans: Fire stands for desire (greed, lust, anger) and ice stands for hatred (coldness, indifference). Desire spreads fast like fire and leads to conflict and destruction. Hatred makes people cold and cruel, and is also "great" enough to end the world. So human emotions can destroy the world.

Q62 ★ (6 marks, 2025) Explore the contrasting images of the tiger in "A Tiger in the Zoo" and in "How to Tell Wild Animals".

Ans:

  • In "A Tiger in the Zoo" (Leslie Norris), the tiger is a sad, angry prisoner. He "stalks the length of his cage" in "quiet rage".
  • The poet imagines him free: hiding in long grass near a water hole, hunting deer, and snarling at villagers at the jungle's edge.
  • Locked in a concrete cell, he ignores visitors and stares at the stars with his brilliant eyes. The poem is serious and shows the cruelty of caging animals.
  • In "How to Tell Wild Animals" (Carolyn Wells), the tiger is described with humour: if a "noble wild beast greets you" while roaming in the jungle, it is the Bengal tiger, with "black stripes on a yellow ground".
  • Here the tiger is a dangerous hunter, and the tone is playful and light.
  • Conclusion: one poem makes us pity the tiger and value freedom; the other makes us laugh while showing its power.

Q63 ★ (3 marks, 2026 extract; 2023 crocodile) How can we tell a crocodile from a hyena, and what are the features of a chameleon? (How to Tell Wild Animals)

Ans: Hyenas come with merry smiles, but if an animal weeps, it is a crocodile (crocodile tears = false, deceptive tears). The chameleon is small, like a lizard, has no ears and no wings. If you see nothing on the tree, that is the chameleon, because it changes colour. The tone is playful and humorous.

Q64 ★ (3 marks, 2024, 2025) What is the theme of "The Ball Poem"?

Ans: A boy loses his ball in the harbour and is deeply upset. The poet does not comfort him or buy another ball, because the boy must learn the "epistemology of loss": how to bear loss and stand up again. Money is external and cannot buy back what is lost. The poem is about growing up and accepting loss.

Q65 ★ (3 marks, 2024, 2026 extract) Why does Amanda drift into fantasy? What does she imagine?

Ans: Amanda is constantly nagged by her mother about homework, posture, her room, shoes, chocolate and acne. To escape, she imagines being a mermaid in a calm emerald sea, an orphan roaming freely and patterning dust with bare feet, and Rapunzel in a peaceful tower. She wants freedom and silence ("the silence is golden, the freedom is sweet").

Q66 (3 marks, SQP extract) What is the message of "The Trees"?

Ans: The trees kept inside the house try to move out to the empty forest. Their roots, leaves and branches struggle to break free, and by morning the forest is full again. The poem shows that nature cannot be imprisoned, and it can also be read as the struggle of women for freedom and their right place.

Q67 (3 marks, SQP) How does the poet use the "cat" metaphor to describe the fog?

Ans: Carl Sandburg says the fog comes "on little cat feet": silently and softly, like a cat. It sits on its "silent haunches" looking over the harbour and city, and then moves on. Like a cat, the fog is quiet, mysterious and unpredictable.

Q68 ★ (3 marks, 2025, SQP) Describe Belinda's pets and the role of Custard. (The Tale of Custard the Dragon)

Ans: Belinda had a black kitten (Ink), a grey mouse (Blink), a yellow dog (Mustard) and a little pet dragon (Custard). Everyone teased Custard as a coward because he cried for a "nice safe cage". But when a pirate attacked, the others ran away and Custard gobbled up the pirate. The poem teaches that we should not judge by appearances.

Q69 (3 marks) Why does the young man say that Anne Gregory will not be loved for herself alone? (For Anne Gregory)

Ans: The young man says Anne's beautiful "yellow hair", like "great honey-coloured ramparts", will make young men love her only for her looks. Anne says she can dye her hair brown, black or carrot so that they love her for herself. The poet replies that only God can love her for herself alone, not her hair.

Part F: Footprints Without Feet

Bholi

Q70 ★ (3 marks, 2026; extract SQP) How did the teacher build up Bholi's confidence on her first day at school?

Ans: The teacher spoke to Bholi in a soft, soothing voice and did not laugh at her stammer. When Bholi managed to say her name, she praised her ("Well done") and patted her. She told her to put the fear out of her heart, and gave her a picture book, promising she would soon be able to read it.

Q71 ★ (3 marks, 2025) "In time you will be more learned than anyone else in the village. Then no one will ever be able to laugh at you." What effect did these words have on Bholi?

Ans: These words filled Bholi with hope and a new confidence for the first time in her life. She felt that she too could be respected. She began to study hard and slowly overcame her stammer and fear. This education later gave her the courage to refuse a greedy groom.

Q72 ★ (6 marks, 2024; Bholi also 2016, 2022, 2023) Trace the character arc of Bholi.

Ans:

  • Beginning: Sulekha (Bholi) fell from a cot as a baby and got pockmarks from smallpox at two. She stammered, was called a simpleton and was neglected by her family. She was shy and frightened.
  • Turning point: she was sent to the village school only because no one would marry her. The kind teacher encouraged her and promised she would become learned.
  • Growth: she studied for years and grew confident; her stammer faded.
  • Climax: her father fixed her marriage to Bishamber, an old lame man who then demanded Rs 5,000 dowry. In front of everyone, Bholi refused to marry "such a mean, greedy and contemptible coward".
  • End: she decided to serve her parents in their old age and teach in the same school.
  • Conclusion: education and kindness changed her from a timid "Bholi" into a bold, self-respecting woman.

The Necklace

Q73 ★ (3 marks, 2026) How did Matilda face "the horrible life of necessity"?

Ans: After buying a new necklace for 36,000 francs, the Loisels had to repay huge loans. Matilda dismissed the servant, moved to a cheap attic and did all the heavy housework herself: washing, cooking, carrying water, bargaining at shops. Her husband worked late copying papers. After ten years she looked old, rough and poor, but she faced it bravely.

Q74 (3 marks, 2025) Why did Mme Loisel leave the ball in a hurry when her husband put a modest wrap on her shoulders?

Ans: The wrap was a plain, everyday garment, and its poverty contrasted with the elegance of her ball dress. Matilda felt ashamed and did not want the other women, who were wearing rich furs, to notice it. So she hurried out of the ball.

Q75 ★ (3 marks, SQP; Necklace 2016, 2018, 2022, 2023) How does the writer use irony in "The Necklace"?

Ans: Matilda borrowed a necklace to look rich for one night, but lost it and spent ten years in real poverty to replace it. Her wish to look like a rich lady turned her into the common, rough woman she had always despised. The final irony: the lost necklace was fake, worth only 500 francs. The irony shows how vanity leads to downfall.

The Thief's Story

Q76 ★ (3 marks, 2026) Why did Hari Singh find it pleasant to work for Anil?

Ans: Anil was easy-going and trusting. He did not mind that Hari's first meal was so bad that he gave it to a stray dog; he simply taught him to cook. He also taught Hari to write his name and to add numbers. Hari could make a rupee a day on shopping, and Anil never complained.

Q77 ★ (3 marks, 2023, 2018 type) Why did Hari Singh return to Anil after stealing his money?

Ans: At the station Hari could not board the train to Lucknow. He realised he would lose much more than 600 rupees: Anil's trust and the chance to learn to read and write and become "a big man". He knew Anil would be hurt, not angry. So he returned and put the money back.

Q78 ★ (6 marks, 2025; 2024 diary type) Anil's acceptance and forgiveness of the thief show empathy and generosity. Elaborate.

Ans:

  • Anil gave Hari, a stranger, a home and work although he earned irregularly himself.
  • He did not scold Hari for his bad cooking; instead he taught him to cook, write his name and add numbers.
  • When Hari stole the 600 rupees and came back, Anil noticed that the notes were wet with rain, so he knew what had happened.
  • He did not say a word or punish him. Instead, he gave Hari a 50-rupee note and said, "Now we'll write sentences regularly."
  • This silent forgiveness made Hari feel trusted, and he smiled genuinely.
  • Conclusion: Anil's empathy changed a thief into an honest learner. Kindness can reform a person better than punishment.

The Midnight Visitor

Q79 ★ (3 marks, 2025, 2024) How did Ausable get rid of Max?

Ans: Max, pointing a pistol, wanted a secret report. Ausable calmly made up a story that a balcony below the window had let someone enter before, and that he had complained to the management. When there was a knock (a waiter bringing a drink Ausable had ordered), Ausable said it was the police. Max jumped out of the window onto the "balcony" which did not exist.

Q80 (6 marks, SQP) Ausable was fat and did not look like a secret agent, yet his presence of mind saved him. Discuss.

Ans:

  • Fowler expected a smart, thrilling agent, but Ausable was fat and ordinary-looking, with nothing mysterious about him.
  • When Max appeared with a pistol, Ausable did not panic.
  • He invented the story of a balcony and of complaining to the management, making it sound real with details.
  • He had ordered a drink earlier; when the waiter knocked, he told Max it was the police coming to check.
  • Frightened, Max climbed out of the window to hide on the balcony and fell, as there was no balcony.
  • Conclusion: the story shows that intelligence and quick thinking matter more than looks.

A Question of Trust

Q81 (3 marks) How was Horace Danby himself robbed? Why is the story called "A Question of Trust"?

Ans: Horace, a locksmith who robbed one safe a year, broke into Shotover Grange. A young lady pretending to be the lady of the house tricked him into opening the safe for her. He did it without gloves, so his fingerprints were on it, and he was jailed. She was a thief too. Horace trusted another thief and was cheated: a question of trust.

Footprints without Feet

Q82 (3 marks) How did Griffin become invisible? Why did he go to Iping?

Ans: Griffin, a brilliant but lawless scientist, swallowed certain rare drugs that made his body invisible. In London he faced cold, hunger and danger, so he stole clothes, bandages, dark glasses and a false nose from a theatrical store. He went to the quiet village of Iping and stayed at an inn to continue his experiments in peace.

The Making of a Scientist

Q83 ★ (3 marks, 2026 extract) What are the "ingredients" in the making of a scientist, according to the lesson?

Ans: Start with a first-rate mind, add curiosity, and mix in the will to win for the right reasons. Richard Ebright had all three. He did not want to win just for prizes; he wanted to do the best job he could.

Q84 ★ (6 marks, 2025) Trace the transformation of Richard Ebright from a curious boy to a respected scientist.

Ans:

  • As a child in Pennsylvania, Ebright collected butterflies, coins and rocks. His mother encouraged him with books, a telescope and microscope.
  • He lost at a county science fair; he realised that real experiments, not just displays, win.
  • His teacher Dr Urquhart guided him. He did projects on the viceroy butterfly copying the monarch, and on the 12 tiny gold spots on the monarch pupa.
  • He won prizes at county and international science fairs, and later found a hormone needed for butterfly growth.
  • His research led to a new theory on how cells read their DNA, published in a scientific journal.
  • He was also a debater, canoeist and photographer. Conclusion: curiosity, hard work and the right attitude made him a great scientist.

A Triumph of Surgery

Q85 ★ (6 marks, 2026; 2024 short) Compare Tricki's condition before and after his stay at the surgery. What does it show about proper care?

Ans:

  • Before: Mrs Pumphrey overfed Tricki with cream cakes, chocolates, malt, cod-liver oil and Horlicks. He got no exercise. He became fat "like a bloated sausage", lazy, and he vomited and refused food.
  • At the surgery: Mr Herriot gave him no food for two days, only water. Tricki played and ran with the other dogs in the hospital.
  • He soon joined in for food and became fit. (Mrs Pumphrey sent eggs, wine and brandy, which the staff enjoyed.)
  • After two weeks: he was lean, hard-muscled and leapt happily into his mistress's arms. She called it "a triumph of surgery", though no operation was done.
  • Lesson: too much love and food can harm; proper diet, exercise and discipline keep us healthy.

The Book that Saved the Earth

Q86 ★ (6 marks, 2026; 2024 short) How does Think-Tank's arrogance and ignorance lead to his defeat?

Ans:

  • Think-Tank, the ruler of Mars, thought himself the "most brilliant intelligence in the universe" and wanted everyone to call him "Your Intelligence" and "Mighty Think-Tank".
  • He wrongly believed the books the Martians found were sandwiches, and that people "ate" them.
  • He took a Mother Goose book of nursery rhymes to be a secret code for an Earth attack.
  • When Noodle read "Humpty Dumpty", Think-Tank saw that his own head was egg-shaped and panicked.
  • He called off the invasion and ordered the Martians to evacuate Mars, so the Earth was saved. Noodle, polite and clever, had handled him well throughout.
  • Conclusion: an arrogant, foolish leader was beaten by a book of rhymes; the theme is wit and humility over power.

6. Quick revision sheet

6a. Formal letter format (Q4, 5 marks; format = 1 mark)

# Part Example
1 Sender's address (top left) 12, Green View Colony / Guwahati
2 Date 6 March 20XX
3 Receiver's designation and address The Editor / The Hindu / Chennai
4 Subject (one line, starts "Subject:") Subject: Need for a community centre in our colony
5 Salutation Sir/Madam,
6 Body: 3 paragraphs (a) why you are writing; (b) the problem + its effects (2-3 points); (c) your suggestions / request + a closing line
7 Complimentary close Yours faithfully, (formal) / Yours sincerely, (if you know the person)

Use only the name and address given in the question. Do not write your real name or roll number.

Ready-made lines:

  • Letter to the Editor (opening): "Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper, I wish to draw the attention of the concerned authorities to..."
  • Letter to an authority (opening): "I am writing to bring to your notice..." / "I wish to request you to..."
  • Effects: "This causes serious health problems such as..." / "Children and the elderly suffer the most."
  • Suggestions: "I suggest that... should..." / "Strict action must be taken against..." / "Awareness campaigns should be organised."
  • Closing: "I hope the authorities will take prompt action." / "Thanking you in anticipation."

Common topics (practise one each): noise from loudspeakers, food adulteration, waste burning, stray animals, poor street lights, parks and walking tracks, water logging, vandalism of monuments, road accidents, rainwater harvesting, community centre, enquiry about a course.

6b. Analytical paragraph plan (Q5, 5 marks)

  1. Introduction (1 line): what the chart/table/points show ("The bar graph shows... from 2020 to 2025.").
  2. Main data: highest → next → lowest, with exact numbers or percentages. For points/SWOT: strengths, then weaknesses.
  3. Comparison: "twice as much as", "whereas", "on the other hand", "however", "in contrast".
  4. Trend / reason: "This suggests that...", "This could be because..."
  5. Conclusion (1 line): overall picture + a suggestion.

Linking words for the organisation mark: firstly, secondly, moreover, furthermore, however, on the other hand, while, overall, in conclusion. No title. About 120 words. Do not simply copy the bullet points.

6c. Grammar rule boxes (Q3)

1. Tenses

Tense Form Signal words
Simple present V1 / V1+s (he, she, it) every day, always, facts
Present continuous is/am/are + V-ing now, at present
Present perfect has/have + V3 since, already, just, yet, recently
Present perfect continuous has/have been + V-ing for two hours, since morning (still going on)
Simple past V2 yesterday, last week, in 2020, ago
Past perfect had + V3 the earlier of two past actions ("had left before I came")
Future will/shall + V1 tomorrow, next week
Passive be (is/was/has been/will be) + V3 "A case was registered."

2. Modals: must = rule/compulsion; should / ought to = advice, duty; can = ability; could = past ability, polite request; may = permission, possibility; might = weaker possibility; would = polite request, likely result; need not = not necessary. After a modal, always use V1 (must go, not must goes).

3. Determiners: a (consonant sound), an (vowel sound: an hour, an MLA), the (a particular thing). this/these (near), that/those (far or "the ones who"). few/a few + countable nouns (books); little/a little + uncountable (water, food, time). "Few/little" = hardly any (negative); "a few/a little" = some (positive). much (uncountable), many (countable). each/every + singular noun. both = two. either/neither = one of two.

4. Subject-verb agreement:

  • Singular subject → singular verb (He plays). Plural → plural (They play).
  • each, every, everyone, either, neither, one of the... → singular ("One of the boys is...").
  • Neither...nor / either...or / not only...but also → verb agrees with the nearer subject.
  • The number of → singular; a number of → plural.
  • Words between subject and verb do not change it: "The box of chocolates is on the table."
  • "With / along with / as well as" → verb agrees with the first subject.

5. Reported speech (said → told/asked; tense goes one step back):

Direct Reported
is/am/are → was/were; was → had been; have/has → had will → would; can → could; may → might; shall → should
now → then; today → that day; yesterday → the previous day; tomorrow → the next/following day this → that; these → those; here → there; ago → before
Statement: said that... Yes/no question: asked if/whether + subject + verb
Wh- question: asked + wh-word + subject + verb (no "?") Order/request/advice: ordered/requested/advised + to + V1

Error-correction method: (1) find the subject; (2) check its verb (singular/plural, tense); (3) check the article/determiner; (4) write only "Error | Correction".

7. Answer-writing tips and a 30-day timetable

7a. Tips to collect marks

  1. Attempt every question. There is no negative marking. A guessed MCQ can give 1 mark; a blank gives 0.
  2. Order of writing (suggested): Grammar → Letter → Analytical paragraph → Literature → Reading. Do what you know best first, but leave at least 40 minutes for the two reading passages.
  3. Letters: the format mark is free. Never skip the subject line, date or "Yours faithfully".
  4. Grammar "Error/Correction": write in the exact format asked (two columns). Do not rewrite the whole sentence unless asked.
  5. Short answers (3 marks): first line answers the question directly; then 2 supporting points from the story; stay near 40-50 words. Use the names of characters.
  6. Long answers (6 marks): write in 3 short paragraphs: introduction, 3-4 points, conclusion (a lesson or value). Even 3 correct points can earn 3-4 marks.
  7. Spelling of names: Lencho, Mandela, Valli, Kisa Gotami, Bholi, Matilda, Hari Singh, Anil, Ausable, Tricki, Ebright, Think-Tank. Correct names improve the accuracy mark.
  8. Reading: for "complete the sentence" items, take words from the paragraph. For "true/false", find the line and compare word by word.
  9. Handwriting and spacing: leave a line between answers; write question numbers clearly in the margin.
