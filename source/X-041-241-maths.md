## 3. The 33-mark plan (target 32/80 in theory)

**Main idea:** You do **not** need the whole book. Learn the **fixed-pattern** questions that come every year: formulas you just plug in, and 4-5 proofs that are always the same. Attempt **every** MCQ: there is **no negative marking**.

### 3a. Study order (easiest and highest return first)

1. **Probability (5)**: dice, cards, coins and balls. Only one formula: P = favourable / total. Learn the 36 dice outcomes.
2. **Statistics (6)**: mean, median, mode of grouped data. Same table method every year, plus Mode = 3 Median - 2 Mean.
3. **Real Numbers (6)**: HCF/LCM by prime factors, HCF × LCM = product, and the "prove √2 / √3 / √5 irrational" proof (same proof every time).
4. **Coordinate Geometry (6)**: just 3 formulas (distance, section, mid-point).
5. **Arithmetic Progressions (4) + one case study (4)**: a_n = a + (n - 1)d and S_n = n/2 [2a + (n - 1)d]. The AP case study comes almost every year.
6. **Trigonometry (6) + Heights and Distances (6)**: learn the table of values (0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°), 3 identities and the "two angles" tower method.
7. **Circles (7)**: two theorems (tangent ⟂ radius; tangents from an outside point are equal) and 3 standard proofs.
8. **Bonus if time:** Quadratic (discriminant, word problems), Pair of linear equations (elimination method), Areas of sectors, Surface areas and volumes, Triangles (BPT proof).

**Section A tip:** Of the 20 one-mark questions, about 12-14 come from the chapters above. Aim for **12 correct out of 20** = 12 marks from Section A alone.

### 3c. How internal assessment helps

- IA is **20 marks**, and most students who attend school and submit work score **15-18**. Because Class X passing is **33 out of 100 combined**, a student with 17/20 in IA needs only **16/80 in theory** to pass. **Do not lose IA marks.**
- **Portfolio (5):** keep the maths notebook complete and neat, with every class worksheet. Get it checked and signed.
- **Lab practical (5):** do the activities from the lab manual (e.g. verifying BPT by paper cutting, finding area of a circle by cutting sectors, probability by tossing coins). Write aim, material, procedure, observation and result in the lab file.
- **Pen-paper tests (10):** periodic tests in school. Treat them as practice for the board paper.
- **Still aim for 32/80 in theory.** It protects you if IA marks come lower than expected.

## 5. Must-do question bank (80 questions)

★ = asked many times, so do this first. Years in brackets are papers where the same or a very similar question appeared. "SQP" = 2026-27 Sample Paper. "NCERT" = textbook question (no board year claimed).

### Chapter 1: Real Numbers

**Q1 ★ (MCQ, SQP; same idea 2023)** The greatest number which divides both 134 and 188, leaving remainders 4 and 6 respectively, is: (A) 13 (B) 26 (C) 39 (D) 65
**Ans: (B) 26.** Subtract the remainders: 134 - 4 = 130 and 188 - 6 = 182. HCF(130, 182): 130 = 2 × 5 × 13, 182 = 2 × 7 × 13, so HCF = 2 × 13 = **26**.

**Q2 (MCQ, 2025)** If HCF(98, 28) = m and LCM(98, 28) = n, then the value of n - 7m is: (A) 0 (B) 28 (C) 98 (D) 198
**Ans: (C) 98.** 98 = 2 × 7², 28 = 2² × 7. HCF = 2 × 7 = 14 = m. LCM = 2² × 7² = 196 = n. n - 7m = 196 - 98 = **98**.

**Q3 (MCQ, 2019)** Two positive integers a and b can be written as a = x³y² and b = xy³, where x, y are prime numbers. Find LCM(a, b).
**Ans: x³y³.** LCM = product of the **highest** power of each prime. (HCF would be the lowest powers: xy².)

**Q4 ★ (3 marks, 2019; √5 in 2023)** Prove that √2 is an irrational number.
**Ans:**
- Suppose √2 is rational. Then √2 = a/b, where a and b are co-prime integers and b ≠ 0.
- Squaring: 2 = a²/b², so a² = 2b². So 2 divides a², hence 2 divides a. Let a = 2c.
- Then 4c² = 2b², so b² = 2c². So 2 divides b², hence 2 divides b.
- Now 2 divides both a and b. This **contradicts** that a and b are co-prime.
- So our assumption is wrong. **√2 is irrational.**
(For √3 or √5, replace 2 by 3 or 5 in every line.)

**Q5 ★ (3 marks, SQP; 2 + 3√5 in 2026; (2 + √3)/5 in 2019)** Given that √3 is irrational, prove that (2 + 3√3) is irrational.
**Ans:**
- Suppose 2 + 3√3 is rational. Then 2 + 3√3 = p/q, where p, q are integers, q ≠ 0.
- So 3√3 = p/q - 2 = (p - 2q)/q, and √3 = (p - 2q)/(3q).
- The right side is rational (integers divided by a non-zero integer), so √3 would be rational.
- But √3 is irrational. This is a **contradiction**. So **2 + 3√3 is irrational.**

**Q6 ★ (2 marks, SQP)** Three bells toll at intervals of 5, 6 and 8 minutes. They all toll together at 10:00 a.m. when the park opens. How many more times do they toll together till the park closes at 7:00 p.m.?
**Ans:**
- LCM(5, 6, 8): 5 = 5, 6 = 2 × 3, 8 = 2³. LCM = 2³ × 3 × 5 = 120 minutes = 2 hours.
- They toll together every 2 hours: 12 noon, 2 p.m., 4 p.m., 6 p.m. (8 p.m. is after closing).
- So they toll together **4 more times**.
*(Other OR in SQP: hall 9.75 m × 6.75 m × 5.25 m, longest ruler = HCF(975, 675, 525) cm = **75 cm**.)*

### Chapter 2: Polynomials

**Q7 ★ (MCQ, SQP)** If f(x) = px² + qx + r, p ≠ 0 and p + r = q, then one of the zeroes of f(x) is: (A) q/p (B) r/p (C) -r/p (D) -q/p
**Ans: (C) -r/p.** Put x = -1: f(-1) = p - q + r = 0 (because q = p + r). So -1 is a zero. Product of zeroes = r/p, so the other zero = (r/p) ÷ (-1) = **-r/p**.

**Q8 ★ (A-R, SQP)** A: If the zeroes of (2k - 1)x² + 4x - 3 are reciprocal of each other, then k = -1. R: If a = c, then the zeroes of ax² + bx + c (a ≠ 0) are reciprocal of each other.
**Ans: (A)** Both true and R explains A. Reciprocal zeroes means product = 1, so c/a = 1, i.e. a = c: 2k - 1 = -3, k = -1.

**Q9 (A-R, 2024)** A: If the graph of a polynomial touches the x-axis at only one point, then the polynomial cannot be a quadratic polynomial. R: A polynomial of degree n (n > 1) can have at most n zeroes.
**Ans: (D)** A is false (y = x² touches the x-axis only at (0, 0) and is quadratic). R is true.

**Q10 ★ (3 marks, 2019)** Find the value of k such that the polynomial x² - (k + 6)x + 2(2k - 1) has sum of its zeroes equal to half of their product.
**Ans:**
- a = 1, b = -(k + 6), c = 2(2k - 1)
- Sum = -b/a = k + 6; Product = c/a = 2(2k - 1)
- k + 6 = ½ × 2(2k - 1) = 2k - 1
- **k = 7**

*(SQP similar, 3 marks: if α, β are zeroes of 2x² - 8x + 5, find (α + 1/β)(β + 1/α). Expand: αβ + 1 + 1 + 1/(αβ). αβ = 5/2, so the value = 5/2 + 2 + 2/5 = **49/10**.)*

### Chapter 3: Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables

**Q11 (MCQ, 2025)** If x = 1 and y = 2 is a solution of the pair of linear equations 2x - 3y + a = 0 and 2x + 3y - b = 0, then: (A) a = 2b (B) 2a = b (C) a + 2b = 0 (D) 2a + b = 0
**Ans: (B) 2a = b.** Put x = 1, y = 2: 2 - 6 + a = 0 gives a = 4; 2 + 6 - b = 0 gives b = 8. So b = 2a.

**Q12 ★ (2 marks, 2019)** Find c if the system cx + 3y + (3 - c) = 0, 12x + cy - c = 0 has infinitely many solutions.
**Ans:**
- Condition: a1/a2 = b1/b2 = c1/c2, so c/12 = 3/c = (3 - c)/(-c)
- c/12 = 3/c gives c² = 36, c = ±6
- 3/c = (3 - c)/(-c) gives -3 = 3 - c, so c = 6
- **c = 6** (satisfies both)
*(Remember: unique solution a1/a2 ≠ b1/b2; no solution a1/a2 = b1/b2 ≠ c1/c2.)*

**Q13 (2 marks, 2024)** Solve 7x - 2y = 5 and 8x + 7y = 15 and verify your answer.
**Ans (elimination):**
- Multiply the first by 7: 49x - 14y = 35. Multiply the second by 2: 16x + 14y = 30.
- Add: 65x = 65, so **x = 1**. Then 7(1) - 2y = 5, so **y = 1**.
- Verify: 7 - 2 = 5 ✓ and 8 + 7 = 15 ✓.

**Q14 ★ (3 marks, 2019)** A father's age is three times the sum of the ages of his two children. After 5 years his age will be two times the sum of their ages. Find the present age of the father.
**Ans:**
- Let father = x years, sum of children's ages = y years. So x = 3y.
- After 5 years: father x + 5; children's sum y + 10 (each child gets 5 years older).
- x + 5 = 2(y + 10), so x - 2y = 15. Put x = 3y: y = 15.
- **Father = 45 years.**

**Q15 ★ (5 marks, 2025)** Vijay invested money in two schemes A and B at 8% and 9% per annum. He received ₹1,860 total annual interest. Had he interchanged the amounts, he would have received ₹20 more. How much did he invest in each scheme?
**Ans:**
- Let ₹x in A and ₹y in B. 8x/100 + 9y/100 = 1860, so 8x + 9y = 186000 ...(1)
- Interchanged: 9x + 8y = 188000 ...(2)
- Add: 17x + 17y = 374000, so x + y = 22000 ...(3)
- Subtract (1) from (2): x - y = 2000 ...(4)
- From (3) and (4): x = 12000, y = 10000.
- **₹12,000 in scheme A and ₹10,000 in scheme B.**
*(SQP case study similar: x + y = 1000 and 10x + 15y = 12750 give 450 small and 550 large bottles in June.)*

### Chapter 4: Quadratic Equations

**Q16 ★ (MCQ, SQP)** If (1 - p) is a root of x² + px + 1 - p = 0, then its roots are: (A) 0, 1 (B) -1, 1 (C) 0, -1 (D) -1, 2
**Ans: (C) 0, -1.** Put x = 1 - p: (1 - p)² + p(1 - p) + (1 - p) = (1 - p)(1 - p + p + 1) = 2(1 - p) = 0, so p = 1. The equation becomes x² + x = 0, i.e. x(x + 1) = 0, roots **0 and -1**.

**Q17 ★ (2 marks, 2017)** Find the value of k for which x² + k(2x + k - 1) + 2 = 0 has real and equal roots.
**Ans:**
- x² + 2kx + (k² - k + 2) = 0. So a = 1, b = 2k, c = k² - k + 2.
- Equal roots: D = b² - 4ac = 0
- 4k² - 4(k² - k + 2) = 0, so 4k - 8 = 0
- **k = 2**
*(2019 MCQ: for real roots of x² + 4x + k = 0, D ≥ 0 gives 16 - 4k ≥ 0, so **k ≤ 4**.)*

**Q18 (2 marks, 2026)** Verify that the roots of (p - q)x² + (q - r)x + (r - p) = 0 are equal when q + r = 2p.
**Ans:**
- D = (q - r)² - 4(p - q)(r - p)
- If q + r = 2p, then r = 2p - q, so q - r = 2q - 2p = -2(p - q) and r - p = p - q.
- D = 4(p - q)² - 4(p - q)(p - q) = 0. So the **roots are equal**.

**Q19 ★ (5 marks, 2025)** A train travels 480 km at a uniform speed. If the speed had been 8 km/h less, it would have taken 3 hours more. Find the speed of the train.
**Ans:**
- Let speed = x km/h. Time = 480/x. New time = 480/(x - 8).
- 480/(x - 8) - 480/x = 3
- 480 × 8 = 3x(x - 8), so 3840 = 3x² - 24x, so x² - 8x - 1280 = 0
- (x - 40)(x + 32) = 0, so x = 40 or -32 (speed cannot be negative)
- **Speed = 40 km/h**

**Q20 ★ (5 marks, SQP)** A rectangular park is 50 m × 40 m. A rectangular pool is built in the middle with a grass strip of uniform width around it. The area of the grass strip is 1184 m². Find the dimensions of the pool.
**Ans:**
- Let the strip width = x m. Pool = (50 - 2x) by (40 - 2x).
- Pool area = 2000 - 1184 = 816 m²
- (50 - 2x)(40 - 2x) = 816, so 4x² - 180x + 2000 = 816, so x² - 45x + 296 = 0
- (x - 8)(x - 37) = 0. x = 37 is impossible (50 - 74 < 0), so x = 8.
- **Pool = 34 m × 24 m** (check: 34 × 24 = 816 ✓)
*(Also asked: 2019 taps fill a tank in 15/8 h, one takes 2 h less → **3 h and 5 h**; 2026 tour ₹5,400, extend 5 days, cut ₹180/day → x² + 5x - 150 = 0 → **10 days, ₹540/day**; 2025 right triangle perimeter 60 cm, hypotenuse 25 cm → sides **15 cm and 20 cm**.)*

### Chapter 5: Arithmetic Progressions

**Q21 (MCQ, SQP)** The middle term of the AP 10, 7, 4, ..., -62 is: (A) -26 (B) -29 (C) -32 (D) -35
**Ans: (A) -26.** a = 10, d = -3. -62 = 10 + (n - 1)(-3), so n - 1 = 24, n = 25. Middle term = 13th = 10 + 12(-3) = **-26**.

**Q22 ★ (MCQ, 2025; S_n given in 2019 too)** If the sum of the first m terms of an AP is 2m² + 3m, then its second term is: (A) 10 (B) 9 (C) 12 (D) 4
**Ans: (B) 9.** S1 = 2 + 3 = 5 = a1. S2 = 8 + 6 = 14. a2 = S2 - S1 = **9**.
*(2019: if S_n = 3n² - 4n, then a_n = S_n - S_(n-1) = **6n - 7**.)*

**Q23 ★ (2 marks, 2019; same type 2017)** How many two-digit numbers are divisible by 3?
**Ans:**
- AP: 12, 15, 18, ..., 99. a = 12, d = 3, a_n = 99
- 99 = 12 + (n - 1) × 3, so (n - 1) = 29, **n = 30**
*(2019 Set III: two-digit numbers divisible by 7: 14, ..., 98 gives **13**. 2017: integers between 200 and 500 divisible by 8: 208, ..., 496 gives **37**.)*

**Q24 ★ (2 marks, 2019)** Which term of the AP 3, 15, 27, 39, ... will be 120 more than its 21st term?
**Ans:**
- a = 3, d = 12. a21 = 3 + 20 × 12 = 243
- Required term = 243 + 120 = 363 = 3 + (n - 1) × 12, so n - 1 = 30
- **31st term**

**Q25 (3 marks, SQP)** Find the sum of integers between 1 and 400 that are multiples of 4 as well as of 5.
**Ans:**
- Multiples of both 4 and 5 = multiples of 20: 20, 40, ..., 380 (400 is not "between")
- 380 = 20 + (n - 1) × 20, so n = 19
- S19 = 19/2 × (20 + 380) = 19 × 200 = **3800**

**Q26 ★ (Case study 4 marks, 2025)** A charity run: the first round is 300 m and each next round is 50 m longer. There are 10 rounds.
(i) Write the 4th, 5th and 6th terms. (ii) Find the distance of the 8th round. (iii)(a) Total distance after 10 rounds. OR (iii)(b) Total distance of the first 6 rounds.
**Ans:**
- (i) a = 300, d = 50: **450 m, 500 m, 550 m**
- (ii) a8 = 300 + 7 × 50 = **650 m**
- (iii)(a) S10 = 10/2 [2 × 300 + 9 × 50] = 5 × 1050 = **5250 m**
- (iii)(b) S6 = 6/2 [600 + 5 × 50] = 3 × 850 = **2550 m**
*(2026 "Kolam" case: 4, 8, 12, ... dots; a = 4, d = 4, a_n = 4n; S_n = 2n(n + 1) = 220 gives **n = 10** squares; 2n(n + 1) = 100 has no whole-number answer, so **100 dots is not possible**.)*

### Chapter 6: Triangles

**Q27 (MCQ, SQP)** The perimeters of two similar triangles are 56 cm and 70 cm. If one side of the first triangle is 14 cm, the corresponding side of the second is: (A) 5 (B) 7.5 (C) 10 (D) 17.5
**Ans: (D) 17.5.** Ratio of perimeters = ratio of sides: 14/x = 56/70, so x = 14 × 70/56 = **17.5 cm**.

**Q28 (MCQ, 2025)** In triangles ABC and DEF, ∠B = ∠E, ∠F = ∠C and AB = 3DE. Then the two triangles are: (A) congruent but not similar (B) congruent as well as similar (C) neither congruent nor similar (D) similar but not congruent
**Ans: (D).** Two equal angles give similarity (AA). Sides are in ratio 3 : 1, so they are not congruent.
*(2025 Section B similar: if ΔABC ~ ΔPQR, AB = 6, BC = 4, AC = 8 and PR = 6 cm, the ratio is 6/8 = 3/4, so PQ = 4.5, QR = 3 and **PQ + QR = 7.5 cm**.)*

**Q29 ★ (5 marks, SQP; BPT used in 2023 and 2026 MCQs/short answers)** Prove that a line drawn parallel to one side of a triangle, to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, divides the other two sides in the same ratio (Basic Proportionality Theorem).
**Ans:**
- **Given:** In ΔABC, DE ∥ BC, D on AB and E on AC. **To prove:** AD/DB = AE/EC.
- **Construction:** Join BE and CD. Draw EN ⟂ AB and DM ⟂ AC.
- ar(ADE) = ½ × AD × EN, ar(BDE) = ½ × DB × EN. So ar(ADE)/ar(BDE) = AD/DB ...(1)
- ar(ADE) = ½ × AE × DM, ar(DEC) = ½ × EC × DM. So ar(ADE)/ar(DEC) = AE/EC ...(2)
- ΔBDE and ΔDEC are on the same base DE and between the same parallels DE and BC, so ar(BDE) = ar(DEC) ...(3)
- From (1), (2), (3): **AD/DB = AE/EC.** Hence proved.
- **Draw the figure** (triangle with DE parallel to BC, the two joins and two perpendiculars). The figure alone carries marks.

**Q56 ★ (3 marks, SQP)** Prove that the parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus.

- So all four sides are equal: **ABCD is a rhombus.**

**Q75 (MCQ, SQP)** T-shirts numbered 4 to 99 are in a box. One is taken at random. P(number is a perfect square or a perfect cube) = ? (A) 1/12 (B) 1/32 (C) 11/96 (D) 5/48
**Ans: (D) 5/48.** Total = 96. Squares: 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81 (8). Cubes: 8, 27, 64 (3). 64 is counted twice, so favourable = 8 + 3 - 1 = 10. P = 10/96 = **5/48**.

**Q76 (MCQ, 2024; 2023 similar)** If the probability of a player winning a game is 0.79, the probability of his losing is: **Ans: 0.21** (1 - 0.79).
*(2023: a girl's probability of winning a lottery prize is 0.08; 6000 tickets were sold, so she bought 0.08 × 6000 = **480** tickets. 2023: 5 of 20 people can't swim, P(can swim) = 15/20 = **3/4**.)*

**Q77 (A-R, 2026)** A: If the probability of an event is 0.2p, p > 0, then p cannot be more than 5. R: P(not E) = 1 - P(E) for an event E.
**Ans: (B)** Both true, but R does not explain A. (A is true because a probability is at most 1: 0.2p ≤ 1 gives p ≤ 5.)

**Q78 ★ (2 marks, SQP)** Two dice are thrown together. Find the probability that the product of the numbers is a prime number.
**Ans:**
- A product is prime only when one die shows 1 and the other a prime: (1,2), (2,1), (1,3), (3,1), (1,5), (5,1) = 6 outcomes
- P = 6/36 = **1/6**

**Q79 ★ (3 marks, 2025; 2017 similar)** Two dice are thrown at the same time. Find the probability that the difference of the numbers on the two dice is 2.
**Ans:**
- Favourable: (1,3), (3,1), (2,4), (4,2), (3,5), (5,3), (4,6), (6,4) = 8
- P = 8/36 = **2/9**
*(2017: sum less than 7: 15 outcomes, P = **5/12**; product less than 16: 25 outcomes, P = **25/36**; doublet of odd numbers (1,1), (3,3), (5,5): P = 3/36 = **1/12**. 2024 MCQ: sum 2, 3 or 5: 1 + 2 + 4 = 7 outcomes, P = **7/36**.)*

**Q80 ★ (2 marks, 2019)** A game consists of tossing a coin 3 times and noting the outcome each time. Getting the same result in all tosses is a success. Find the probability of losing the game.
**Ans:**
- Outcomes: HHH, HHT, HTH, HTT, THH, THT, TTH, TTT = 8
- Success: HHH, TTT = 2. P(win) = 2/8 = 1/4
- **P(lose) = 1 - 1/4 = 3/4**
*(2019: a die is thrown once; P(prime) = 3/6 = **1/2**; P(number between 2 and 6, i.e. 3, 4, 5) = **1/2**. 2023: bag with 4 red, 3 blue, 2 yellow balls; P(yellow) = **2/9**, P(red) = **4/9**.)*

## 6. Quick revision sheet

### 6a. Formulas (learn these and you can do most of the paper)

| Chapter | Formula |
|---|---|
| Real Numbers | HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers (for 2 numbers only). HCF = lowest powers of common primes; LCM = highest powers of all primes |
| Polynomials | For ax² + bx + c: sum of zeroes α + β = -b/a; product αβ = c/a. Quadratic with given zeroes: x² - (sum)x + (product) |
| Linear equations | Unique: a1/a2 ≠ b1/b2. No solution (parallel): a1/a2 = b1/b2 ≠ c1/c2. Infinite (coincident): a1/a2 = b1/b2 = c1/c2 |
| Quadratic | D = b² - 4ac. D > 0: two distinct real roots; D = 0: equal roots; D < 0: no real roots. Roots x = [-b ± √D]/(2a) |
| AP | a_n = a + (n - 1)d. S_n = n/2 [2a + (n - 1)d] = n/2 (a + l). a_n = S_n - S_(n-1) |
| Coordinates | Distance = √[(x2 - x1)² + (y2 - y1)²]. Mid-point = ((x1 + x2)/2, (y1 + y2)/2). Section (m : n) = ((mx2 + nx1)/(m + n), (my2 + ny1)/(m + n)). On x-axis y = 0; on y-axis x = 0 |
| Triangles | BPT: if DE ∥ BC then AD/DB = AE/EC. Similar triangles: sides in same ratio, perimeters in same ratio |
| Trigonometry | sin = P/H, cos = B/H, tan = P/B; cosec = 1/sin, sec = 1/cos, cot = 1/tan; tan = sin/cos |
| Identities | sin²A + cos²A = 1; 1 + tan²A = sec²A; 1 + cot²A = cosec²A |
| Circles | Tangent ⟂ radius at point of contact. Tangents from an outside point are equal. Tangent length = √(d² - r²) |
| Areas | Circumference 2πr; area πr²; arc = (θ/360) × 2πr; sector = (θ/360) × πr² = ½ × arc × r; segment = sector - triangle (triangle = ½ r² sin θ) |
| Volumes | Cube a³; cuboid lbh; cylinder πr²h; cone (1/3)πr²h; sphere (4/3)πr³; hemisphere (2/3)πr³ |
| Surface areas | Cylinder CSA 2πrh; cone CSA πrl, l = √(r² + h²); sphere 4πr²; hemisphere CSA 2πr², TSA 3πr²; cuboid 2(lb + bh + hl); cube 6a² |
| Statistics | Mean = Σfx/Σf. Median = l + [(N/2 - cf)/f] × h. Mode = l + [(f1 - f0)/(2f1 - f0 - f2)] × h. **3 Median = Mode + 2 Mean** |
| Probability | P(E) = favourable/total; 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1; P(not E) = 1 - P(E). Sure event P = 1; impossible event P = 0 |

### 6c. Key definitions (1-mark favourites)

- **Rational number:** can be written as p/q, p and q integers, q ≠ 0. **Irrational:** cannot (√2, √3, π). Rational + irrational = irrational; non-zero rational × irrational = irrational.
- **Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic:** every composite number can be written as a product of primes in exactly one way (apart from order).
- **Zero of a polynomial:** value of x that makes p(x) = 0. On a graph, the number of zeroes = number of times the graph **meets the x-axis**.
- **Consistent pair:** has at least one solution. **Inconsistent:** no solution.
- **Tangent:** a line that touches a circle at exactly one point. **Secant:** cuts it at two points.
- **Angle of elevation:** angle above the horizontal when looking up. **Angle of depression:** angle below the horizontal when looking down. (Angle of elevation from A to B = angle of depression from B to A.)
- **Median:** the middle-most observation. **Mode:** the most frequent value. **Modal class:** the class with the highest frequency.
- A 52-card pack: 4 suits (hearts ♥, diamonds ♦ red; spades ♠, clubs ♣ black), 13 each; **12 face cards** (J, Q, K of each suit); 26 red, 26 black; 4 aces.

### 6e. Diagrams to learn (draw neatly in pencil; the figure itself earns marks)

1. **BPT figure:** ΔABC, line DE ∥ BC with D on AB and E on AC; join BE and CD; drop EN ⟂ AB and DM ⟂ AC.
2. **Two tangents from an external point:** circle with centre O, point P outside, tangents PA and PB, radii OA and OB with right-angle marks at A and B, and the line OP.
3. **Quadrilateral / parallelogram around a circle:** four sides touching the circle at P, Q, R, S; mark the equal tangent pieces with the same letter.
4. **Heights and distances (two angles):** vertical tower/lighthouse, horizontal ground line, two observation points on the same side, angles marked at the ground (elevation) or at the top with a horizontal line drawn (depression). Label h, x and the given distance.
5. **Combined solid:** cylinder with cone on top (tent), or hemisphere with cone on top (toy). Mark r, h and slant height l.
6. **Sector and segment:** circle, two radii making angle θ, chord joining their ends; shade the part asked.

## 7. Answer-writing tips (to collect step marks) and a 30-day timetable

### 7a. Tips
1. **Write the formula first.** In a 3-mark sum, the formula alone often gets ½-1 mark, substitution another 1 mark. Never write only the final number.
2. **Proofs:** always write **Given, To prove, Construction (if any), Proof** with a reason in brackets after every line ("radius ⟂ tangent", "tangents from an external point are equal", "AA similarity").
3. **Draw the figure** in every geometry, circles and heights-and-distances question. A correct, labelled figure is usually worth ½-1 mark.
4. **Word problems (linear / quadratic):** start with "Let ... = x". Writing the correct equation is half the marks even if you cannot solve it.
5. **Reject impossible answers in words:** "speed cannot be negative, so x = 40". This line carries marks.
6. **Statistics:** make the full table (class, x, f, fx or cf). Write the formula with l, f1, f0, f2, h listed separately before substituting.
7. **Probability:** write the total outcomes and **list** the favourable outcomes. Give the answer as a fraction in lowest terms.
8. **MCQ and A-R:** never leave one blank; there is no negative marking. In A-R, first decide "Is A true? Is R true?", then "Does R explain A?".
9. **Case studies (Section E):** parts (i) and (ii) are 1 mark each and usually very easy. Always attempt them, even if you skip (iii).
10. **Choose the easier OR option.** Usually pick the numerical over the proof, unless it is one of the standard proofs you have learnt (√2 irrational, BPT, tangent proofs).
11. **Order of attempting:** Section A (25 min) → case-study (i), (ii) parts → questions you know from this pack → the rest. Keep 10 minutes to check signs and units.
12. **Units:** cm, cm², cm³, m/min. Write them in the final answer.
