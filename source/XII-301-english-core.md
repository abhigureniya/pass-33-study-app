# CBSE Class XII English Core (301): Pass Pack 2026-27

**Who this is for:** students who need to **pass**, meaning at least 33% in the theory paper and 33% in internal assessment.
**How to use it:** First learn the **writing formats** (Section 6a). They give the easiest marks in the paper. Then learn the starred (★) answers in Section 5. Practise by **writing** the answers, not only reading them.

**How writing is marked (2026-27 marking scheme):**

| Task | Marks split |
|---|---|
| Notice (4) | Format 1, Content 2, Spelling and grammar 1 |

## 3. The 33-mark plan (target about 40-45% = 32-36 marks in theory)

**Main idea:** English has **no "deleted" safe chapter** and no numericals, but it has many **easy marks**: format marks in writing, MCQs in the passages and extracts, and short answers that come again and again. Attempt **every** question. There is no negative marking, and a half-correct answer in simple English still earns marks.

### 3a. Study order (easiest and highest return first)

1. **Writing formats (18 marks available):** learn the 6 formats in Section 6a by heart. The format alone is 4 marks (1 in each question). Then learn a few ready-made "content" lines.
2. **Reading (22 marks available):** no learning, only practice. Most answers are **in the passage**. Learn the tricks in Section 7a.
3. **Extract questions Q7-Q9 (16 marks):** most are MCQs or "complete the sentence". If you know the **story/poem summary** (Section 6c), you can answer them.
4. **The 5 poems:** short, and asked every year (extract or short answer). Learn the poet, the theme and 2-3 key lines of each.
5. **Flamingo prose short answers (Q10):** learn the ★ answers in Section 5.
6. **Vistas (Q8, Q11, Q13):** learn the ★ answers. The Enemy, On the Face of It and The Tiger King come almost every year.
7. **Long answers (Q12, Q13):** learn 4 ready long answers (Section 5, Part G). Even a half-correct long answer gets 2-3 marks.

### 3b. Target-marks table (theory)

| Part | Max | Target for a weak student | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 + Q2 Reading | 22 | 10 | All MCQs + "find the word/line" questions; copy key lines from the passage |
| Q3 Notice | 4 | 3 | Perfect format (1) + basic details (1.5) + simple grammar (0.5) |
| Q4 Invitation / Reply | 4 | 3 | Same as notice |
| Q5 Letter / Job application | 5 | 2.5 | Format (1) + 3 paragraphs using the cues |
| Q6 Article / Report | 5 | 2.5 | Heading + by-line (1) + 3 paragraphs using the cues |
| Q7-Q9 Extracts | 16 | 7 | MCQs, fill-in-the-blanks, true/false |
| Q10-Q11 Short answers | 14 | 5 | 7 short answers, half marks each at least |
| Q12-Q13 Long answers | 10 | 3 | Ready answers from Part G |
| **Total** | **80** | **36 (45%)** | Pass needs 27, so this gives a 9-mark buffer |

### 3c. How internal assessment saves you

IA is **20 marks**, and you need **7** to pass it. Most students get high marks here, but only if they:
- **Submit the project on time** (5 marks for the written report/script, up to about 1000 words, and 5 marks for the viva). Suggested projects from the curriculum: an interview-based research report, a report on a podcast or TV documentary, an audio/video script, or a one-act play.
- **Speak in the speaking test**, even simply. Marks are given for interaction, fluency, pronunciation, and vocabulary/grammar. Saying something is always better than staying silent.
- **Practise the listening test** with the teacher's audio clips.

IA marks do **not** add to your theory pass mark. You must still get 27 out of 80 in the written paper.

## 5. Must-do question bank (80 questions)

★ = asked many times, so do this first. Years in brackets are board papers (from those read) where the same or a very similar question appeared. "SQP" = 2026-27 Sample Paper. Model answers follow the 2026-27 marking scheme where it covers the same question; the rest are written to the NCERT text.
Word limits: short answers 40-50 words; long answers 120-150 words. The model answers below keep to these limits so you can learn them as they are.

### Part A: Writing (Q1-Q9), with full sample answers

**Q1 ★ Notice (4 marks; notices in 2019, 2020, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026, SQP).** You are Shivani/Shivam, President, Sports Club of ABC International School, Newtown. Write a notice about an inter-house handball tournament, with participation criteria, registration process and contact information. Put it in a box. (2026)

**Model answer (write it inside a box):**

> **ABC INTERNATIONAL SCHOOL, NEWTOWN**
> **NOTICE**
> 10 November 20XX
> **INTER-HOUSE HANDBALL TOURNAMENT**
> The Sports Club is organising an Inter-House Handball Tournament for students of Classes IX-XII.
> Dates: 25-27 November 20XX | Time: 2 pm-5 pm | Venue: School Sports Ground
> Eligibility: each house can send one team of 12 players (boys and girls).
> Registration: give names to your House Captain by 18 November.
> For details, contact the undersigned.
> *(Signature)*
> Shivani
> President, Sports Club

**Checklist for full format mark:** box, school name at top centre, NOTICE, date on the left, heading, and name + designation + signature at the bottom left. Answer **what, when (date, time), where, who can take part, how to register, whom to contact**.

**Q8 ★ Article (5 marks; articles in 2019, 2020, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026, SQP).** You are Rajni/Raghav, upset about the increase in cyber bullying among school students. Write an article exploring the reasons and suggesting solutions. (2026)

**Model answer (about 150 words):**

> **CYBER BULLYING: STOP THE HIDDEN HURT**
> *By Raghav*
>
> Today almost every teenager has a phone, and with it has come a new danger: cyber bullying. Hurtful messages, fake profiles and embarrassing photos are making many students anxious, lonely and afraid to go to school.
>
> Why does it happen? Peer pressure makes some students join in to look "cool". Low self-esteem makes others hurt people to feel powerful. A lack of empathy, and the freedom to hide behind a screen, make bullies feel safe.
>

### Part B: Reading skills (Q10-Q12), solved from the 2026-27 SQP

You cannot learn the passage in advance, but you can learn the **question types**. These three are from the SQP with the official answers.

**Q10 (MCQ / tone, SQP)** The passage about the Geminids meteor shower gives facts on its origin, causes and how to watch it. The writer's tone is best described as: (A) persuasive and promotional (B) explanatory and informative, aimed at scientific understanding (C) speculative and argumentative (D) celebratory and emotional.
**Ans: (B).** The passage explains facts in a neutral way. *(Tip: factual passages are almost always "informative / objective".)*

**Q11 (find evidence, SQP)** Which evidence from paragraph 1 tells us that the Geminids are exceptionally active?
**Ans:** "Observation rates can exceed 100 meteors per hour." *(Tip: "evidence" = copy the exact line or number from the paragraph named.)*

**Q12 (case-based data, 2 marks, SQP)** UPI transactions grew from 4.21 billion (Oct 2021) to 16.58 billion (Oct 2024), and their value from ₹7.71 lakh crore to ₹23.49 lakh crore. Rohan pays for groceries and transport by phone. Give two reasons why UPI is more suitable for him in 2024 than in 2021.
**Ans (any two, 1 mark each):** (1) The huge rise in the number of transactions shows the system is now reliable and stable for daily use. (2) It is now accepted almost everywhere, even by small roadside vendors, so he does not need cash. (3) The rise in value shows people trust UPI even for bigger payments.

### Part C: Flamingo prose, short answers 40-50 words (Q13-Q37)

#### The Last Lesson (Alphonse Daudet)

**Q13 ★ (2019)** Whom did M. Hamel blame for the neglect of learning French by boys like Franz?
**Ans:** M. Hamel blamed everyone. The **parents** preferred to send children to work on farms and in mills to earn money. Franz himself was **lazy** and kept putting off learning. M. Hamel blamed **himself** too, because he sent pupils to water his flowers and gave a holiday when he wanted to go fishing.

**Q14 ★ (2024, 2026)** "I won't scold you, little Franz; you must feel bad enough." Why must Franz feel bad? / How did the people realise the value of their language only when they were about to lose it?
**Ans:** Franz could not say the rule for participles, and it was the **last French lesson**, as an order from Berlin said only German would be taught in Alsace and Lorraine. He felt sorry for wasting time. The villagers sitting in the class also regretted not learning French; the fear of losing it made them value it.

**Q15 What did M. Hamel say about the French language and about language in general?**
**Ans:** He said French was the most beautiful language in the world, the clearest and the most logical. He asked them to guard it, because when a people are enslaved, as long as they hold fast to their language, it is as if they had the key to their prison.

#### Lost Spring (Anees Jung)

**Q16 ★ (2023, 2026, SQP)** What vicious circle are the bangle makers of Firozabad trapped in? Why can they not imagine a different future?
**Ans:** They are trapped by the **sahukars, middlemen, policemen, keepers of law, bureaucrats and politicians**, who together keep them poor. They are also bound by caste and the belief that bangle-making is their **God-given lineage (karam)**. Fear of the police, lack of education and of leaders keep them from organising, so suffering seems unchangeable.

**Q17 ★ (2019, long answer idea)** "Seemapuri, a place on the periphery of Delhi, yet miles away from it, metaphorically." Explain.
**Ans:** Seemapuri is close to Delhi, but life there is very different. About 10,000 ragpickers, refugees from Bangladesh (1971), live in mud huts with no sewage, drainage or running water. They have ration cards but no identity. For their children, garbage is "gold", their daily bread. Delhi's comforts are far away from them.

**Q18 What was Saheb's new job, and was he happy with it?**
**Ans:** Saheb started working at a tea stall for 800 rupees and all his meals. He was **not** happy. Earlier, as a ragpicker, he was his own master. Now the steel canister he carried was heavier than the plastic bag on his shoulder, because the canister belonged to the tea-stall owner. He had lost his freedom.

#### Deep Water (William O. Douglas)

**Q19 ★ (2020, 2023)** How did Douglas's fear of water start?
**Ans:** At three or four, a wave knocked him down on a California beach and frightened him. At ten or eleven, at the YMCA pool, a big boy of about eighteen picked him up and threw him into the deep end. He nearly drowned, going down three times, and the terror of water stayed with him for years.

**Q20 ★ (2019, 2025)** Why was Douglas determined to overcome his fear, and how did the instructor make him a swimmer?
**Ans:** The fear spoiled his fishing, canoeing and swimming, so he decided to beat it. He hired an instructor, who tied a rope to a belt round him through a pulley and taught him, bit by bit for months, to put his face in water, exhale under water, inhale above it, and kick his legs. Finally he taught all of him to swim.

**Q21 ★ (SQP, 2026)** What does Douglas's experience suggest about fear?
**Ans:** Fear is made worse by **anticipation**, by imagining what may happen. His terror came more from the memory and imagined danger than from water itself. Through steady practice he overcame it and learned that "All we have to fear is fear itself" (President Roosevelt). Facing fear brings freedom.

**Q22 ★ (2023)** Who was Edla Willmansson? Why did she come to the forge at night?

**Q23 ★ (2024)** What did the peddler say in his defence when it was clear he was not the person the ironmaster thought him to be?
**Ans:** He said it was not his fault. He had never pretended to be anyone but a poor trader; it was the ironmaster who mistook him. He said the whole world is a big rattrap, and warned that the ironmaster too might one day be tempted by good things and be caught.

**Q24 ★ (SQP)** How did the Christmas episode change the peddler?
**Ans:** Edla treated him like a captain, with trust and kindness, even after she knew the truth, and asked that he spend Christmas with them. This woke his conscience. #### Indigo (Louis Fischer)

**Q25 ★ (2020)** What was the arrangement between the sharecroppers of Champaran and their British landlords?
**Ans:** Most of the land was owned by British planters and worked by Indian tenants. By a long-term contract, the peasants had to plant **15% of their land (3 parts in 20) with indigo** and give the whole indigo harvest to the landlords as rent. This was called the tinkathia system.

**Q26 ★ (2026)** How did Rajendra Prasad's servants treat Gandhi?
**Ans:** Gandhi went with Rajkumar Shukla to Rajendra Prasad's house in Patna. The servants knew Shukla as a poor yeoman and thought Gandhi was another peasant. **Q27 ★ (2025)** Give an account of Gandhi's reception at Motihari.
**Ans:** At Motihari, a huge crowd greeted Gandhi at the station. He was served a notice to leave Champaran at once, but he refused. The next day thousands of peasants gathered around the court. This spontaneous demonstration was the beginning of their freedom from fear of the British. The case was later dropped.

**Q28 ★ (2019)** Why did Gandhi consider the Champaran episode a turning point in his life?
**Ans:** Gandhi said, "What I did was a very ordinary thing. I declared that the British could not order me about in my own country." Champaran showed that peaceful civil disobedience could succeed. It taught the peasants courage and self-reliance, and it became the start of his struggle against British rule.

#### The Interview (Christopher Silvester / Umberto Eco)

**Q32 ★ (2026)** What does Umberto Eco call "interstices"?

### Part D: Flamingo poetry (Q38-Q52)

#### My Mother at Sixty-Six (Kamala Das)

**Q38 ★ (2019, 2026)** Why does the poet compare her mother's face to a "late winter's moon"?
**Ans:** It is a **simile**. A late winter's moon is pale, dim and about to set. Her mother's face looked pale, lifeless and tired with age, like that moon. It suggests the mother is near the end of her life, which awakens the poet's fear of losing her.

**Q39 ★ (2019, 2023)** What was the poet's "childhood fear"? Why did she smile and smile?
**Ans:** Her childhood fear was the fear of **losing her mother** (separation and death). At the airport she hid this pain. She only said "see you soon, Amma" and smiled and smiled, to hide her sadness and give her mother, and herself, hope and courage.

**Q40 ★ (SQP, 2024)** What contrast does the poet show between her mother and the scenes outside the car?
**Ans:** Inside, her mother dozed with her mouth open, her face ashen like a corpse, a sign of old age and decay. Outside, young trees were "sprinting" and merry children were "spilling out of their homes", signs of youth, energy and life. The contrast shows life moving on while her mother ages.

#### Keeping Quiet (Pablo Neruda)

**Q41 ★ (2023, 2026)** Why does the poet ask us to count to twelve and keep still?
**Ans:** Twelve stands for the hours on a clock or months of a year. The poet asks everyone to stop all activity and not speak in any language for a moment. This shared silence will make us look inward, understand ourselves and others, and stop the harm we do to people and nature.

**Q42 ★ (2025)** "What I want should not be confused with total inactivity." Explain.
**Ans:** The poet is not asking for death or laziness. He wants a short pause for **introspection**. "Life is what it is about." Stillness will help us see how our single-minded rush causes wars, sadness and destruction, and will help us live better.

**Q43 What will happen during the "exotic moment" of silence?**
**Ans:** There will be no rush and no engines. Fishermen will not harm whales; the salt gatherer will look at his hurt hands; those who prepare green wars, wars with gas and fire will stop and walk with their brothers. All will be together in a sudden strangeness, and peace and brotherhood will grow.

#### A Thing of Beauty (John Keats)

**Q44 ★ (2025, SQP)** "A thing of beauty is a joy for ever." Explain. / Why is beauty everlasting?
**Ans:** A beautiful thing gives us joy that never ends. Its loveliness keeps increasing and never passes into nothingness. Like a quiet bower, it gives us peaceful sleep full of sweet dreams, good health and quiet breathing. Its memory comforts us long after we see it.

**Q45 ★ (2026)** What does Keats refer to by "dark spirits"?
**Ans:** "Dark spirits" means our **sad and gloomy mood**. Despondence, the lack of noble people, gloomy days and unhealthy ways of searching fill us with sadness. Some shape of beauty removes this "pall" (cover of sadness) from our dark spirits and gives hope.

**Q46 List the things of beauty mentioned in the poem.**
**Ans:** The sun, the moon, old and young trees giving shade to sheep, daffodils in green surroundings, clear rills (streams) that make a cool cover for themselves, the mid-forest thicket with musk-rose blooms, the grand stories of the mighty dead, and all lovely tales. Together they are an endless fountain of immortal drink.

#### A Roadside Stand (Robert Frost)

**Q47 ★ (2025)** How does the poet show the mean and selfish nature of city dwellers?
**Ans:** City people rush past in their cars without looking at the stand. If one stops, it is only to ask the way or to complain that the stand spoils the landscape, or that the sign is badly painted (N and S turned wrong). They do not buy. Even those who help the poor are called "greedy good-doers" and "beneficent beasts of prey".

**Q48 ★ (2026, SQP)** Why is the stand's plea called "pathetic"? Why does the poet say it is not asking for "a dole of bread"?
**Ans:** The small stand begs travellers to stop and buy, but they ignore it, which makes its plea sad and helpless. The rural people do not want charity (a dole of bread). They want to **earn** some city money through fair trade, the cash that keeps cities prosperous.

**Q49 What promise was made to the rural poor, and how does the poet feel about it?**
**Ans:** The rural poor were told they would be moved to villages next to theatres and stores, where they would not have to think for themselves. These "good-doers" would take away their freedom and land. The poet feels deep pain at their wait and longs to end it all with one blow.

#### Aunt Jennifer's Tigers (Adrienne Rich)

**Q50 ★ (2023, 2024)** Why do Aunt Jennifer's tigers not fear the men beneath the tree? What do they represent?
**Ans:** The tigers are proud, bold and confident, "topaz denizens of a world of green". They pace in "sleek chivalric certainty". They represent the **freedom and courage** Aunt Jennifer lacks in her own life. Through them she expresses her hidden wish to be free and fearless.

**Q51 ★ (2020)** "The massive weight of Uncle's wedding band / Sits heavily upon Aunt Jennifer's hand." Explain.
**Ans:** The wedding ring stands for **marriage and the husband's control**. Its weight is not physical but emotional; the duties and fear of an unhappy, male-dominated marriage crush her. That is why her fingers flutter and she finds even the ivory needle hard to pull through the wool.

**Q52 ★ (2020, SQP)** "When Aunt is dead, her terrified hands will lie / Still ringed with ordeals she was mastered by." What is the irony?
**Ans:** Even in death Aunt Jennifer will not be free; her hands stay "ringed" by the trials of marriage. But the tigers she made will go on prancing, proud and unafraid. The irony is that she creates fearless tigers while she herself remains timid and suppressed all her life.

#### Journey to the End of the Earth (Tishani Doshi)

**Q59 ★ (2020, 2025)** What was Gondwana? How was it different from today's world?
**Ans:** Gondwana was a huge **supercontinent**, centred around present-day Antarctica, about 650 million years ago. The climate was much warmer and it had many kinds of plants and animals. There were no humans. It broke up into separate countries around the time the dinosaurs died out.

**Q60 ★ (2025)** Why did Tishani Doshi feel she was walking into a giant ping-pong ball?
**Ans:** In Antarctica everything was white, with an endless blue sky. There were **no human markers**: no trees, buildings, billboards or other reference points. Without these, she lost all sense of perspective and time, as if she were inside a huge white ping-pong ball.

**Q61 ★ (2024, 2026, SQP)** Why is Antarctica a good place to study climate change? / How is the book a warning?
**Ans:** Antarctica has no human population, so it is a pristine place. Its ice cores hold half-million-year-old carbon records, so it shows clearly how the earth's climate has changed. Retreating glaciers and collapsing ice shelves warn us: will the West Antarctic ice sheet melt? Small changes, like a few degrees of warming, can have huge effects, so humans must act responsibly now.

### Part F: Extract practice (Q71-Q72), the question style used in Q7-Q9

**Q71 ★ Keeping Quiet extract (2026; Keeping Quiet extracts also 2023, 2025)**
"Now we will count to twelve / and we will all keep still. / For once on the face of the Earth / let's not speak in any language, / let's stop for one second, / and not move our arms so much."
(i) "we will all keep still" reflects: (A) the poet's willingness to keep quiet (B) the significance of stillness (C) the poet's appeal for collective engagement (D) the purpose of counting to twelve. **Ans: (C)**, he asks **all** of us to be still together.
(ii) What does the poet want us to stop? **Ans:** all our activity: speaking, moving and the rush of work, for a moment.
(iii) A: The poet advocates keeping quiet. R: He believes silence can bring profound changes in our perspective and lifestyle. **Ans: (B)** Both true, and R explains A.
(iv) The poet urges people to count to twelve so that **everyone stops at the same moment and becomes still and silent.**
(v) Counting to twelve is a **synchronized** effort.
(vi) How can this message promote peace today? **Ans:** If people and nations pause to reflect before acting, they will understand one another and avoid fights and wars.

"And in dark hutments, next to lines of flames of flickering oil lamps, sit boys and girls with their fathers and mothers, welding pieces of coloured glass into circles of bangles. ### Part G: Long answers, 120-150 words (Q73-Q80)

**How to write a long answer for 5 marks:** 1 opening line that answers the question directly + 3-4 points from the story (names, events, a short quote) + 1 closing line with the message. Keep to about 150 words.

**Q73 ★ (2020; bangle makers also asked 2023, 2026, SQP)** The life of the bangle makers of Firozabad shows the grinding poverty and traditions that condemn thousands to a life of misery. Elaborate.
**Ans:** Firozabad is the centre of India's glass-bangle industry, and about 20,000 children work there illegally. Families like Mukesh's work in dark, dingy cells beside hot furnaces, without air or light, so many children lose their eyesight. They live in poverty in stinking lanes.
They cannot escape because they are trapped in a **vicious circle** of the sahukars, middlemen, policemen, keepers of law, bureaucrats and politicians. Fear of the police, and having no leader, stop them from forming cooperatives. They also believe bangle-making is their **"karam"**, their God-given lineage. Even Savita, a young girl, makes bangles that are a symbol of a married woman's happiness, yet the old woman who has made bangles all her life has not had a full meal.
Only Mukesh dares to dream of becoming a motor mechanic. Education and courage are the only way to break this circle.

**Q74 ★ (2026; The Rattrap 2019, 2025; Going Places 2019)** The peddler's temptation for money and Sophie's unrealistic dreams were the root causes of their suffering. Analyse with reference to "The Rattrap" and "Going Places".
**Ans:** Both characters suffer because they chase something unreal.
The peddler believed the world is a rattrap that tempts people with "bait". Yet he himself fell into it: he stole the old crofter's thirty kronor, then lost his way in the forest and wandered in fear. He also accepted the ironmaster's invitation by pretending to be Captain von Stahle. Only Edla's kindness freed him, and he returned the money.
Sophie, a poor girl due to work in a biscuit factory, dreamed of owning a boutique, becoming an actress and meeting football star Danny Casey. She imagined a date with Danny and waited for him by the canal, but he never came. Her fantasy left her sad and alone, and her family did not believe her.
So greed and daydreams trap us. Honest work, realistic goals and the kindness of others help us escape.

**Q75 (2026)** Analyse the portrayal of fear and freedom in "Deep Water" and "Aunt Jennifer's Tigers".
**Ans:** Both texts show how fear can imprison a person, but they end differently.
In "Deep Water", William Douglas developed a terror of water after being thrown into the deep end of the YMCA pool as a boy. The fear spoiled his fishing, canoeing and swimming for years. He fought back: he hired an instructor and practised for months, and then swam alone in Lake Wentworth and Warm Lake. He won **freedom** from fear, learning that "All we have to fear is fear itself."
Aunt Jennifer, in Rich's poem, is crushed by the weight of an unhappy marriage, shown by "Uncle's wedding band". Her hands are "terrified" and stay "ringed with ordeals" even in death. Her only freedom is in her art: the tigers she sews are proud, fearless and free.
So Douglas finds real freedom through his own effort, while Aunt Jennifer finds it only in imagination.

**Q76 (2025; both poems asked many years)** "My Mother at Sixty-Six" and "Aunt Jennifer's Tigers" explore the themes of pain and conflict. Explain.
**Ans:** Both poems show women's pain, and a conflict between inner feelings and outward behaviour.
In "My Mother at Sixty-Six", Kamala Das sees her ageing mother dozing in the car, her face ashen "like that of a corpse" and pale "as a late winter's moon". She feels her old childhood fear of losing her mother. But she hides this pain; she says only "see you soon, Amma" and smiles. The conflict is between her fear and her wish to stay hopeful.
In "Aunt Jennifer's Tigers", the pain is caused by a male-dominated marriage. The heavy wedding band and her "terrified hands" show her suffering. Her inner wish for courage and freedom is shown through the bold tigers she embroiders, who "do not fear the men beneath the tree".
Both poems show that people often hide their pain and find small ways, like a smile or art, to cope with it.

**Q77 ★ (2023, 2024, 2025, SQP)** Dr Sadao is torn between his duty as a doctor and his duty as a patriotic Japanese. Elaborate. / Is he both a true patriot and a compassionate human being?
**Ans:** Dr Sadao Hoki, a Japanese surgeon, finds a wounded American prisoner of war washed up on the beach outside his house during the war. As a patriot, he knows he should hand the enemy over to the police. But as a doctor, he cannot let a wounded man die.
He chooses humanity. He and his wife Hana take the soldier in, and Sadao removes the bullet and saves his life, even though the servants leave the house in protest. Then he does his duty as a citizen too: he tells the General. The General promises to send his private assassins, but forgets. Sadao finally helps the soldier escape by giving him his boat, food and a flashlight, so that he is not a danger to his country either.
So Sadao is both a loyal patriot and a compassionate human being. His duty as a doctor to save life wins over hatred.

**Q78 ★ (2019, 2026, SQP)** Derry and Mr Lamb both have physical impairments, but their attitudes to life are completely different. / People with physical impairments deserve acceptance, not sympathy. How does "On the Face of It" show this?
**Ans:** Derry, a fourteen-year-old boy, has a face burnt by acid. He feels ugly and unwanted. He hates people's pity and stares, so he hides from everyone and is bitter and full of self-pity.
Mr Lamb has a tin leg, but he is cheerful and open. Children call him "Lamey-Lamb", but he does not mind. His garden gate is always open, and he loves bees, crab apples and even weeds. He tells Derry that what matters is how you see the world, not how the world sees you, and that a person can choose to live fully.
Mr Lamb does not pity Derry; he treats him as a normal friend and gives him work. This acceptance gives Derry new confidence, and he defies his mother to return to the garden.
The play shows that people with disabilities need acceptance and respect, not sympathy, and that a positive attitude is more important than appearance.

**Q79 ★ (2020; Tiger King every year)** How did the Tiger King meet his end? What is ironical about his death?
**Ans:** When the Maharaja of Pratibandapuram was born, the astrologers said he would be killed by a tiger. To escape this, he decided to kill 100 tigers. He banned tiger hunting by anyone else, spent huge sums, nearly lost his throne to a British officer, and even married a princess from a state with many tigers.
The 100th tiger was actually brought from the People's Park in Madras. The king's bullet missed it, but his frightened hunters killed it so that the king would not know.
On his son's third birthday, the king bought a **wooden toy tiger**, crudely made by a carpenter. A sliver from it pierced his right hand. It became infected, and he died during the operation.
The irony is that the king who killed so many real tigers was killed by a toy tiger. The prophecy came true, showing that no one can escape fate, and the story satirises the pride and cruelty of rulers.

**Q80 (2025; Memories of Childhood every year)** "Memories of Childhood" gives the autobiographical episodes of Zitkala-Sa and Bama. Both come from marginalised communities. How do they reflect on their relationship with mainstream culture?
**Ans:** Both writers faced discrimination as children, though in different countries and cultures.
Zitkala-Sa, a Native American girl, was taken to a missionary school. There she had to wear tight clothes and hard shoes, and her long hair was cut, even though among her people short hair was worn only by mourners and cowards. She hid under a bed but was dragged out and tied to a chair. She felt like "one of many little animals driven by a herder" and lost her spirit. Yet she resisted.
Bama, a Dalit girl from Tamil Nadu, saw an elder of her street carrying vadai by a string for a landlord, without touching it. Her brother Annan explained untouchability and told her that **education** could remove such indignities. She studied hard and stood first.
Both show the pain of being treated as inferior, and both chose courage and learning to fight back.

## 6. Quick revision sheet

### 6a. Writing formats (learn by heart; 1 format mark in each of Q3-Q6)

| Task | Words | Format to remember | Closing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Notice** | about 50 | **Box**; organisation name (top centre); NOTICE (centre); date (left); heading; body (what, when, where, who, how to register, contact); signature, name, designation (bottom left) | name + designation |
| **Formal invitation (card)** | about 50 | **Third person**: "X requests the pleasure of your company at ... on (day, date) at (time) at (venue)"; chief guest / highlight; RSVP | no signature, no "Dear" |
| **Formal reply** | about 50 | Sender's address, date, receiver, subject; thank, accept/decline, repeat date-time-place, reason (if declining). Or third person if replying to a card | "Yours sincerely" |
| **Letter to the Editor** | 120-150 | Sender's address, date, "The Editor", newspaper, city, subject, "Sir/Madam"; para 1 purpose, para 2 problem + effects, para 3 suggestions, closing line | **"Yours truly"** + name |
| **Job application + bio-data** | 120-150 | Sender's address, date, receiver (HR Manager, company, city), subject; mention the advertisement; show you match each requirement; request interview; "Enclosure: Bio-data"; bio-data with personal details, qualifications, experience, skills, references | **"Yours sincerely"** + name |
| **Article** | 120-150 | **Heading** + **by-line** (By ...); intro, causes/effects, solutions, conclusion | none |
| **Report** | 120-150 | **Headline**, **by-line** (name + designation), **place and date**; para 1: what, why, who, when, where; para 2: how (details); para 3: result / future steps | none |

**Ready-made useful lines:**
- Letter to the Editor opening: "Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper, I wish to draw the attention of the authorities to ..."
- Letter closing: "I hope the authorities concerned will take immediate action."
- Job application opening: "In response to your advertisement in *(paper)* dated ..., I wish to apply for the post of ..."
- Job application closing: "I would be grateful for an opportunity to appear for an interview."
- Article ending: "If we act together today, we can build a better tomorrow."
- Report opening: "*(Club)* of *(school)* organised *(event)* on *(date)* in *(venue)* to *(purpose)*."

**Use the cues!** Every letter, article and report question gives bullet cues. Turn **each cue into one or two sentences**. That alone covers most of the 2 content marks.

### 6c. Poetic devices that are asked in extracts

| Device | Meaning | Example from the syllabus |
|---|---|---|
| Simile | comparison using "like/as" | "pale as a late winter's moon"; "ashen, like that of a corpse" (My Mother at Sixty-Six) |
| Metaphor | direct comparison | "A bower quiet for us" (A Thing of Beauty); "flower of cities" (A Roadside Stand) |
| Personification | human qualities to things | "young trees sprinting" (My Mother at Sixty-Six) |
| Alliteration | same sound repeated | "prancing, proud"; "sleek chivalric certainty" (Aunt Jennifer's Tigers) |
| Repetition | words repeated for effect | "smile and smile and smile" (My Mother at Sixty-Six) |
| Oxymoron | opposite words together | "greedy good-doers", "beneficent beasts of prey" (A Roadside Stand) |
| Transferred epithet | adjective given to the wrong noun | "terrified hands" (Aunt Jennifer's Tigers) |
| Rhyme scheme | pattern of end sounds | A Thing of Beauty and Aunt Jennifer's Tigers are in rhyming couplets (aabb) |

### 6d. Reading section question types and tricks

| Question type | What to do |
|---|---|
| "Which evidence / line from para X ..." | Copy the exact line or number from that paragraph |
| Analogy (A : B :: C : ?) | Find how A and B are related (synonym? cause?), then find a word from the named paragraph with the same link |
| Tone / main idea MCQ | Factual passages: "informative / objective". Choose the option that covers the **whole** passage |
| "Complete the sentence" | Use words from the passage; keep it short and grammatical |
| Data / chart (case-based) | Say "increased / decreased / highest / lowest" and quote the figures |
| 2-mark "evaluate / assess" | Give a judgement word (largely / partly / highly) + one reason from the passage |
| Fill with one word from brackets | Read the sentence twice with each word; pick the one that matches the passage |

## 7. Answer-writing tips and a 30-day timetable

### 7a. Tips to collect marks
1. **Format first.** In Q3-Q6, the format mark is the easiest mark in the paper. Draw the box for a notice. Write "Yours truly" for a letter to the editor and "Yours sincerely" for a job application.
2. **Use every cue** given in the writing question. One cue = one or two sentences.
3. **Never leave an MCQ blank.** There is no negative marking. But write **only one** option; the marking scheme gives zero if you write two.
4. **For literature short answers (2 marks):** write 3 short sentences. Sentence 1 answers the question directly; sentence 2 gives a detail from the chapter (a name or event); sentence 3 gives the reason or result.
5. **Name the characters and the chapter** in long answers. Examiners look for "textual evidence".
6. **Simple English is fine.** Short, correct sentences get the grammar mark. Avoid long sentences that you are not sure of.
7. **Choose the easier option** in every question, and do not attempt both. If you attempt an extra question, only the better one is counted.
8. **Order of writing the paper:** Section B (writing) first while you are fresh (about 40 min), then Section C (literature, about 70 min), then Section A (reading, about 50 min). Write the question numbers exactly as in the paper (e.g. 7(a)(iii)).
9. Keep to the word limit roughly. A long answer of 120-150 words is about 12-15 lines.
10. Leave 10 minutes to check spellings of names: Douglas, Edla, Champaran, Sadao, Zitkala-Sa, Derry.
