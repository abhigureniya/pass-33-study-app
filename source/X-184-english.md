# CBSE Class X English Language and Literature (184): Pass Pack 2026-27

**Who this is for:** students who need to **pass**, meaning at least 33 out of 100 overall (theory 80 + internal assessment 20 added together).
**How to use it:** Learn the two fixed formats (formal letter and analytical paragraph) first, then the grammar rules, then the starred (★) literature answers in Section 5. Write the answers in your own notebook; do not only read them.

## 1. Exam at a glance (2026-27)

**The SQP note says:** "There is no change in the Question Paper Design and Assessment Pattern for Academic Session 2026-27."

**Two board exams (from 2026):** the main exam is in February. An optional second exam in May lets you improve your score in up to 3 subjects. If English goes badly in February, you can try again in May.

**How the marks are given (2026-27 marking scheme):**

| Question type | Marks split |
|---|---|
| Formal letter (5) | Format 1 + Content 2 + Organisation of ideas 1 + Accuracy 1 |
| Analytical paragraph (5) | Content 2 + Organisation 2 + Accuracy 1 (no title, no heading) |

The other 15 marks follow CBSE circular Acad-11/2019, which schools usually split as periodic tests 5, multiple assessment 5 and portfolio (notebook/class work) 5. Ask your teacher for your school's exact split.

**Not in the syllabus:** "The Hack Driver" and the poem "Animals" are **out**. Skip old PYQs on them. Some older papers (2016, 2018) had a different pattern (story writing, more grammar types); use them only for literature practice.

## 3. The 33-mark plan (target about 40% of theory = 32+ marks)

**Main idea:** English is the easiest subject to pass, because **30 marks (reading + grammar + writing formats) need almost no memorising.** Then add the 10 most repeated literature chapters.

### 3a. Study order (highest return first)

1. **Formal letter format (Q4):** learn the 8-part layout in Section 6. Format = 1 sure mark; a simple, correct body gives 2-3 more.
2. **Analytical paragraph (Q5):** learn the 4-part plan (introduction, points, comparison/highest-lowest, conclusion). The data or points are given in the question.
3. **Grammar (Q3):** learn the 5 rule boxes in Section 6 and do the 24 practice items in Section 5. 6 out of 10 is easy.
4. **Reading (Q1, Q2):** read the question first, then find the paragraph it names. Answer every MCQ.
5. **Literature, most repeated chapters:** Bholi, The Necklace, Madam Rides the Bus (Valli), Nelson Mandela, The Thief's Story, A Letter to God + The Sermon at Benares (pair), Mijbil the Otter, Glimpses of India, A Tiger in the Zoo + How to Tell Wild Animals (pair), Amanda!.
6. **Bonus chapters:** The Midnight Visitor, A Triumph of Surgery, The Book that Saved the Earth, The Ball Poem, Custard the Dragon, The Proposal.

### 3b. Target-marks table (theory 80)

| Part | Max | Target for a weak student | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading Q1 + Q2 | 20 | 10 | All MCQs, true/false, fill-ins; copy key words from the paragraph |
| Grammar Q3 | 10 | 6 | 5 rule boxes (Section 6c) |
| Letter Q4 | 5 | 3 | Format 1 + 3-4 simple content points |
| Analytical paragraph Q5 | 5 | 3 | Use every point given; add "highest/lowest" and a conclusion |
| Extracts Q6 + Q7 | 10 | 5 | MCQs and fill-ins are in the lines given |
| First Flight short answers Q8 | 12 | 4 | Two answers of 2 marks each from the ★ list |
| Footprints short answers Q9 | 6 | 2 | One answer (Bholi / Necklace / Thief's Story) |
| Long answers Q10 + Q11 | 12 | 4 | 5-6 key points each from the ★ long answers |
| **Total** | **80** | **37 (46%)** | Safe target 32; this leaves a 5-mark buffer |

### 3c. How internal assessment helps

- IA is 20 marks. Most regular students score 14-18. If you score even 12 in IA, you need only **21 out of 80** in theory to reach 33 overall.
- **ALS (5 marks):** speak clearly, finish your sentences, answer the teacher's questions about the listening text. Practise a 1-minute talk on a familiar topic (my school, my favourite festival).
- **Portfolio / notebook:** complete and neat class work is easy marks. Submit everything on time.
- **Periodic tests:** treat them as practice for the board pattern.

## 5. Must-do question bank (86 questions)

★ = asked many times, so do this first. Years in brackets are papers read where the same or a very similar question appeared. "SQP" = 2026-27 Sample Paper. Questions without a year are standard NCERT-based practice.

### Part A: Reading skills (Q1-Q2 pattern)

**How to attack a passage:** (1) Read the questions first. (2) Each question names a paragraph; go to that paragraph only. (3) For MCQs, cut the options that are clearly wrong. (4) For 2-mark answers, write two separate points.

**Q1 ★ (MCQ, SQP)** The word 'mainstay' in "audio routines have become a mainstay in people's daily lives" suggests that audiobooks have ______ (overpowered people's lives / become a support in people's lives).
**Ans: become a support in people's lives.** "Mainstay" means a main support.

**Q2 (Assertion-Reason, SQP)** Assertion: The most preferred scenario for listening to audiobooks is when travelling. Reason: During travel, listeners struggle with short attention span problems.
**Ans: (C)** The assertion is true (63% listen on the go), but the reason is false (the passage does not say this).
**Method for every A-R:** Is A true? Is R true? Does R explain A? Both true + explains = (A); both true, does not explain = (B); A true, R false = (C); A false, R true = (D).

**Q3 (Fact or opinion, SQP)** Classify: (i) BPA and lead are used in making mass-produced plastic toys. (iv) Wooden toys engage children's minds in deeper and more meaningful ways than plastic toys.
**Ans:** (i) is a **Fact** (it can be checked). (iv) is an **Opinion** (it is a judgement: "deeper", "more meaningful"). **Tip:** words like best, better, should, beautiful, meaningful usually mark an opinion.

**Q4 (2 marks, 2026 pattern)** Mention any two major challenges faced by the cosmetics industry (Paragraph 5).
**Ans:** Any two, copied from the paragraph: (1) intense competition; (2) complying with regulations; (3) ensuring product quality; (4) copied products / ethical sourcing. **Tip:** 2-mark "mention any two" = write two short points, numbered.

### Part B: Grammar (Q3, 10 of 12)

Rule boxes are in Section 6c. For "Error / Correction" items, write only the wrong word and the right word in the format given.

**Q5 ★ (SQP)** Error correction: "Neither the software developers nor the project manager were aware of the compatibility issues."
**Ans:** Error: **were** → Correction: **was**. With "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject (the project manager = singular).

**Q6 ★ (SQP)** Error correction: "Each of the participants in the Science Fair have been given a set of guidelines."
**Ans:** Error: **have** → Correction: **has**. "Each" is singular.

**Q7 ★ (2026)** Error correction: "True tradition is not in celebrations, but in the culture we carries forward."
**Ans:** Error: **carries** → Correction: **carry**. "We" takes a plural verb.

**Q8 (2026)** Choose: "I don't know what it is. It feel like an incredibly lucky day for me." (A) don't-doesn't (B) is-have (C) feel-feels (D) lucky-luckily
**Ans: (C) feel → feels.** "It" is singular, so the verb takes -s in the present tense.

**Q9 (2026)** Error correction: "The farmer told his sons that he had hidden a treasure in the fields but he had forget the actual place."
**Ans:** Error: **forget** → Correction: **forgotten**. Past perfect = had + past participle (V3).

**Q10 (2026)** Error correction: "All tools and frequently touched surfaces must be disinfect after each client's treatment."
**Ans:** Error: **disinfect** → Correction: **disinfected**. Passive voice = be + V3.

**Q11 ★ (SQP)** "This hill station ______ (lose) its natural beauty since the last time I saw it."
**Ans: has lost.** "Since" + a result in the present = present perfect (has/have + V3).

**Q12 (2026)** "The group ______ (walk) for hours through forests and hills before it reached the barbed wire fence."
**Ans: had walked** (or had been walking). The action happened before another past action ("reached"), so use past perfect.

**Q13 (SQP)** "We are writing to confirm that your application ______ by our selection committee last week." (A) has been reviewed (B) was reviewed (C) has been reviewing (D) will be reviewed
**Ans: (B) was reviewed.** "Last week" = a finished past time, so simple past (passive). Do not use present perfect with "last week", "yesterday", "in 2020".

**Q14 (2026)** "A case ______ against the unidentified truck driver for causing damage." (A) was registered (B) is register (C) have been registered (D) may registered
**Ans: (A) was registered.** Simple past passive (was + V3). "A case" is singular, so not "have".

**Q15 (2026)** Replace the underlined word: "Since Sheenam **study** diligently for months, she will clear the exam." (A) studying (B) was studied (C) has been studying (D) have been studied
**Ans: (C) has been studying.** "For months" + still going on = present perfect continuous (has/have been + V-ing).

**Q16 ★ (SQP)** "All students ______ wear their identification badges at all times on campus." (A) might (B) could (C) must (D) may
**Ans: (C) must.** A rule or compulsion = must.

**Q17 (SQP)** "If you practise regularly, you ______ win the quiz competition next month." (might / must)
**Ans: might.** A possibility, not a certainty.

**Q18 (2026)** "Anita ______ (ought to / would / have to) likely complete the project on time as her team is collaborating effectively."
**Ans: would.** "Would likely" shows a probable result.

**Q19 ★ (SQP)** "Please note that ______ applicants who have submitted incomplete forms are required to resubmit." (A) these (B) those (C) this (D) that
**Ans: (B) those.** Plural noun (applicants) + "who..." pointing to a particular group = those.

**Q20 (SQP)** "Saira was disappointed when she saw there was ______ food left to serve the guests." (A) few (B) least (C) any (D) little
**Ans: (D) little.** Food is uncountable; "little" = hardly any (a negative sense, matching "disappointed").

**Q21 (2026)** "______ (Several/Both/All) people attended the convention because the entry was open to all at a very nominal fee."
**Ans: Several.** "Both" is only for two. "All people" is too general here; the sense is "many people". (Several is the answer in common keys; accept your teacher's key if different.)

**Q22 (SQP)** Error correction: "What among these books have you read before?" (A) What-Which (B) among-between (C) these-this (D) have-has
**Ans: (A) What → Which.** Use "which" when choosing from a limited group.

**Q23 ★ (SQP)** Report the doctor's statement: Doctor: "You should reduce your sugar intake and exercise regularly."
**Ans:** The doctor **advised** the patient **to reduce** his/her sugar intake and exercise regularly. (Advice → advised + to + verb.)

**Q24 ★ (SQP)** Isha: "I was absent yesterday because I had to visit the dentist for my regular check-up." Complete: Isha explained that ______.
**Ans:** Isha explained that **she had been absent the previous day because she had had to visit the dentist for her regular check-up.** (was → had been; yesterday → the previous day; I → she; my → her.)

**Q25 ★ (SQP)** Juhi asked her friend, "Are you prepared for the mathematics test next week?" Complete: Juhi asked her friend ______.
**Ans:** Juhi asked her friend **if/whether she was prepared for the mathematics test the following week.** (Yes/No question → if/whether; are → was; next week → the following week; no question mark.)

**Q26 ★ (2026)** Sahil said to Shekhar, "Which are your books?" (A) Sahil asked Shekhar which his books were. (B) ...which books are yours. (C) ...which has been his books. (D) ...which are his books.
**Ans: (A).** In reported questions the word order becomes a statement (subject + verb: "his books were") and the tense moves back (are → were).

**Q27 (2026)** Suhani to Sneha: "Which books are we going to purchase today from the Book Fest?" Complete: Suhani asked Sneha ______.
**Ans:** Suhani asked Sneha **which books they were going to purchase that day from the Book Fest.** (we → they; are → were; today → that day.)

**Q28 (2026)** Harry: "Good morning Bobby, how do you do?" Bobby: "Quite well, thank you; but these are hard times." Complete: ...Bobby thanked him and said that he was quite well, but remarked ______.
**Ans:** **that those were hard times.** (these → those; are → were.)

### Part C: Writing (Q4 letter, Q5 analytical paragraph)

The formats are in Section 6a and 6b. Below are four full model answers (about 120 words in the body/paragraph). Learn **Q29** as your master template; most letters can be changed from it by swapping the problem and the solutions.

**Q29 ★ (Letter to the Editor; 2026, similar 2016, 2018, 2024, 2025)** You are Sanjana/Sanjit of Avenue Apartments, XYZ Colony, Sakshamgarh. You read in the newspaper about adulteration in food and dairy products. Write a letter to the editor of a national daily expressing your views, highlighting the health risks and suggesting measures. (about 120 words)

**Model answer:**

Avenue Apartments  
XYZ Colony  
Sakshamgarh  

6 March 20XX  

The Editor  
The Times of India  
New Delhi  

Subject: Rising food adulteration is a threat to public health  

Sir/Madam,  

Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper, I wish to draw the attention of the authorities to the growing problem of adulteration in food and dairy products.  

Recent reports show that milk is mixed with water and detergent, spices with colour and sawdust, and sweets with harmful dyes. Such food causes stomach infections, food poisoning, kidney and liver damage, and even cancer in the long run. Children and old people suffer the most.  

I suggest that the food safety department should check shops and dairies regularly and punish offenders strictly. Simple home tests for adulteration should be publicised, and consumers should buy only products with the FSSAI mark.  

I hope the authorities take quick action.  

Yours faithfully,  
Sanjana  
(Sanjana)  

**Marks check:** Format (addresses, date, subject, salutation, close) = 1; problem + effects + measures = Content 2; clear paragraphs = Organisation 1; correct grammar = Accuracy 1.

Write a letter to the Director, Municipal Board, requesting maintenance of the colony parks, installation of lighting poles and repair of walking tracks. (about 120 words)

**Q31 ★ (Analytical paragraph on points; 2026)** Analyse the given details of school activities (student councils, clubs; activities in the morning assembly; celebration of important days; competitions; sports and academic activities) and write an analytical paragraph about the impact of school activities on students. Focus on leadership, responsibility, communication skills, discipline, daily motivation and team building. (about 120 words)

**Model answer (no title):**
School activities play a major role in shaping a student's personality. Firstly, student councils and clubs develop leadership and a sense of responsibility, as students plan events and take decisions. Secondly, activities in the morning assembly, such as speeches and news reading, build confidence and communication skills. Moreover, celebrating important days teaches students about culture and values, while competitions encourage healthy rivalry and hard work. Sports, on the other hand, build discipline, fitness and team spirit, because players learn to win and lose together. Academic activities like quizzes keep students motivated every day. However, these activities must be balanced with studies. In conclusion, school activities turn students into confident, disciplined and cooperative young people who are ready for life.

**Q32 ★ (Analytical paragraph on a chart; 2023 type, 2024, 2025 type)** The bar chart shows the hours per week spent by teenagers on: Social media 14, Studies outside school 10, Outdoor games 4, Reading books 2. Write an analytical paragraph in about 120 words. *(Practice data, not from a paper.)*

**Model answer (no title):**
The bar chart shows how many hours a week teenagers spend on four activities. Social media takes the largest share at 14 hours, which is more than all the other leisure activities put together. Studies outside school come second with 10 hours, showing that students still give importance to self-study. However, outdoor games get only 4 hours a week, which is less than one-third of the time spent on social media. Reading books is the least popular activity at just 2 hours. Overall, the data suggests that screens are replacing physical activity and reading among teenagers. If this trend continues, it may affect their health and concentration. Therefore, parents and schools should encourage outdoor games and reading habits.

**Analytical paragraph checklist:** introduction (what the chart/points show) → highest → next → lowest (with exact numbers) → comparison words (whereas, however, twice as much) → conclusion/trend. **Never** give a title, and never just list the points without analysing them.

### Part D: First Flight (prose and play)

Short answers are in about 40-50 words (3 marks). Long answers are in about 100-120 words (6 marks). For long answers, write 5-6 points in paragraphs, with an introduction line and a conclusion line.

#### A Letter to God

**Q33 (3 marks, 2023)** What did Lencho hope for and why?
**Ans:** Lencho hoped for rain, or at least a shower. His field of ripe corn, dotted with flowers, needed only a downpour to give a good harvest. When the rain came, he called the big drops "new coins". But the rain turned into a hailstorm that destroyed his whole crop.

**Q34 (3 marks, SQP)** How does the hailstorm work as more than just a weather event in the story?
**Ans:** The hailstorm is the turning point. It destroys Lencho's crop and leaves his family facing hunger, so it tests his faith. Because of it, Lencho writes to God, and the postmaster and his employees show kindness by collecting money. So the storm reveals both Lencho's strong faith and human goodness.

**Q35 ★ (3 marks)** Why did Lencho call the post office employees "a bunch of crooks"? What is ironic about it?
**Ans:** Lencho had asked God for 100 pesos but received only 70. He was sure God could not make a mistake, so he believed the post office employees had stolen the rest. The irony is that these were the very people who had collected the money out of kindness to save his faith.

#### Nelson Mandela: Long Walk to Freedom

**Q36 ★ (3 marks, 2024; extract 2026)** "We, who were outlaws not so long ago, have today been given the rare privilege to be host to the nations of the world on our own soil." What is the significance of this statement?
**Ans:** Under apartheid, South Africa's white government treated Black people as second-class citizens, and many countries had cut off relations with South Africa. Mandela and his comrades were called outlaws and jailed. Now, after the first democratic, non-racial election, they were hosting world leaders. It marked the victory of justice, peace and human dignity.

**Q37 ★ (MCQ, 2026 extract)** The tone of Mandela in the inaugural lines is: 1. unifying 2. determined 3. aggressive 4. pessimistic 5. hopeless. (A) 1, 2 and 4 (B) 2 and 4 (C) 1 and 2 (D) 4 and 5
**Ans: (C) 1 and 2: unifying and determined.**

**Q38 ★ (3 marks)** What are the "twin obligations" Mandela mentions? Why could a Black man not fulfil them under apartheid?
**Ans:** Every man has twin obligations: to his family (parents, wife and children) and to his people, community and country. In apartheid South Africa, a Black man who tried to serve his people was punished and isolated, and torn from his family. So he could not fulfil both duties.

**Q39 ★ (6 marks, 2023)** Mandela said, "If people can learn to hate, they can be taught to love." Discuss.
**Ans:**
- Mandela believed that **no one is born hating** another person because of the colour of skin, background or religion. Hatred is learnt from society.
- Since hate is taught, love can also be taught, because "love comes more naturally to the human heart than its opposite".
- Even in the darkest times in prison, he saw "a glimmer of humanity" in a guard, which kept his hope alive.
- He believed that the oppressor is also a prisoner of hatred; both must be freed.
- This belief helped him forgive, unite Black and white South Africans and build a "rainbow nation".
- Conclusion: Mandela's words show that goodness can be brought out in everyone through love, education and example.

#### Two Stories about Flying

**Q40 ★ (3 marks)** How did the young seagull's mother make him fly?
**Ans:** The young seagull was afraid to fly and was starving on the ledge. His mother flew towards him with a piece of fish in her beak and stopped just short of him. Maddened by hunger, he dived at the fish, fell outwards and downwards, then his wings spread out and he began to fly.

#### The Sermon at Benares

**Q56 (3 marks, 2023)** What did Siddhartha Gautama see by chance when he was about twenty-five years old?

**Q57 ★ (6 marks, 2024, 2025, 2026)** How do Lencho ("A Letter to God") and Kisa Gotami ("The Sermon at Benares") respond to suffering and loss? How do they overcome it?
**Ans:**

- His faith was so strong that he did not doubt God even when he received only 70 pesos; he blamed the post office employees instead. His faith helped him face the loss.
- **Kisa Gotami** lost her only son. In her grief she carried the dead body from house to house, asking for medicine to bring him back.

### Part E: First Flight (poems)

**Q60 ★ (3 marks)** How did the "dust of snow" change the poet's mood? (Dust of Snow)
**Ans:** The poet was in a sad, regretful mood. A crow sitting on a hemlock tree shook down fine snow on him. This small natural moment cheered him up and "saved some part of a day" he had "rued" (regretted). The poem shows that small things in nature can change our mood.

**Q61 ★ (3 marks, 2026)** In "Fire and Ice", what do fire and ice stand for? How can they destroy the world?
**Ans:** Fire stands for **desire** (greed, lust, anger) and ice stands for **hatred** (coldness, indifference). Desire spreads fast like fire and leads to conflict and destruction. Hatred makes people cold and cruel, and is also "great" enough to end the world. So human emotions can destroy the world.

**Q62 ★ (6 marks, 2025)** Explore the contrasting images of the tiger in "A Tiger in the Zoo" and in "How to Tell Wild Animals".
**Ans:**
- In **"A Tiger in the Zoo"** (Leslie Norris), the tiger is a sad, angry prisoner. He "stalks the length of his cage" in "quiet rage".
- The poet imagines him free: hiding in long grass near a water hole, hunting deer, and snarling at villagers at the jungle's edge.
- Locked in a concrete cell, he ignores visitors and stares at the stars with his brilliant eyes. The poem is serious and shows the cruelty of caging animals.
- In **"How to Tell Wild Animals"** (Carolyn Wells), the tiger is described with humour: if a "noble wild beast greets you" while roaming in the jungle, it is the Bengal tiger, with "black stripes on a yellow ground".
- Here the tiger is a dangerous hunter, and the tone is playful and light.
- Conclusion: one poem makes us pity the tiger and value freedom; the other makes us laugh while showing its power.

**Ans:** A boy loses his ball in the harbour and is deeply upset. ## 6. Quick revision sheet

### 6a. Formal letter format (Q4, 5 marks; format = 1 mark)

| # | Part | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sender's address (top left) | 12, Green View Colony / Guwahati |
| 2 | Date | 6 March 20XX |
| 3 | Receiver's designation and address | The Editor / The Hindu / Chennai |
| 4 | Subject (one line, starts "Subject:") | Subject: Need for a community centre in our colony |
| 5 | Salutation | Sir/Madam, |
| 6 | Body: 3 paragraphs | (a) why you are writing; (b) the problem + its effects (2-3 points); (c) your suggestions / request + a closing line |
| 7 | Complimentary close | Yours faithfully, (formal) / Yours sincerely, (if you know the person) |
| 8 | Name (and designation) | Maya Nair / President, RWA |

**Use only the name and address given in the question.** Do not write your real name or roll number.

**Ready-made lines:**
- Letter to the Editor (opening): "Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper, I wish to draw the attention of the concerned authorities to..."
- Letter to an authority (opening): "I am writing to bring to your notice..." / "I wish to request you to..."
- Effects: "This causes serious health problems such as..." / "Children and the elderly suffer the most."
- Suggestions: "I suggest that... should..." / "Strict action must be taken against..." / "Awareness campaigns should be organised."
- Closing: "I hope the authorities will take prompt action." / "Thanking you in anticipation."

**Common topics (practise one each):** noise from loudspeakers, food adulteration, waste burning, stray animals, poor street lights, parks and walking tracks, water logging, vandalism of monuments, road accidents, rainwater harvesting, community centre, enquiry about a course.

### 6b. Analytical paragraph plan (Q5, 5 marks)

1. **Introduction (1 line):** what the chart/table/points show ("The bar graph shows... from 2020 to 2025.").
2. **Main data:** highest → next → lowest, with exact numbers or percentages. For points/SWOT: strengths, then weaknesses.
3. **Comparison:** "twice as much as", "whereas", "on the other hand", "however", "in contrast".
4. **Trend / reason:** "This suggests that...", "This could be because..."
5. **Conclusion (1 line):** overall picture + a suggestion.

Linking words for the organisation mark: firstly, secondly, moreover, furthermore, however, on the other hand, while, overall, in conclusion. **No title. About 120 words. Do not simply copy the bullet points.**

### 6c. Grammar rule boxes (Q3)

**1. Tenses**

| Tense | Form | Signal words |
|---|---|---|
| Simple present | V1 / V1+s (he, she, it) | every day, always, facts |
| Present continuous | is/am/are + V-ing | now, at present |
| Present perfect | has/have + V3 | since, already, just, yet, recently |
| Present perfect continuous | has/have been + V-ing | for two hours, since morning (still going on) |
| Simple past | V2 | yesterday, last week, in 2020, ago |
| Past perfect | had + V3 | the earlier of two past actions ("had left before I came") |
| Future | will/shall + V1 | tomorrow, next week |
| Passive | be (is/was/has been/will be) + V3 | "A case was registered." |

**2. Modals:** must = rule/compulsion; should / ought to = advice, duty; can = ability; could = past ability, polite request; may = permission, possibility; might = weaker possibility; would = polite request, likely result; need not = not necessary. **After a modal, always use V1** (must go, not must goes).

**3. Determiners:** a (consonant sound), an (vowel sound: an hour, an MLA), the (a particular thing). this/these (near), that/those (far or "the ones who"). few/a few + countable nouns (books); little/a little + uncountable (water, food, time). "Few/little" = hardly any (negative); "a few/a little" = some (positive). much (uncountable), many (countable). each/every + singular noun. both = two. either/neither = one of two.

**4. Subject-verb agreement:**
- Singular subject → singular verb (He **plays**). Plural → plural (They **play**).
- each, every, everyone, either, neither, one of the... → **singular** ("One of the boys **is**...").
- Neither...nor / either...or / not only...but also → verb agrees with the **nearer** subject.
- The number of → singular; a number of → plural.
- Words between subject and verb do not change it: "The box **of** chocolates **is** on the table."
- "With / along with / as well as" → verb agrees with the **first** subject.

**5. Reported speech (said → told/asked; tense goes one step back):**

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| is/am/are → was/were; was → had been; have/has → had | will → would; can → could; may → might; shall → should |
| now → then; today → that day; yesterday → the previous day; tomorrow → the next/following day | this → that; these → those; here → there; ago → before |
| Statement: said that... | Yes/no question: asked **if/whether** + subject + verb |
| Wh- question: asked + wh-word + subject + verb (no "?") | Order/request/advice: ordered/requested/advised + **to** + V1 |

**Error-correction method:** (1) find the subject; (2) check its verb (singular/plural, tense); (3) check the article/determiner; (4) write only "Error | Correction".

## 7. Answer-writing tips and a 30-day timetable

### 7a. Tips to collect marks
1. **Attempt every question.** There is no negative marking. A guessed MCQ can give 1 mark; a blank gives 0.
2. **Order of writing (suggested):** Grammar → Letter → Analytical paragraph → Literature → Reading. Do what you know best first, but leave at least 40 minutes for the two reading passages.
3. **Letters:** the format mark is free. Never skip the subject line, date or "Yours faithfully".
4. **Grammar "Error/Correction":** write in the exact format asked (two columns). Do not rewrite the whole sentence unless asked.
5. **Short answers (3 marks):** first line answers the question directly; then 2 supporting points from the story; stay near 40-50 words. Use the names of characters.
6. **Long answers (6 marks):** write in 3 short paragraphs: introduction, 3-4 points, conclusion (a lesson or value). Even 3 correct points can earn 3-4 marks.
7. **Spelling of names:** Lencho, Mandela, Valli, Kisa Gotami, Bholi, Matilda, Hari Singh, Anil, Ausable, Tricki, Ebright, Think-Tank. Correct names improve the accuracy mark.
8. **Reading:** for "complete the sentence" items, take words from the paragraph. For "true/false", find the line and compare word by word.
9. **Handwriting and spacing:** leave a line between answers; write question numbers clearly in the margin.
