## 5. Must-do question bank (72 questions)

Key: ★ = repeated in board papers, learn first. Years are given only for papers actually read. "SQP" = CBSE Sample Paper 2026-27. "NCERT" = important textbook question, year not tagged.

**Assertion-Reason answer code (same in every paper):** (A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A. (B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A. (C) A is true but R is false. (D) A is false but R is true.

## SECTION A: BIOLOGY

### Chapter: Life Processes

**Q1 (MCQ, SQP)** During photosynthesis, what happens to carbon dioxide and water as glucose is formed? (A) CO2 gains hydrogen, while water loses electrons (B) CO2 loses oxygen, while water gains oxygen (C) CO2 is oxidised, while water is reduced (D) Both CO2 and water undergo reduction
**Ans: (A).** CO2 is reduced (gains hydrogen) to glucose; water is oxidised (loses hydrogen/electrons) and releases oxygen.

**Q2 (MCQ, SQP)** Which substances are reabsorbed in the kidney tubules? (A) Glucose and salts (B) Glucose and starch (C) Glycogen and salts (D) Glycogen and starch
**Ans: (A) Glucose and salts** (also amino acids and a large amount of water).

**Q3 (2 marks, NCERT)** Write the three events that take place during photosynthesis.
**Ans:**
1. Absorption of light energy by chlorophyll.
2. Conversion of light energy to chemical energy and splitting of water into hydrogen and oxygen.
3. Reduction of carbon dioxide to carbohydrates (glucose).

**Q4 (3 marks, NCERT)** Show the breakdown of glucose by various pathways.
**Ans:** All three start the same way: Glucose (6-carbon) → Pyruvate (3-carbon) + energy, in the **cytoplasm**.
- (a) In yeast, no oxygen (anaerobic): Pyruvate → Ethanol + CO2 + energy
- (b) In our muscles, lack of oxygen: Pyruvate → Lactic acid + energy (causes muscle cramps)
- (c) With oxygen (aerobic), in **mitochondria**: Pyruvate → CO2 + water + a lot of energy

**Q5 (3 marks, NCERT)** How are the lungs designed in human beings to maximise the area for exchange of gases?
**Ans:**
- Inside the lungs the bronchi divide into smaller tubes, the bronchioles.
- Bronchioles end in balloon-like structures called **alveoli**.
- Alveoli give a very large surface area (about 80 square metres if spread out) for gas exchange.
- Their walls are thin and have a rich network of blood capillaries, so O2 and CO2 exchange quickly.

**Q6 (3 marks, NCERT)** Why is it necessary to separate oxygenated and deoxygenated blood in mammals and birds? What is double circulation?
**Ans:**
- Mammals and birds need a lot of energy to keep their body temperature constant. Separating the two kinds of blood gives a highly efficient supply of oxygen to the body.
- **Double circulation:** blood passes through the heart **twice** in one complete cycle of the body: heart → lungs → heart (pulmonary) and heart → body → heart (systemic).

**Q7 (5 marks, NCERT)** Draw a labelled diagram of the human excretory system. Describe how urine is formed.
**Ans:** Diagram: two kidneys, two ureters, urinary bladder, urethra (also show aorta and vena cava if you can).
- The **nephron** is the basic filtering unit of the kidney.
- Blood enters the glomerulus (a cluster of capillaries) inside Bowman's capsule, where it is **filtered**.
- The filtrate has glucose, amino acids, salts, water and urea.
- In the tubule, useful substances (glucose, amino acids, salts, most of the water) are **selectively reabsorbed**.
- What is left is urine. It goes through the ureter to the urinary bladder and leaves through the urethra.

### Chapter: Control and Coordination

**Q8 ★ (3 marks, 2017, 2024, 2026)** What is a reflex action? Draw/describe the reflex arc.
**Ans:**
- **Reflex action:** a sudden, automatic and quick response to a stimulus, without thinking (for example, pulling the hand away from a hot object).
- **Reflex arc (path):** Receptor (skin) → Sensory neuron → Relay neuron in the **spinal cord** → Motor neuron → Effector (muscle of the arm).
- Reflex arcs are formed in the spinal cord so the response is quick, before the brain processes the message.

**Q9 ★ (2 marks, 2019, 2024, 2025, 2026)** How does the touch-me-not plant (Mimosa) respond to touch? How is this different from the movement of a shoot towards light?
**Ans:**
- In touch-me-not, the leaves fold when touched. Cells change shape by changing the amount of water in them. This movement is **not growth-related** and does not depend on direction.
- A shoot bending towards light (phototropism) is a **growth movement**, caused by auxin. It depends on the direction of the stimulus.

**Q10 ★ (3 marks, 2019, 2024, 2025, 2026)** Name the plant hormones that (a) promote cell elongation and help shoots bend towards light (b) promote cell division (c) inhibit growth / cause wilting of leaves. Also name one hormone that promotes stem growth.
**Ans:** (a) **Auxin** (b) **Cytokinin** (c) **Abscisic acid** (ABA). **Gibberellin** promotes stem growth.
Extra point: In phototropism, auxin moves to the **shaded side** of the shoot, so those cells grow longer and the shoot bends towards light.

**Q11 (3 marks, NCERT)** Name the hormone and gland: (a) regulates blood sugar (b) needs iodine (c) causes changes at puberty in boys (d) prepares body for emergency.
**Ans:**
- (a) **Insulin**, pancreas (less insulin causes diabetes)
- (b) **Thyroxin**, thyroid (iodine deficiency causes goitre)
- (c) **Testosterone**, testes
- (d) **Adrenaline**, adrenal gland
Also: growth hormone (pituitary): too little causes dwarfism; oestrogen (ovaries) for girls.

**Q12 (MCQ, NCERT)** Which part of the brain maintains posture and balance of the body? (A) Cerebrum (B) Cerebellum (C) Medulla (D) Pons
**Ans: (B) Cerebellum.** Medulla controls involuntary actions like blood pressure, salivation and vomiting.

**Q13 (A-R, NCERT)** A: The shoot of a plant grows towards light. R: Auxin accumulates on the side of the shoot facing the light.
**Ans: (C).** A is true. R is false: auxin moves to the **shaded** side.

### Chapter: How do Organisms Reproduce?

**Q14 ★ (5 marks, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2025)** Draw a labelled diagram of the human female reproductive system. What happens to the uterus if the egg is not fertilised? What is the role of the placenta?
**Ans:** Diagram labels: **ovary, oviduct (fallopian tube), uterus, cervix, vagina.**
- Ovaries make eggs (one egg per month) and hormones. Fertilisation takes place in the **oviduct**.
- If the egg is **not fertilised**, the thick, spongy lining of the uterus breaks down and comes out through the vagina as blood and mucus. This is **menstruation** (every month, lasts 2-8 days).
- **Placenta:** a disc-like tissue embedded in the uterine wall. It has villi on the embryo's side, which give a large surface area. Glucose and oxygen pass from mother's blood to the embryo, and wastes from the embryo pass to the mother's blood.

**Q15 (3 marks, NCERT)** Draw a longitudinal section of a flower and label the reproductive parts.
**Ans:** Labels: **stamen (anther + filament)** = male part, makes pollen; **pistil/carpel (stigma, style, ovary)** = female part; ovary contains ovules, each with an egg cell. Also label petal and sepal.
- After pollination, the pollen tube grows from the stigma through the style to the ovule. Fertilisation forms the zygote; the ovule becomes the **seed** and the ovary becomes the **fruit**.

**Q16 (MCQ, SQP)** In a mustard flower, the anthers were removed in the bud stage and the bud was covered with a paper bag. What will most likely happen? (A) Seeds and fruit by self-pollination (B) More insects, larger flowers but no fruit (C) No seeds or fruits (D) Seeds and fruits identical to the parent
**Ans: (C).** No anthers means no pollen of its own, and the bag stops pollen from other flowers, so there is no pollination and no fertilisation.

**Q17 (3 marks, NCERT)** List the methods of contraception. Why should sex-selective abortion (female foeticide) be stopped?
**Ans:**
- **Barrier methods:** condom, diaphragm. They stop sperm reaching the egg and also protect against sexually transmitted diseases (STDs) like HIV/AIDS.
- **Hormonal (oral pills):** change the hormone balance so eggs are not released.
- **Copper-T / loop** in the uterus: prevents pregnancy.
- **Surgical:** vasectomy (vas deferens blocked in males) and tubectomy (oviduct blocked in females).
- Female foeticide is illegal and reduces the female-male ratio of society.

**Q18 (2 marks, NCERT)** Differentiate between binary fission and multiple fission. Give one example each.
**Ans:** Binary fission: one cell divides into **two** daughter cells (Amoeba, Leishmania). Multiple fission: one cell divides into **many** daughter cells at once (Plasmodium, the malaria parasite).

**Q19 (2 marks, NCERT)** What is vegetative propagation? Give two advantages.
**Ans:** Growing new plants from roots, stems or leaves (not seeds), for example Bryophyllum leaves, sugarcane, rose, banana.
Advantages: (1) plants that do not make seeds (banana, orange, rose, jasmine) can be grown; (2) new plants are exactly like the parent and give flowers and fruits sooner.

**Q20 (2 marks, NCERT)** Why is variation useful for the survival of a species?
**Ans:** If the environment changes (for example, water temperature rises), some individuals with variations may survive while others die. So variation helps the species survive over time. DNA copying with small changes produces this variation.

### Chapter: Heredity

**Q21 ★ (5 marks, 2016, 2018, 2019, 2022, 2024, 2025, 2026, 2026E2)** Mendel crossed a pure tall pea plant (TT) with a pure dwarf pea plant (tt). Show the cross. Write the F1 and F2 results and the ratios. What do you conclude?
**Ans:**
- Parents: TT (tall) x tt (dwarf). Gametes: T and t.
- **F1:** all **Tt**, all plants **tall**. (Tall is dominant, dwarf is recessive.)
- F1 selfed: Tt x Tt. Gametes: T, t from each.

| | T | t |
|---|---|---|
| **T** | TT (tall) | Tt (tall) |
| **t** | Tt (tall) | tt (dwarf) |

- **F2 phenotype ratio:** Tall : Dwarf = **3 : 1**
- **F2 genotype ratio:** TT : Tt : tt = **1 : 2 : 1**
- **Conclusion:** traits are controlled by factors (genes) that come in pairs; one is dominant; the recessive trait reappears in F2, so it was present but hidden in F1. Each gamete gets only one factor of each pair.

**Q22 (4 marks, case-based, SQP)** A pea plant with violet flowers and tall height (VVTT) is crossed with a pea plant with white flowers and dwarf height (vvtt). (a) Write the F1 genotype and phenotype. (b) In F2, what fraction will be tall with white flowers? (c) What is the F2 phenotype ratio?
**Ans:**
- (a) F1: **VvTt**, all violet and tall.
- (b) Tall white plants = **3 out of 16** (3/16).
- (c) F2 ratio: violet tall : violet dwarf : white tall : white dwarf = **9 : 3 : 3 : 1**. This shows traits are inherited **independently**.

**Q23 (3 marks, NCERT)** How is the sex of a child determined in human beings?
**Ans:**
- Women have two X chromosomes (XX), so all eggs carry X.
- Men have one X and one Y (XY), so half the sperms carry X and half carry Y.
- Egg (X) + sperm (X) → **XX = girl**. Egg (X) + sperm (Y) → **XY = boy**.
- So the sex of the child depends on the **father's** sperm; there is a 50% chance of either.

**Q24 (MCQ, NCERT)** A cross between a tall plant (TT) and a short plant (tt) gave F1 plants. How many F2 plants out of 400 will be short (dwarf)?
**Ans: 100.** Dwarf = 1/4 of the F2, so 400 / 4 = 100.

**Q25 (A-R, NCERT)** A: In F1 of a cross between pure tall and pure dwarf pea plants, all plants are tall. R: Tall is the dominant trait.
**Ans: (A).** Both true and R explains A.

### Chapter: Our Environment

**Q26 ★ (3 marks, 2017, 2022, 2024, 2025, 2026, SQP)** What is biomagnification? Will its level be different at different trophic levels? Explain with a food chain.
**Ans:**
- **Biomagnification:** the increase in concentration of harmful, non-biodegradable chemicals (like pesticides, e.g. DDT) at each higher level of the food chain.
- Example chain: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle. Pesticide sprayed on crops enters the soil and water, then plants, then animals.
- These chemicals are not broken down or excreted, so they collect in the body. **The highest level is in the top consumer (eagle, or human beings).**

**Q27 ★ (A-R, SQP)** A: The number of trophic levels in a food chain is limited. R: On average, only 10% of the food eaten by organisms is available for the next level.
**Ans: (A).** Both true and R explains A. Because 90% of energy is lost at each step, very little energy is left after 3-4 levels.

**Q28 ★ (2 marks, NCERT; 10% law asked 2017, 2022, 2024, 2025, 2026)** In a food chain, producers have 10,000 J of energy. How much energy will be available to the tertiary consumers?
**Ans:** 10% law: Producer 10,000 J → primary consumer 1,000 J → secondary consumer 100 J → tertiary consumer **10 J**.

**Q29 ★ (3 marks, 2022, 2024, 2025, 2026E2)** How is ozone formed in the upper atmosphere? Why is the ozone layer important? Why is it getting damaged?
**Ans:**
- High-energy UV rays split O2 into free oxygen atoms (O2 → O + O). These atoms combine with O2 to form ozone: O + O2 → **O3**.
- The ozone layer **absorbs harmful UV radiation** from the sun, which can cause skin cancer in humans.
- Damage: man-made chemicals like **CFCs (chlorofluorocarbons)**, used in refrigerators and fire extinguishers, reduce the ozone. In 1987 UNEP agreed to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels.

**Q30 (2 marks, NCERT)** Give two differences between biodegradable and non-biodegradable substances, with examples.
**Ans:** Biodegradable substances are broken down by bacteria and other decomposers (vegetable peel, paper, cotton cloth). Non-biodegradable substances are not broken down by biological processes and stay in the environment for a long time (plastic, polythene, DDT, glass).

## SECTION B: CHEMISTRY

### Chapter: Chemical Reactions and Equations

**Q31 (2 marks, NCERT)** Balance and name the type: (a) Fe + H2O → Fe3O4 + H2 (b) CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2
**Ans:** (a) **3Fe + 4H2O → Fe3O4 + 4H2** (iron with steam). (b) CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2 + heat: **combination** reaction and **exothermic** (quick lime + water gives slaked lime, used for whitewashing).

**Q32 (3 marks, NCERT)** Give one example each of (a) thermal decomposition (b) electrolytic decomposition (c) photolytic decomposition.
**Ans:**
- (a) CaCO3 → CaO + CO2 (on heating). Also 2FeSO4 → Fe2O3 + SO2 + SO3 (green crystals turn brown).
- (b) 2H2O → 2H2 + O2 (by electricity; hydrogen volume is double that of oxygen).
- (c) 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl2 (in sunlight; white silver chloride turns grey). Used in black and white photography.

**Q33 (2 marks, NCERT)** Why does the colour of copper sulphate solution change when an iron nail is dipped in it?
**Ans:** Iron is more reactive than copper, so it **displaces** copper: Fe + CuSO4 → FeSO4 + Cu. The blue solution turns light green (FeSO4) and a brown coating of copper forms on the nail. This is a **displacement reaction**.

**Q34 (2 marks, NCERT)** What is a precipitation (double displacement) reaction? Give an example.
**Ans:** A reaction in which ions are exchanged and an insoluble solid (precipitate) forms.
Example: Na2SO4 + BaCl2 → **BaSO4 (white precipitate)** + 2NaCl.

**Q35 (3 marks, NCERT)** In the reaction CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O, identify the substance oxidised and the substance reduced. Define oxidation and reduction.
**Ans:**
- H2 is **oxidised** (gains oxygen to form H2O). CuO is **reduced** (loses oxygen to form Cu).
- Oxidation: gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen. Reduction: loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen.
- This is a **redox** reaction because both happen together.

**Q36 (2 marks, NCERT)** What is rancidity? Write two ways to prevent it.
**Ans:** When fats and oils are oxidised, their smell and taste change. This is rancidity.
Prevention: (1) add antioxidants; (2) pack in nitrogen gas (chips packets), keep in airtight containers.

### Chapter: Acids, Bases and Salts

**Q37 ★ (3 marks, 2018, 2025, 2026, 2026E2)** What is bleaching powder? How is it prepared? Write two uses.
**Ans:**
- Formula: **CaOCl2** (calcium oxychloride).
- Prepared by the action of chlorine on dry slaked lime: **Ca(OH)2 + Cl2 → CaOCl2 + H2O**.
- Uses: (1) bleaching cotton and linen in the textile industry and wood pulp in paper factories; (2) disinfecting drinking water; (3) oxidising agent in industries.

**Q38 ★ (3 marks, 2018, 2025, 2026, 2026E2, SQP)** What is Plaster of Paris? How is it obtained? What happens when water is added to it?
**Ans:**
- Formula: **CaSO4·½H2O** (calcium sulphate hemihydrate).
- Obtained by heating gypsum at **373 K**: CaSO4·2H2O → CaSO4·½H2O + 1½H2O.
- With water it sets into a hard mass of gypsum again: CaSO4·½H2O + 1½H2O → CaSO4·2H2O.
- Uses: plaster for supporting fractured bones, making toys, decoration, smooth surfaces.
- It should be stored in a moisture-proof container.

**Q39 ★ (2 marks, 2018, 2025, 2026, 2026E2, SQP)** What is water of crystallisation? Give two salts that have it.
**Ans:** The fixed number of water molecules present in one formula unit of a salt crystal.
Examples: copper sulphate **CuSO4·5H2O** (blue; turns white on heating), washing soda **Na2CO3·10H2O**, gypsum CaSO4·2H2O.

**Q40 (3 marks, NCERT)** Write the chemical name, formula and two uses of (a) baking soda (b) washing soda.
**Ans:**
- (a) Baking soda: sodium hydrogencarbonate, **NaHCO3**. Uses: baking powder (gives CO2 to make cake soft and spongy); antacid; soda-acid fire extinguishers. On heating: 2NaHCO3 → Na2CO3 + H2O + CO2.
- (b) Washing soda: sodium carbonate decahydrate, **Na2CO3·10H2O**. Uses: glass, soap and paper industries; removing permanent hardness of water; cleaning.

**Q41 (3 marks, NCERT; SQP asks the products)** What is the chlor-alkali process? Name the products and where they form.
**Ans:** Electricity is passed through brine (aqueous NaCl):
**2NaCl + 2H2O → 2NaOH + Cl2 + H2**
- **Chlorine gas** at the **anode**; **hydrogen gas** at the **cathode**; **sodium hydroxide** solution forms **near the cathode**.
- Uses: NaOH (soaps, degreasing), Cl2 (PVC, disinfectant, bleaching powder), H2 (fuel, margarine).

**Q42 (MCQ, NCERT)** A solution turns red litmus blue. Its pH is likely to be: (A) 1 (B) 4 (C) 5 (D) 10
**Ans: (D) 10.** A base turns red litmus blue, and bases have pH more than 7.

**Q43 (2 marks, NCERT)** Why does tooth decay start when the pH in the mouth falls below 5.5? How can it be prevented?
**Ans:** Bacteria in the mouth break down sugar and food particles to produce acids. Below pH 5.5 the acid attacks **tooth enamel (calcium hydroxyapatite)**. Prevention: clean the mouth after eating; use toothpaste, which is basic and neutralises the acid.

### Chapter: Metals and Non-metals

**Q44 ★ (5 marks, 2018, 2024, 2026, 2026E2)** Describe the steps to extract a metal of medium reactivity (zinc) from its ores. Also explain electrolytic refining of copper with a labelled diagram.
**Ans:**
- **Roasting** (for sulphide ore, heat in excess air): 2ZnS + 3O2 → 2ZnO + 2SO2
- **Calcination** (for carbonate ore, heat in limited air): ZnCO3 → ZnO + CO2
- **Reduction with carbon:** ZnO + C → Zn + CO
- **Electrolytic refining (copper):**
  - Anode = **impure copper** (thick block); cathode = **thin strip of pure copper**; electrolyte = **acidified copper sulphate solution**.
  - When current passes, pure copper from the anode dissolves and is deposited on the cathode.
  - Insoluble impurities settle below the anode as **anode mud**.

**Q45 (3 marks, NCERT)** Show the formation of NaCl and MgCl2 by transfer of electrons. Why do ionic compounds have high melting points?
**Ans:**
- Na (2,8,1) loses 1 electron → Na+; Cl (2,8,7) gains 1 electron → Cl-. Na+ and Cl- attract to form **Na+Cl-**.
- Mg (2,8,2) loses 2 electrons → Mg2+; each of two Cl atoms gains 1 electron → 2Cl-. Forms **MgCl2**.
- Ionic compounds have high melting points because a lot of energy is needed to break the **strong forces of attraction between oppositely charged ions**.

**Q46 (2 marks, NCERT)** What is corrosion? Write two methods to prevent rusting of iron.
**Ans:** The slow eating away of metals by air, moisture and acids. Iron forms reddish-brown rust (needs both air and water).
Prevention: painting, oiling, greasing, **galvanisation** (coating with zinc), chrome plating, anodising, making alloys (stainless steel).

**Q47 (2 marks, NCERT)** Why is sodium kept immersed in kerosene oil? Why is aluminium used for cooking utensils even though it is reactive?
**Ans:** Sodium reacts so vigorously with oxygen and water that it catches fire in open air, so it is stored in kerosene. Aluminium forms a thin protective **oxide layer** (Al2O3) that stops further reaction; it is also a good conductor of heat.

**Q48 (MCQ, NCERT)** Which reaction will NOT take place? (A) Zn + CuSO4 (B) Fe + CuSO4 (C) Cu + FeSO4 (D) Mg + FeSO4
**Ans: (C).** Copper is less reactive than iron, so it cannot displace iron.

### Chapter: Carbon and its Compounds

**Q49 ★ (2 marks, 2017, 2018, 2025, SQP)** What happens when ethanol is heated with excess concentrated sulphuric acid at 443 K? Write the equation and the role of the acid.
**Ans:** Ethanol loses water and forms **ethene**:
**CH3CH2OH → CH2=CH2 + H2O** (hot conc. H2SO4, 443 K)
Conc. H2SO4 acts as a **dehydrating agent** (removes water).

**Q50 ★ (3 marks, 2016, 2025, 2026, SQP)** What is esterification? Write the equation. What happens when the ester is treated with NaOH?
**Ans:**
- Ethanoic acid reacts with ethanol in the presence of an acid catalyst (conc. H2SO4) to form a sweet-smelling **ester**:
  **CH3COOH + CH3CH2OH → CH3COOC2H5 + H2O** (ethyl ethanoate)
- Esters are used in perfumes and flavouring agents.
- With NaOH, the ester gives back alcohol and the sodium salt of the acid: CH3COOC2H5 + NaOH → C2H5OH + CH3COONa. This is **saponification** (used to make soap).

**Q51 (3 marks, NCERT)** Explain the cleansing action of soap (micelle formation).
**Ans:**
- A soap molecule has two ends: an **ionic (hydrophilic) end** that dissolves in water, and a long **hydrocarbon (hydrophobic) tail** that dissolves in oil.
- In water, soap molecules arrange in clusters called **micelles**: tails point inward towards the oily dirt, ionic ends point outward into water.
- The oily dirt collects in the centre of the micelle, which stays in the water as an emulsion and is rinsed away.
- Soap does not work well in hard water because it forms an insoluble scum with calcium and magnesium salts. Detergents work in hard water.

**Q52 (2 marks, NCERT)** What is a homologous series? Write two characteristics.
**Ans:** A series of carbon compounds with the same functional group and similar chemical properties, where each member differs from the next by a **-CH2-** unit.
Features: (1) successive members differ in molecular mass by **14 u**; (2) same chemical properties, gradual change in physical properties (melting and boiling points). Example: CH4, C2H6, C3H8.

**Q53 (2 marks, NCERT)** What is an addition reaction? Give the industrial use.
**Ans:** Unsaturated hydrocarbons add hydrogen in the presence of a catalyst (nickel or palladium) to become saturated.
Example: R2C=CR2 + H2 → R2CH-CHR2 (Ni catalyst). Used for **hydrogenation of vegetable oils** (to make vanaspati ghee).

**Q54 (MCQ, NCERT)** The functional group present in propanal is: (A) -OH (B) -COOH (C) -CHO (D) C=O (ketone)
**Ans: (C) -CHO (aldehyde).** Ending "-al" = aldehyde, "-ol" = alcohol, "-one" = ketone, "-oic acid" = carboxylic acid.

## SECTION C: PHYSICS

### Chapter: Light: Reflection and Refraction

**Q55 ★ (3 marks, SQP; lens numericals 2018, 2019, 2025)** An object is placed 24 cm from a convex lens of focal length 12 cm. Find the image position, magnification and nature of the image.
**Ans:**
- u = -24 cm, f = +12 cm. Lens formula: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f
- 1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/12 + 1/(-24) = 2/24 - 1/24 = 1/24, so **v = +24 cm**
- m = v/u = 24 / (-24) = **-1**
- Image is **real, inverted, same size**, 24 cm behind the lens (object at 2F gives image at 2F).

**Q56 ★ (3 marks, SQP)** A concave lens forms an image 1/3 the size of an object placed 24 cm in front of it. Find the image distance and the focal length.
**Ans:**
- u = -24 cm. For a concave lens the image is virtual and erect, so m = +1/3.
- m = v/u, so v = m x u = (1/3)(-24) = **-8 cm** (same side as object).
- 1/f = 1/v - 1/u = 1/(-8) - 1/(-24) = -3/24 + 1/24 = -2/24 = -1/12, so **f = -12 cm**.
- Power P = 1/f (in metres) = 1/(-0.12) = about **-8.3 D**.

**Q57 (3 marks, NCERT)** A concave mirror has a focal length of 15 cm. An object is placed 10 cm in front of it. Find the image position and nature.
**Ans:**
- u = -10 cm, f = -15 cm. Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f
- 1/v = 1/f - 1/u = -1/15 + 1/10 = (-2 + 3)/30 = 1/30, so **v = +30 cm** (behind the mirror)
- m = -v/u = -30/(-10) = **+3**
- Image is **virtual, erect and 3 times enlarged**. (Object between F and P.)

**Q58 (2 marks, NCERT)** The refractive index of diamond is 2.42. What does this mean?
**Ans:** Refractive index n = speed of light in vacuum / speed of light in the medium. n = 2.42 means light travels **2.42 times slower** in diamond than in vacuum. Diamond has the highest refractive index among common materials.

**Q59 (2 marks, NCERT)** Find the power of a concave lens of focal length 2 m. What is 1 dioptre?
**Ans:** P = 1/f = 1/(-2 m) = **-0.5 D**. 1 dioptre is the power of a lens whose focal length is 1 metre (1 D = 1 m^-1).

**Q60 (2 marks, NCERT)** Name the mirror used (a) as a rear-view mirror in vehicles (b) by dentists / in torches and headlights. Give reasons.
**Ans:**
- (a) **Convex mirror:** always gives an erect, diminished image and a wider field of view.
- (b) **Concave mirror:** dentists see a large image of teeth; in torches and headlights it makes a strong parallel beam of light.

### Chapter: The Human Eye and the Colourful World

**Q61 ★ (5 marks, 2017, 2018, 2026E2)** What is myopia? Write two causes. How is it corrected? Draw ray diagrams.
**Ans:**
- **Myopia (near-sightedness):** a person can see nearby objects clearly but not distant objects. The image of a distant object forms **in front of the retina**.
- Causes: (1) **excessive curvature** of the eye lens; (2) **elongation of the eyeball**.
- Correction: **concave lens** of suitable power, which diverges the rays so the image forms on the retina.
- Diagrams: (a) far point of myopic eye, (b) rays from a distant object meeting before the retina, (c) concave lens in front of the eye bringing the image onto the retina.
- Compare: **Hypermetropia** (far-sightedness): image forms behind the retina; corrected by a **convex lens**. Presbyopia (old age): corrected by **bifocal** lenses.

**Q62 ★ (3 marks, 2019, 2026)** How is a rainbow formed? Draw a diagram.
**Ans:**
- A rainbow is a natural spectrum seen in the sky after rain, **opposite to the sun**.
- Tiny water droplets in the air act like small prisms.
- Sunlight is **refracted and dispersed** as it enters the droplet, then **internally reflected** inside it, and **refracted again** as it comes out.
- Diagram: a raindrop with white light entering, splitting into colours, reflecting at the back, and coming out with violet and red separated.

**Q63 (2 marks, NCERT)** Why is the colour of the clear sky blue?
**Ans:** Air molecules and tiny particles in the atmosphere are smaller than the wavelength of visible light. They scatter **blue light (shorter wavelength)** much more than red light. This scattered blue light enters our eyes, so the sky looks blue. (Danger signals are red because red light is scattered the least and can be seen from far.)

**Q64 (2 marks, NCERT)** Why do stars twinkle but planets do not?
**Ans:** Starlight passes through layers of air with changing refractive index (**atmospheric refraction**). Stars are very far and act as point sources, so the amount of light entering the eye keeps changing and the star seems to twinkle. Planets are much nearer and act as extended sources; the changes cancel out, so they do not twinkle.

### Chapter: Electricity

**Q65 ★ (3 marks, 2017, 2018, 2024, 2026E2)** How will you connect three resistors of 6 ohm each so that the combination has a resistance of (a) 9 ohm (b) 4 ohm?
**Ans:**
- (a) **Two in parallel, then the third in series:** parallel part = (6 x 6)/(6 + 6) = 3 ohm; total = 3 + 6 = **9 ohm**.
- (b) **Two in series, then in parallel with the third:** series part = 6 + 6 = 12 ohm; total = (12 x 6)/(12 + 6) = 72/18 = **4 ohm**.
- Draw both circuit diagrams.

**Q66 ★ (3 marks, 2022, 2024, 2026)** An electric refrigerator rated 400 W operates 8 hours a day. What is the cost of energy to run it for 30 days at Rs 3.00 per kWh?
**Ans:**
- Energy per day = P x t = 400 W x 8 h = 3200 Wh = 3.2 kWh
- Energy for 30 days = 3.2 x 30 = **96 kWh**
- Cost = 96 x 3 = **Rs 288**

**Q67 ★ (3 marks, 2018, 2024, 2025, 2026)** What is resistivity? On what factors does the resistance of a conductor depend? Why are alloys used in heating devices?
**Ans:**
- R = ρ l / A, where ρ (rho) is **resistivity**, l is length, A is area of cross-section. SI unit of resistivity: **ohm metre**.
- Resistance increases with **length**, decreases with **area of cross-section**, and depends on the **material** and **temperature**.
- Alloys (like **nichrome**) are used in heaters, irons and toasters because they have **high resistivity** and **do not oxidise (burn) easily at high temperatures**.
- Tungsten is used in bulb filaments because of its very high melting point (3380 °C).

**Q68 (3 marks, NCERT)** An electric lamp of 100 ohm, a toaster of 50 ohm and a water filter of 500 ohm are connected in parallel to a 220 V source. What is the resistance of an electric iron that takes the same current as all three together? What is that current?
**Ans:**
- 1/R = 1/100 + 1/50 + 1/500 = (5 + 10 + 1)/500 = 16/500, so **R = 31.25 ohm**
- I = V/R = 220 / 31.25 = **7.04 A**

**Q69 (2 marks, NCERT)** Why is parallel combination used in domestic wiring rather than series?
**Ans:** (1) Each appliance gets the **full 220 V**. (2) Each appliance can be switched on or off separately, and if one fails the others keep working. (3) The total resistance is lower, so each appliance draws the current it needs.

### Chapter: Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

**Q70 ★ (3 marks, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2022, 2024, 2026)** Draw the magnetic field lines around a straight current-carrying conductor. State the rule used to find their direction.
**Ans:**
- Field lines are **concentric circles** around the wire, closer together near the wire (field is stronger near the wire).
- **Right-hand thumb rule:** hold the wire in your right hand with the **thumb pointing in the direction of current**; the curled **fingers show the direction of the magnetic field**.
- The field gets stronger if the current increases and weaker as you move away from the wire.

**Q71 ★ (3 marks, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2022, 2024, 2026)** Draw the field lines inside and around a current-carrying solenoid. What is an electromagnet?
**Ans:**
- Field lines outside are like those of a **bar magnet**; one end acts as N pole, the other as S pole.
- **Inside the solenoid the field lines are parallel straight lines**, so the field is **uniform** there.
- **Electromagnet:** a soft iron core placed inside a solenoid carrying current becomes a strong magnet. It loses magnetism when current is switched off.

**Q72 ★ (3 marks, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2022, 2024, 2026)** State Fleming's left-hand rule. What happens to the force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field if the current is reversed?
**Ans:**
- **Fleming's left-hand rule:** stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the **left hand** so they are at right angles to each other. **Forefinger = magnetic field**, **middle finger = current**, then **thumb = direction of force (motion)** of the conductor.
- If current is reversed, the **force is reversed** (the rod moves the other way).
- The force is largest when the current is **perpendicular** to the field, and zero when parallel.

| Lens magnification: m = h'/h = v/u | |

| R = ρ l / A | Resistivity ρ in ohm metre |

| Parallel: 1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3 | Same voltage across all |
| Charge: Q = I t | Coulomb |

**Sign rules (New Cartesian):** measure all distances from the pole/optical centre; object always on the left, so **u is always negative**; distances to the right are +, to the left are -; heights up are +, down are -.

### 6b. Key definitions (1-mark favourites)
- **Oxidation / reduction:** gain / loss of oxygen (or loss / gain of hydrogen).
- **Exothermic / endothermic:** heat given out (respiration, burning) / heat taken in (decomposition of CaCO3).
- **pH:** scale from 0 to 14; below 7 acidic, 7 neutral, above 7 basic.
- **Neutralisation:** acid + base → salt + water.
- **Alloy:** a homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or a metal and a non-metal (brass = Cu + Zn, bronze = Cu + Sn, solder = Pb + Sn).
- **Homologous series:** same functional group, differ by -CH2-.
- **Nutrition types:** autotrophic (plants), heterotrophic (animals, fungi).
- **Transpiration:** loss of water as vapour from the aerial parts (leaves) of a plant.
- **Tropism:** directional growth movement of a plant part (phototropism, geotropism, hydrotropism, chemotropism).
- **Gene:** a section of DNA that provides information for one protein (trait).
- **Dominant / recessive trait:** the one that shows in F1 / the one that stays hidden in F1.
- **Trophic level:** each step of a food chain.
- **Power of accommodation:** ability of the eye lens to change its focal length (near point 25 cm, far point infinity for a normal eye).
- **Dispersion:** splitting of white light into seven colours (VIBGYOR) by a prism.
- **1 ohm:** resistance of a conductor when 1 A flows through it at 1 V.
- **Electric power:** rate of doing work / using energy; 1 W = 1 V x 1 A.

6. 2NaHCO3 → Na2CO3 + H2O + CO2 (heating baking soda)
7. Fe + CuSO4 → FeSO4 + Cu (displacement)
8. Na2SO4 + BaCl2 → BaSO4 + 2NaCl (double displacement, white ppt)
9. 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl2 (sunlight)

11. 2ZnS + 3O2 → 2ZnO + 2SO2 (roasting); ZnO + C → Zn + CO (reduction)

15. 2CH3COOH + Na2CO3 → 2CH3COONa + H2O + CO2 (acid + carbonate gives CO2, which turns lime water milky)

## 7. Answer-writing tips (to collect step marks) and a 30-day timetable

### 7a. Tips
1. **Never leave a question blank.** Write the formula or definition at least; CBSE gives step marks.
2. **Numericals:** write Given (u, f, V, R...), the formula, put values with signs, then the answer with unit. The formula alone often gets ½ to 1 mark.
3. **Diagrams:** draw with a pencil, label with straight lines; a neat labelled diagram can earn half the marks of a 3-mark question.
4. **Equations:** always balance them and write conditions (443 K, conc. H2SO4, sunlight, catalyst).
5. **Biology answers:** use points, use the key terms (alveoli, nephron, auxin, placenta, dominant). Underline them.
6. **Assertion-Reason:** first check A alone, then R alone. Only if both are true, ask "does R explain A?"
7. **Case-based questions:** most answers are in the passage itself. Read it twice; the first two parts are usually easy.
8. **Internal choice:** pick the option you can draw or calculate fully.
9. **Order:** do the sections you know best first, but write the question number exactly as printed.
10. **Time:** about 30 minutes for the 20 MCQs, 2 hours for the rest, last 15-20 minutes to recheck.
